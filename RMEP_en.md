The Regulatory Model of the Energy of the Void and Its Connection with Dark Matter and Dark Energy

*A scientific hypothesis and its empirical test on the SPARC catalog (175 galaxies)*

Anonymous work (for peer review)

Version 5.0. Canonical version, consistent with per-object runs. Updated as of November 2026.

*Keywords: dark matter, dark energy, vacuum energy, galaxy rotation curves, cosmological constant, NFW profile, metastable (false) vacuum, SPARC catalog*

## Abstract

We propose the regulatory model of the energy of the void (RMEP) — an alternative to the dark matter hypothesis on a single mechanism: local configurations of the vacuum condensate with a dusty gravitational response, whose scale and amplitude are determined by the baryonic state of the system. The relativistic formulation preserves standard GR: the condensate enters the stress–energy tensor as ordinary mass, the metric is not modified — standard gravity outside the regulation zone, standard lensing, the speed of gravitational waves equal to the speed of light.

The empirical test was performed on the full SPARC catalog sample (175 galaxies, 3391 data points). At an equal number of free parameters, the RMEP profile — a condensate density with a flat core — describes the rotation curves substantially better than the standard NFW profile: RMEP wins in 107 cases against 31, with 37 ties; the median χ²_red values are 23.2 against 36.3. The core–cusp problem is solved without introducing additional physics — the flat core is a property of the profile. The fit reveals an empirical scaling of the regulation scale α ≈ 5 kpc·(V_max/100 km/s)^1.89 with a bootstrap interval of the exponent [1.34; 2.41] and a decrease of the central density with system mass: two degrees of freedom per galaxy reduce to a function of the baryonic state. The model reproduces the radial correlation of the discrepancy with acceleration — the structural basis of the RAR — and predicts a finite total regulation mass yielding a Keplerian segment of the rotation curve in 72 of 175 objects (a distinguishing prediction against divergent halos). The universality test of the transition acceleration has been performed by calculation: the median transition acceleration 0.14·a₀ reproduces the a₀ phenomenon of MOND to order of magnitude without postulating a constant, and the revealed systematic trend with mass is a new distinguishing prediction.

In cosmology the model inherits the standard background by construction (agreement with H(z), BAO and CMB), and the distinguishing power is transferred to structural tests: the evolution of the regulation fraction inside the effective radii of disks at redshifts z ~ 1–2.3, the evolution of the transition-acceleration scale, the mass step of SNe Ia through the stellar population age and potential depth, the low CMB multipoles through the integrated Sachs–Wolfe effect, and the DESI outcome on w(z) with two distinguishing outcomes. In the strong gravitational field the model introduces no observable changes: the external dynamics of black holes and the photosphere agree with the EHT and LIGO/Virgo data; the internal field structure of the center of compact objects is treated as a physical interpretation within the model, not as an independent quantitative result.

The limits of the model are stated explicitly: the galactic part has been quantitatively tested; clusters and lensing are described qualitatively; the predictions of earlier versions, not reproduced in the current formulation, have been archived with the reasons recorded. A program of distinguishing checks with pre-registered outcome criteria is formulated — from the age residuals of the RAR on existing data to the numerical implementation of the regulation evolution system; the final judgment on the viability of the model belongs to the listed checks.

# Section 0 *describes the physical picture (interpretation) of the model; quantitative results are deferred to Sections 6–8.*

## 0.1. What the energy of the void is

The energy of the void (vacuum) is not a "creator" of the Universe and not a source striving to generate matter or space. Its fundamental properties are **the drive toward equilibrium, observance of the law of energy conservation, and the second law of thermodynamics**.

The energy of the void is a dynamic medium with its own thermodynamic characteristics (pressure, temperature, energy density; their role as triggers is considered in Section 2). Fluctuations occur in it, and local states can accumulate energy without passing into observable matter. A gravitational field is present in the medium as an integral property of the medium itself.

**Status of the assumptions.** (1) The thesis of a medium with accumulation is an assumption of the model, not a consequence of established physics. In the standard theory the vacuum is a state of fields with minimal energy; fluctuations in it are interpreted as virtual particles. RMEP relies on the general idea of non-zero vacuum activity but introduces its own interpretation of this activity, not reducible to standard QFT. (2) The application of the second law of thermodynamics to the vacuum medium is part of the same assumption: in standard physics the vacuum is not described as a thermodynamic system with entropy. RMEP ascribes pressure, temperature and density to the medium, and in this frame the second law sets the direction of processes — from less probable states toward equilibrium. This is why the "drive toward equilibrium" in the model is not an intuition but a consequence of the accepted thermodynamic picture of the medium.

## 0.2. Why the energy of the void contains no quantum fields of the Standard Model

The energy of the void is a special medium whose conditions differ from the conditions inside regions of dark matter. Different regimes operate in it: the density, temperature regime and character of spacetime curvature are not comparable to intra-material ones.

The role of the Heisenberg uncertainty principle. In the model it is considered not as a limiter on the rate of accumulation but as a structural regulator of the states of the medium: fluctuations have no sharp localization or definite energy — they exist as a set of potential states. It is precisely this uncertainty that allows the medium to accumulate energy without prematurely triggering particle formation processes.

*Status: an interpretation of the model. In standard physics the uncertainty principle is a property of quantum systems, not a separate "regulator"; such usage is an extension of the concept and is adopted in RMEP as a postulate.*

The distinction from standard QFT — a terminological clarification. In standard quantum field theory the vacuum is also an active medium with fluctuations; RMEP does not deny this activity. The difference lies elsewhere: in RMEP, before the failure the medium **contains no fields of the Standard Model** — neither gauge nor fermion ones — and consequently there are no localized excitations with prescribed quantum numbers. The fields of the Standard Model that describe particles arise only together with dark matter at the moment of the failure, when local conditions of the medium reach sufficient definiteness. The quantum nature of the medium itself is preserved — its regime changes, not the absence of quantization.

*Status: the central assumption of RMEP. In standard physics fields exist everywhere, regardless of the presence of matter; the thesis of the birth of fields together with dark matter contradicts this position and therefore must be justified within the model, not derived from standard theory.*

Thought experiment: what would happen if the fields of the Standard Model existed in the medium. If the energy of the void contained fields generating localized excitations, the medium would strive to form composite structures. This leads to one of two scenarios:

- **Collapse** — local collapse of the medium. If the fields of the Standard Model existed in the medium before the failure, their excitations would begin to combine into bound states: pairs, composite particles, larger structures. Each combination concentrates energy in a limited volume, and all three thermodynamic parameters of the medium grow consistently: the energy density increases, the temperature rises locally, the pressure on the gravitational field grows. The medium has no release mechanism: it does not yet possess the energy-drain channels that, in a working Universe, are provided by gravity and stabilized matter. The growing pressure and density strengthen the local curvature of spacetime, which draws in the surrounding excitations even more strongly — the process closes on itself in an avalanche-like (runaway) manner. The result is local collapse: a region of the medium collapses into a state with density, temperature and pressure incompatible with the preservation of the medium's own structure.
- **Runaway growth** — a rapid growth of energy leads to uncontrolled production of new states exceeding the capacity of the system to maintain equilibrium.

*Status: intuitive argumentation, not a calculation. Both scenarios are treated in the model as violations of the energy balance — "collapse" as a concentration of energy beyond the threshold without compensation, "runaway" as a runaway process. The quantitative verification of this claim (the existence of a stable metastable state as a consequence of the absence of fields) is an open task of the model; Sections 2–3 do not solve it but use it as a premise.*

Therefore, the fields of the Standard Model do not exist in the energy of the void. They appear only at the moment of the failure — together with dark matter, when the failure creates local conditions with sufficient definiteness for their emergence. Where there is dark matter, there are quantum fields — they stabilize it under the control of the gravitational field.

## 0.3. How dark matter and the quantum fields of the Standard Model appear

The emergence of dark matter, the fields of the Standard Model, space, and ultimately the observable Universe is not a planned process but a consequence of an energy failure of the energy of the void (vacuum). In terms of a metastable medium, the failure is described as nucleation of a true-vacuum bubble: a local fluctuation in which one or a group of parameters (density, temperature, pressure) exceeds a critical threshold under gravitational pressure. There is no external influence — the system deviates from equilibrium by itself.

**Status: randomness with a computable probability.** The randomness of the failure does not mean arbitrariness: in the theory of metastable vacuum the decay rate per unit volume is given by the expression Γ/V = A·exp(−S_E/ħ), where S_E is the instanton action (for a thin-walled bubble S_E = 27π²σ⁴/(2ε³), where σ is the surface tension of the bubble wall, ε is the energy difference of the phases). RMEP inherits this apparatus: the failure is a rare but lawful event whose probability is determined by the parameters of the medium. Hence the first falsifiable consequence of the model: the characteristic waiting time of the failure is finite and depends on the energy density of the false vacuum (quantitative formulation — an open task; the frame is established apparatus, Coleman, 1977).

The energy of the void is in a metastable (false) state. The false vacuum is not a separate trigger but a characteristic of the entire medium: it is in this metastable state that temperature, pressure and density can fluctuate up to the threshold. The failure is a transition from the false vacuum to a new state, accompanied by the release of energy, which becomes dark matter.

**Status:** **the metastability relies on established apparatus** (the physics of vacuum phase transitions; the false vacuum is an accepted concept). **The identity "released energy = dark matter" is an assumption of the model**: standard physics does not identify the products of vacuum decay with dark matter.

The law of energy conservation is not violated globally. Locally a redistribution occurs: energy leaves the vacuum into dark matter, and gravity locks it there so that the overall balance is preserved. The failure is a local deviation, not a violation of a fundamental law.

At the moment of the failure the following happens:

1. **Dark matter is born** — as a by-product of exceeding the threshold. In the explosive regime (when all three triggers — pressure, temperature, density — fire simultaneously) dark energy does not appear: the process is too fast for its formation. Dark energy can arise only at the stabilization stage, when the system calms down and the processes slow down (see 4.4 and 4.6).
2. **Together with it the fields of the Standard Model appear** — they did not exist before the failure but arise as an accompanying structure when the failure creates local conditions with sufficient definiteness (see 0.2).
3. **The fields of the Standard Model stabilize dark matter under the control of the gravitational field.** Why this is necessary: after the birth of dark matter, complex structures (composite states) begin to form, absorbing energy, raising the temperature locally and lowering the pressure. Without the fields this process would run unchecked — dark matter would fly apart or collapse (the scenarios of 0.2). The fields hold it from decay, and the gravitational field sets the boundaries within which the process proceeds.
4. **The gravitational field controls the entire process** — it defines the boundaries, holds the system, and does not let energy back into the vacuum.

**Status of items 2–4: the functional roles of the fields are an interpretation of the model.** The mechanism by which quantum fields "stabilize" dark matter is not specified in the model; in standard physics a field does not perform a "holding" function as an agent. This is adopted as a working frame consistent with the self-consistency of the cycle, rather than derived from standard theory.

Sequence:

Energy of the void (only the gravitational field, superposition) → accumulation → failure (bubble nucleation, threshold exceeded) → birth of dark matter + appearance of the fields of the Standard Model → formation of complex structures (energy absorption, pressure reduction) → the fields stabilize, gravity controls → stabilization → a new cycle of accumulation.

|---|---|---|
| Gravitational | In the energy of the void — before and after the failure | Holds the structure of the vacuum, accumulates pressure, brings the system to the threshold, cuts dark matter off from unlimited access to the source while permitting dosed feeding |

## 0.5. Why one Universe

In RMEP the observable Universe is unique, and the model has a reason for this: the failure does not recur.

**Formulation of the problem.** In the standard theory of metastable vacuum, bubble nucleation is possible independently at any location: if the decay probability per unit volume is non-zero, many such events should occur over a long time. RMEP must explain why one bubble fired, not many. The model's answer is this: **accumulation is a global process, not a set of independent local fluctuations.** The medium accumulates energy as a whole; the threshold is exceeded once, and the accumulated resource is spent entirely — on the birth of dark matter. After the failure the energy is locked by gravity inside the regions of dark matter and does not return to the vacuum (0.3, item 4), and the medium itself returns to an equilibrium state with a new structure. For a new failure a new full cycle of accumulation up to the threshold would be required — and the conditions for it are absent in the post-failure medium: the resource that could fluctuate to the critical threshold is distributed among the structures of stabilized matter.

**Status: an assumption of the model.** The thesis of the global character of accumulation and the one-time nature of the failure is an interpretation of RMEP; standard theory does not require one-time occurrence and permits multiple acts of nucleation. The quantitative verification (showing that the post-failure medium cannot reach the threshold again) is an open task of the model.

A separate caveat: the statement "there are no other Universes" in the strict sense is not observationally testable — regions beyond event horizons are inaccessible. Therefore the position of this section should be understood not as an empirical prediction but as a consequence of the model: if its mechanism is accepted, no repeated failure arises. The distinction between "uniqueness as a consequence" and "uniqueness as an observable fact" is fundamental; the work under review claims only the former.

## 0.6. Cyclicity — not "creation" but "repair"

In the regulatory model of the energy of the void (RMEP), the pulsational cycle (accumulation → failure → stabilization → a new cycle of accumulation) is not a mechanism for producing matter but an attempt of the system to return to equilibrium after each failure. Each failure is a deviation from equilibrium; each stabilization is a return to energy conservation. Dark matter, the fields of the Standard Model and space are not the goal of the process but stable by-products that gravity localized and "sealed off".

**Separating the two levels of the cycle — an answer to the question of the second law of thermodynamics.** At every step of the cycle the second law operates, and it operates in one direction: accumulation is a drift — a slow shift toward the threshold under the thermodynamic pressure of the medium; the failure is the relaxation of the accumulated excess; stabilization is the transition of the system into a locally less stressed state. No step reverses entropy. Yet the cycle as a whole can repeat — and there is a reason for this: the system is not isolated from its own past. Energy locked by gravity in the matter of past cycles returns to the medium through the destruction of structures (proton destruction, black holes — Section 4), and the limiting case of return is the maximal breakthrough, which restarts the medium entirely (Section 4.7). Therefore, for the medium as a whole the cycle is not "heat death" but a closed circulation: locally entropy grows at each step, but globally the conserved energy circulates rather than degrading.

### 0.6.1. Destruction of baryonic matter

The baryon cycle spends its resource through three channels:

- **Destruction in interactions** — in the nuclei of active galaxies, in cosmic ray collisions, in the interiors of neutron stars. This is the only channel partially supported by standard physics: hard interactions destroy nuclei and alter the composition of matter, although the proton as a particle survives in them.
- **Internal instability** — protons are unstable "by construction": they are formed of quarks, which are themselves a product of the cycle, and are not obliged to exist forever.
- **Black holes** — extreme tidal forces destroy matter down to elementary components; the released energy returns to the energy of the void. It is this channel that closes the global circulation on which 0.6 relies.

**Role in the cycle.** The totality of the channels ensures a continuous return of matter to the medium: failures and interactions destroy structures, black holes disintegrate them into components, and energy flows back into the energy of the void — this is precisely the return mechanism that makes the cycle of 0.6 closed while preserving the second law of thermodynamics.

### 0.6.2. The life cycle of dark matter

Dark matter has its own "timer" — a life cycle distinct from the baryonic one. Dark matter does not maintain itself indefinitely: its existence depends on the influx of energy from the energy of the void. The feeding is not free access to the source: gravity cut dark matter off from the unlimited sink (0.3–0.4), and the influx proceeds only in dosed form, under the control of the gravitational field. While the feeding arrives, dark matter develops and grows more complex. When the flow weakens or stops, the decay of dark matter begins: the structures cease to be maintained and gradually lose organization, and the energy contained in them is subject to return to the medium (return mechanisms — Section 4; see also 0.6.1 for the baryonic channel).

**Status: an assumption of the model.** The dependence of dark matter on feeding and its decay have no analogue in standard cosmology, where dark matter is treated as a conserved component requiring no "nutrition". RMEP adopts this thesis as a condition of the closedness of the energy cycle: without feeding and decay the return of energy to the medium would be impossible, and the cycle of 0.6 would not close.

### 0.6.3. The interaction of the two cycles

| Phase | Protons | Dark matter | What is observed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early cycle | Formed at an increasing rate | Receives feeding from the vacuum | Baryonic matter dominates |
| Mature cycle | Partial destruction, energy → vacuum | Stable, feeding still arrives | Equilibrium of both components |
| Late cycle | Destruction exceeds reproduction | Feeding decreases, decay | Dominance of dark matter |
| Finale of the cycle | Destruction of the bulk of protons | Fully exhausted, decay | Maximal breakthrough |

**Key conclusion of the table.** The baryonic cycle is spent faster, while dark matter is exhausted more slowly. Therefore in the late phases of the cycle dark matter dominates — not because it grows, but because it decays more slowly. This ratio agrees with the observed composition of matter in the Universe: dark matter accounts for about 85% of all matter. RMEP treats this not as a coincidence but as the natural state of the late phase of the cycle.

**Status: qualitative agreement, not a quantitative derivation.** The model explains the sign of the ratio (more dark matter) but does not derive the number 85% from its parameters — quantitative agreement with measurements of the cosmological parameters (the ΛCDM plane) is an open task of Sections 6–8. The asymmetry of the decay rates of the two cycles is an assumption of the model requiring an indication of the cause (see below).

**Cause of the asymmetry.** In RMEP it follows from the difference in the construction of the cycles (0.6.1–0.6.2): baryonic matter is destroyed by active channels (interactions, black holes) from the moment of its formation, whereas dark matter decays only when the feeding weakens — it has no analogue of "destruction in interactions". Dark matter is spent more slowly not because it is sturdier by nature, but because its cycle contains fewer expenditure channels.

### 0.6.4. The maximal breakthrough

When the feeding from the vacuum ceases, dark matter gradually decays (0.6.2), the fields of the Standard Model lose stability and vanish together with the structure that engendered them. In the final stage the massive decay of protons (0.6.1) completes the baryonic cycle. Space ceases to exist as a stable geometry — it "dissolves" together with the vanishing matter.

**Status: an assumption of the model.** The dissolution of space, the vanishing of the fields together with matter, and the restart of the medium have no analogue in standard physics and are adopted as a condition of the closedness of the cycle. The quantitative verification is an open task of Sections 6–8; the qualitative linkage with 4.7 — where the maximal breakthrough is treated as the limiting case of energy return — is preserved.

### 0.6.5. A testable prediction

If dark matter decays more slowly than baryonic matter (0.6.3), the ratio of dark matter to baryonic matter must grow over the course time of the cycle: in regions at a later phase, the dark matter fraction is higher. Operationally, the same scale-separation principle applies as in 4.6: high redshift shows a system in the early phase, low redshift — in the late one.

**Status: a falsifiable prediction.** It is formulated operationally, the direction of the effect is given, the refutation criterion is stated. The current status is partial agreement with the data on disk galaxies under two recorded tensions (the global ratio 1:5 since recombination; non-monotonicity with mass). The prediction is not refuted but is not unambiguously confirmed either; quantitative agreement is an open task of Sections 6–8.

### 0.6.6. Black holes as a mechanism of energy return

A black hole is a region of extreme gravitational field in which tidal forces destroy infalling matter down to elementary components. The return of energy to the energy of the void proceeds through two channels of different significance:

- **Channel A — return through the accretion disk.** Matter falling into the disk is heated, part of its energy is emitted as radiation, and this energy returns to the medium quickly and observably. The disk gives up energy fast and visibly — this is the observable part of the return.
- **Channel B — return from beyond the horizon.** Energy that has crossed the horizon is inaccessible to an external observer; the only return mechanism permitted by standard physics is Hawking radiation, which for astrophysical black holes of stellar masses is so slow that the evaporation times exceed the current age of the Universe by dozens of orders of magnitude. RMEP accepts this channel as complete but extremely stretched in time: the final return of the conserved energy occurs on scales far exceeding the lifetimes of stars and galaxies.

**Status: channel A — consistent with observations and standard physics; channel B — an assumption of the model.** The thesis of the complete return of energy from beyond the horizon has no confirmation: Hawking radiation has not been observed, and the information paradox remains unresolved. RMEP must acknowledge that the closedness of the cycle rests on an undiscovered mechanism, and its empirical verification is an open task (including the fundamental question: is information preserved upon return). The "tornado"/"vortex" metaphor is figurative, not physical: the gravitational field of a black hole does not vortex in the mechanical sense; tidal stretching is described by the standard Schwarzschild/Kerr geometry.

**Role in the cycle.** The totality of the channels provides a staged return: the disk gives up energy quickly and observably, the horizon conserves the remainder over ultra-long times. For the global circulation of 0.6 this means: the return proceeds continuously but asymmetrically — the larger part is conserved, and it is precisely this that makes the maximal breakthrough (0.6.4) a rare event rather than a constant process.

# Section 1. The Concept of the Energy of the Void

## 1.1. Excitation of the energy of the void

The energy of the void is not a static background: processes of particle creation and annihilation run in it continuously. In the regulatory model of the energy of the void (RMEP) the medium has two regimes of excitation:

- **Partial excitation** — a local fluctuation with a small increase in the number of elementary particles. The energy remains inside the system: the medium absorbs the perturbation and returns to equilibrium without releasing anything outward. Functionally, this is the accumulation channel: each such fluctuation leaves in the medium a contribution to the overall pressure (0.3).
- **Strong excitation** — an excess of the *local* threshold at which part of the energy goes beyond the vacuum and enters dark energy. This is not a full-scale failure but a "leak" across the boundary. Functionally, this is the safety channel: it discharges excess pressure locally, without bringing the medium to the global threshold.

**Accumulation as the growth of potential states.** Accumulation proceeds not as "particles piling up in a heap" but as the growth of the density of potential states — configurations of the medium that after the failure (0.3) are realized as real particles and fields. Coexisting in the medium are: virtual particles, constantly created and annihilating — a regime confirmed by standard physics (the Casimir effect, the Lamb shift); exotic particles — not-yet-discovered excitations of the medium; candidates for accumulation — electrons, photons, quark precursors, axions, majorons. The latter two are hypothetical particles: axions remain one of the leading dark-matter candidates in standard physics as well, while majorons appear in neutrino mass models.

## 1.2. Relation to quantum field theory

The standard formulation of quantum field theory (QFT) describes particles as excitations of the corresponding fields, and the vacuum as the lowest-energy state of the fields. Within the regulatory model of the energy of the void (RMEP) this picture is correct *after* the failure (0.3), when the fields of the Standard Model have already been separated and stabilized by gravity. Before the failure, particles act as potential states of energy (1.1) — not yet shaped into field excitations of the medium.

**Ontological relation.** QFT and RMEP do not compete: QFT describes the behavior of fields after their emergence, RMEP — the origin of the fields themselves and the medium preceding them. The divergence concerns only the status of the vacuum: for QFT the vacuum is the lowest state of the fields; for RMEP it is the substrate from which the fields are born and into which they return. QFT remains an exact effective theory within the cycle; RMEP claims to describe its edges — the beginning (the failure) and the completion (the maximal breakthrough, 0.6.4).

**Status: an interpretive extension, not a denial.** All working predictions of QFT (Lamb shifts, renormalization, the structure of interactions) are inherited by RMEP in full: within the cycle the model offers nothing that contradicts QFT. The new content of RMEP in this subsection is ontological, not computational: the medium is primary, the fields are its stable excitations. Subsection 1.2 by itself generates no testable consequences; its role is to fix the place of QFT in the structure of the model and to remove the potential objection that "the theory contradicts quantum field theory."

## 1.3. Difference from the standard picture (ΛCDM)

ΛCDM (the standard cosmological model with dark energy and cold dark matter) is built on general relativity: gravity responds to already-existing mass and energy — matter curves spacetime, and the gravitational field is a consequence of the distribution of sources. Gravity in this picture is passive: without a source it does not exist.

In the regulatory model of the energy of the void (RMEP) the gravitational field is primary and actively governs the medium before the appearance of mass:

- **Holding the metastable state.** Gravity holds the vacuum in a metastable state (the false vacuum is a state that is locally stable but not absolutely stable). The notion of the false vacuum is itself standard: the metastability of the electroweak vacuum is discussed in standard physics on the basis of the measured Higgs boson mass. What is new in RMEP is that gravity is treated as a factor *holding* the metastability, not merely as a field generated by sources.
- **Bringing to the threshold.** Accumulation (0.3, 1.1) proceeds under gravitational control: gravity doses the leaks and does not let the medium discharge its excess prematurely — this is its regulatory function.
- **Separation at the failure.** At the moment of the failure (0.3) gravity cuts the nascent dark matter off from unlimited access to the source (0.4, with the synchronized correction on dosed feeding), controlling the energy balance between the medium and the conserved matter.

In sum: gravity is not a passive response to mass but an active regulator of the cycle, governing the excitation of the energy of the void before mass even appears.

**Tension with general relativity — an honest acknowledgment.** In GR, the gravitational field is determined by the distribution of energy–momentum: the theory knows no source without mass or energy. The RMEP thesis of "gravity before mass" cannot be formulated in these terms without additional assumptions — for example, that the gravitational field exists as an independent geometric structure of the medium, requiring no source in the pre-failure phase. RMEP adopts this as the core of its ontology: gravity is a property of the medium itself, not a response to its contents. The model inherits GR throughout the intra-cyclic range: wherever mass exists, the predictions coincide with the verified ones.

**Status: the core of the model, an assumption.** Active gravity before the appearance of mass is the central assumption of RMEP, with no analogue in standard physics; it is neither refuted by observations (the pre-failure phase is unobservable) nor supported by them. The only indirect contact is the very fact of the Universe's existence: standard cosmology starts from a hot initial state given a priori and does not answer where it came from — RMEP occupies precisely this niche. The quantitative formalization (which field describes pre-failure gravity, how it passes into GR at the birth of mass) is the key open task of Sections 2 and 6–8.

# Section 2. The Regulator Mechanism

The regulator is the gravitational field governing the cycle of transformations of the energy of the void (ontological status — 1.3: gravity as a property of the medium itself, not a response to its contents). The regulatory cycle consists of four phases:

1. **Accumulation** (2.1) — the growth of the density of potential states under the hold of metastability.
2. **Failure** (2.2–2.3) — the birth of dark matter; at the global failure — also of the fields of the Standard Model.
3. **Stabilization** (2.4) — relaxation of the medium, formation of quarks and baryonic matter.
4. **Energy return** (2.5) — the staged return of conserved energy to the medium (0.6.1, 0.6.6), culminating in the maximal breakthrough (0.6.4).

The evolution of matter (0.4–0.6) unfolds within phases 3–4.

## 2.1. The accumulation phase

- Creation and annihilation of virtual particles run in the medium continuously — a regime confirmed by standard physics and inherited by RMEP (1.1, 1.2).
- Accumulation proceeds as the growth of the density of potential states (1.1) — configurations that after the failure are realized as real particles and fields.
- The growth of density and energy loads the medium itself: its geometric state ("curvature" as the state of the gravitational structure, 1.3) changes, the excitation densifies, the load on the gravitational field grows. "Pressure" and "effective temperature" here are characteristics of the intensity of excitation, not thermodynamic quantities: before particles appear, there is no temperature in the strict sense.
- As long as the load has not reached the critical threshold, accumulation proceeds. The regulator is active: it doses the leaks through strong excitation (1.1) and holds the metastability (1.3) — the growth of pressure is consistent with the regulation regime, not in conflict with it.

**Status: mixed.** Virtual particles — standard physics; the growth of the density of potential states — an interpretation of RMEP; "curvature," "pressure," "effective temperature" in the pre-failure phase are working quantities of the model, their strict definitions and the transition to ordinary thermodynamics after the failure are an open task of Sections 6–8. The mechanism by which gravity holds the metastability is a separate open task.

## 2.2. The failure condition

The failure threshold is set by a group of parameters of the medium (2.1): pressure, "effective temperature," and energy density. The event is initiated by whichever parameter reaches the threshold first; the remaining parameters determine not "yes/no" but the character of the event. It is essential to separate two regimes:

- **The global failure** (0.3) — a one-time event of the cycle: dark matter *and* the fields of the Standard Model are born. The thesis of 0.5 on the impossibility of a repeated failure refers precisely to it.
- **The local discharge** — an excess of the local threshold in an individual region of the medium: only dark matter is born. The fields by this moment already exist from the time of the global failure, so their re-creation is not required. Local discharges are multiple and continue throughout the entire cycle.

Thus is built the **ladder of thresholds**: partial excitation (accumulation) → strong excitation (leak into dark energy, 1.1) → local threshold (discharge: the birth of dark matter) → global threshold (the failure). Each level is its own form of excitation of the medium, and none cancels the previous one.

*The character of the event depending on which parameter fired first:*

| Which parameter fired first | Where this is likely | Character of the event |
|---|---|---|
| Pressure | Deep gravitational wells (centers of massive galaxies, clusters) | Massive discharge: much dark matter, a steep distribution close to the NFW profile |
| "Effective temperature" | Regions of intense particle creation (active galactic nuclei, star-forming regions) | "Hot" discharge: dark matter is born with high excitation intensity and relaxes more slowly |
| Energy density | Regions of slow prolonged accumulation (halo periphery, void boundaries) | "Cold" moderate discharge: less dark matter, a more diffuse distribution |

**Caveat on the analogy.** "Hot" and "cold" discharge in the model refer to the state of dark matter at birth and its relaxation rate — not to hot and cold dark matter in standard cosmology (where the classification is set by particle velocities in the early Universe, and hot candidates are tightly constrained by observations — under 1% of the composition). These notions must not be conflated; the correspondence is only an analogy of the type "compact/diffuse distribution."

## 2.3. The failure phase

- When the threshold is reached, the failure occurs: the global one — the birth of dark matter and the fields; the local one — the discharge of dark matter (2.2).
- Part of the energy of the void passes into the birth zone.
- The fields of the Standard Model appear at the global failure (1.2) and stabilize the newborn dark matter; at a local discharge, stabilization is provided by the already-existing fields.
- The gravitational field closes the direct channel of access to the source: it cuts the dark matter off from unlimited access to the energy of the void, permitting dosed feeding (0.4, 0.6.2).
- Dark matter retains the dosed feeding and relies on the accumulated resource.

## 2.4. The stabilization phase

- The excitation of the medium subsides: "effective temperature," pressure and energy density return to equilibrium values.
- The medium returns to equilibrium — accumulation continues in the post-failure medium.
- Dark matter relaxes: it loses its excess excitation ("hot" discharges — more slowly).
- The formation of quarks inside dark matter proceeds (0.6.1) — on the fields that have existed since the global failure; then — baryonic matter and protons.

## 2.5. The energy return phase

- Part of the protons is destroyed (interactions, internal instability, black holes) — 0.6.1.
- The liberated energy returns to the medium — via the channels of 0.6.6: the radiation of accretion disks (channel A) and the ultra-long return from beyond the horizon (channel B). Return into the geometry of the medium (1.3) is a working image; its strict mechanism is an open task of Sections 6–8.
- Black holes conserve part of the energy over ultra-long times — this sets the staged character of the return and the rarity of the maximal breakthrough (0.6.6).
- The returned energy feeds the continuation of the cycle — accumulation and local discharges.

## 2.6. The complete circuit

The complete circuit = the four phases above in sequence: accumulation (2.1) → failure (2.2–2.3) → stabilization (2.4) → energy return (2.5). The evolution of matter (0.4–0.6) unfolds within phases 3–4; each pass of the local circuit is one "pulsation" (Section 3).

## 2.7. The key property of dark matter

Dark matter has no return channel of its own into the energy of the void. This follows from the design of the barrier installed by the regulator at the failure (0.4, 2.3):

- **The barrier governs the flow in both directions — and doses both.** Gravity cut dark matter off from *unlimited* access to the source but preserved dosed feeding (0.6.2). Hence, the channel between dark matter and the medium is not shut fast but placed under the regulator's control: the input (feeding) is dosed, and the output (return) is dosed as well. "Cutting off" in RMEP is not a wall but the transfer of the channel under control.
- **Baryonic matter has return channels; dark matter does not.** Baryonic matter returns energy actively: through the destruction of protons and black holes (0.6.1, 0.6.6). Dark matter does not interact and has no active channels — its return is possible only passively, as it fades with the weakening of the feeding (0.6.2). That is why its expenditure is stretched over the entire cycle (0.6.3): not because it is intrinsically sturdier, but because its cycle contains fewer expenditure channels.
- **Not an energy deficit but the structure of the channel.** The formulation "dark matter does not have enough energy to overcome the barrier" is imprecise: it implies that with sufficient accumulated energy the return would become possible — which would make the property quantitative and breakable. In RMEP the return is blocked not by an energy deficit but by the design of the channel: the regulator does not let dark matter back except in a dosed fashion, as it fades. While the feeding arrives, dark matter does not fade and, therefore, does not return — it conserves energy.

**Consistency with the adopted text.** There is no contradiction with 0.6.2: there, the return of energy by dark matter is described as fading — a passive return as the feeding weakens, which is what is stated here. Consistent with 0.6.4: "fully exhausted, decay" is the moment when the passive return is complete; the full exhaustion of dark matter at the finale acquires a qualitative justification (the mechanism — fading), while the quantitative side remains with Sections 6–8.

**Status: an assumption of the model.** The unilaterally dosed character of the channel and the absence of active return channels for dark matter are a construction of RMEP without an analogue in standard physics. This block has no observational contact and must not claim any: its role is the internal mechanics of the cycle, explaining simultaneously the asymmetry of rates (0.6.3) and the source of the final impulse (0.6.4).

# Section 3. The Pulsational Cycle

In the regulatory model the system does not evolve monotonically but pulses: local discharges (2.2)
alternate with phases of accumulation and stabilization, creating wave-like dynamics. The term
**pulsation** here means the same as a full pass of the local circuit (2.6): accumulation → local
discharge → stabilization. Pulsations manifest themselves as local bursts of density and excitation
of the medium, which together form the observed structure.

## 3.1. The nature of pulsations

Pulsations are not external impacts but internal oscillations of the system: different regions of
the medium reach the local threshold (2.2) at different times. In some places the pressure threshold
fires first, in others the density threshold; therefore pulsations proceed in "waves" rather than
simultaneously over the whole volume.

The energy of the void lives and pulses on its own — this is a basic property of the medium (1.3),
requiring no external source. Pulsations are fed by two sources:

- **Internal** — the medium's own dynamics, self-sufficient and independent.
- **Return** — energy returned to the medium through proton destruction and black holes
(0.6.1, 0.6.6, 2.5); it is not external with respect to the system (all energy circulates within
the cycle), but external with respect to the medium: it enters from outside its equilibrium
dynamics. Return energy amplifies pulsations and accelerates the reaching of thresholds in regions
of slow accumulation.

## 3.2. Local and global pulsations

**Local pulsations** — local discharges in individual regions (2.2) that shape the structure of
dark matter. This is the current, continuously ongoing process: one region discharges, its
neighbors accumulate, then the next one discharges. A local pulsation includes all four phases of
the local circuit (2.6).

**The global pulsation** — the maximal breakthrough (0.6.4), which resets the entire cycle. This
is the explosive regime in which all three parameters (2.2) reach the global threshold
simultaneously. The process is rapid: dark matter forms sharply, the gravitational field and the
fields of the Standard Model operate in the limiting regime. The locked stock of dark energy
returns to the medium together with its dissolution (0.6.4, 3.5, see the statuses) — the new cycle
begins with the medium in a clean state.

## 3.3. The mechanism of damping and renewed growth

After a local discharge, a region loses its excess energy and enters the stabilization phase (2.4)
— the pulsation there temporarily "weakens," while neighboring regions continue to accumulate. As
a result the pulsation shifts, creating the effect of a wave. At this point return energy joins
the cycle: it feeds the medium in regions of slow accumulation and accelerates the reaching of the
threshold.

## 3.4. Amplitude and frequency of pulsations

The amplitude of a pulsation inherits the character of the discharge (2.2, the table): by pressure
— a strong pulsation (much dark matter, a steep profile); by "effective temperature" — a medium
one (a "hot" discharge, slower to relax); by energy density — a weak one (a small discharge, a
sparse distribution). The frequency of pulsations is set by the accumulation time of a region up
to the local threshold and is accelerated by return energy (3.3).

## 3.5. Dark energy as a locked component

The channel between the medium and dark energy is unidirectional: energy of the void → dark
energy, but not back. Dark energy accepts leaks directly (1.1) — its connection to the medium is
direct at the input; there is no channel at the output: once separated from the equilibrium
dynamics of the medium, this stock has no path of return into it. Thus a hierarchy of channels is
built up: the medium exchanges energy freely in both directions; dark energy only accepts; dark
matter accepts in dosed fashion and returns only passively, as it fades (2.7).

This is fundamentally important for the dynamics: the locked dark energy preserves a stock that
continues to influence the gravitational field and hence the discharge thresholds — therefore the
pulsations do not decay to zero over the course of the cycle.

**The fate of the locked stock at the finale of the cycle — a resolution of the model.** The
unidirectionality of the channel holds within a cycle. At the maximal breakthrough (0.6.4) the
medium dissolves together with the structures that cut it off — and the locked stock of dark
energy returns into the unified flow together with all the conserved energy. Without this
assumption the cycle is not closed: the return of "all remaining matter" (0.6.4) would not cover
dark energy. The property "does not return" in 3.5 holds for all phases of the cycle except the
transition between cycles — by the same scheme by which the one-time nature of the failure in 0.5
is lifted by the maximal breakthrough.

## 3.6. Connection with the life cycles

Pulsations are connected with two parallel life cycles. **The proton cycle:** the destruction of
protons releases energy that returns to the medium and feeds the pulsations (0.6.1, 2.5).
**The dark-matter cycle:** while the dosed feeding arrives (0.6.2), dark matter develops and
deepens gravitational wells, accelerating accumulation. The two cycles form a feedback that
regulates the frequency and amplitude of pulsations: more return — more frequent discharges; more
dark matter — larger discharges in deep wells.

## 3.7. Mathematical interpretation

Let $\rho_{\varphi}$ be the density of potential states in the medium, $P$ the pressure, and
$T_{\mathrm{eff}}$ the "effective temperature" (2.1). A local discharge occurs when at least one
of the parameters reaches its critical value:


$$
\max\left(P - P_c,\; T_{\mathrm{eff}} - T_c,\; \rho_{\varphi} - \rho_c\right) \geq 0,
$$


where $P_c, T_c, \rho_c$ are the critical thresholds. For the wave dynamics of pulsations a
phenomenological wave equation with damping and a source is introduced:


$$
\frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial t^2} = c_{\mathrm{vac}}^2 \nabla^2 \psi - \gamma\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial t} + S_{\mathrm{ret}}(t),
$$


where $c_{\mathrm{vac}}$ is the effective speed of propagation of the pulsation in the medium,
$\gamma$ the damping coefficient, and $S_{\mathrm{ret}}(t)$ the source of return energy (the
inflow from the destruction of protons and black holes, 3.1). The period of a local pulsation is


$$
\tau_{\mathrm{pulse}} \sim L / c_{\mathrm{vac}},
$$


where $L$ is the characteristic size of the accumulation region. The speed $c_{\mathrm{vac}}$
is a free parameter of the model: the excitation propagates in the medium, not as a signal in a
ready-made spacetime, and need not coincide with the speed of light. For the halo of a typical
galaxy ($L \sim 10\text{–}50\ \mathrm{kpc}$) at $c_{\mathrm{vac}} \sim 0{,}01\text{–}0{,}1c$
the period is $\tau_{\mathrm{pulse}} \sim 3\times10^{5}\text{–}2\times10^{7}$ years; the value is
bounded from below by observational data and remains an open parameter of Sections 6–8.

## 3.8. Connection with observed phenomena

The pulsational mechanism gives qualitative correspondences to several observed effects. The
status of all three is interpretation: standard physics explains these phenomena by its own means
(the plasma dynamics of clusters, the gravitational evolution of voids); RMEP offers an
alternative treatment of the mechanism, not an explanation of the unexplained.

- **Temperature gradients in clusters of galaxies** — qualitatively consistent with "hot"
discharges (3.4): in standard physics the temperature profiles of the hot plasma of clusters are
shaped by its dynamics; RMEP interprets them as traces of discharges by "effective temperature."
- **Shells and walls at the boundaries of voids** — qualitatively consistent with the wave of
discharges: in standard physics the boundaries of voids are regions of elevated density between
voids and filaments; RMEP sees in them traces of successive pulsations.
- **Local density peaks** — traces of past discharges; a direct analogy with the "cold" discharges
of halo peripheries (2.2).

**The distinguishing criterion.** For the RMEP treatment to become a prediction rather than a
retelling, one must indicate an observable difference between "traces of discharges" and standard
structural evolution — for example, a systematic connection of the orientation or periodicity of
shells with the direction of the nearest deep wells. Such a formulation is an open task of
Sections 6–8.

## 3.9. Distinction from inflationary perturbations

In inflation, primary perturbations are quantum fluctuations stretched by exponential expansion,
setting the initial conditions against which the spectrum of the cosmic microwave background is
checked. In RMEP, pulsations are a secondary, continuously ongoing process that shapes structure
at all scales, including today: not "once at the beginning" but a recurring mechanism.

**A mandatory caveat.** RMEP does not abolish the observed spectrum of primary perturbations of
the CMB — it is well measured and is a strict constraint for any model. Consistency requires one
of two things: either the primary spectrum is generated by the global failure (in which case the
mechanism of its generation is an open task of Sections 6–8), or the model adopts the inflationary
stage as a special case of a transition between cycles. Without this choice, Section 3 remains a
description of the current dynamics and does not claim to replace inflation on the question of
initial conditions.

# Section 4. Energy Flow and Its Regulatory Mechanisms

## 4.1. The complete scheme of the energy flow

The regulatory model describes a closed circuit of energy transformation in which each link performs
a functional role determined by the gravitational field as the primary regulator. The circuit includes
the direct path (the birth of material structures from the energy of the void) and the return path
(the return of energy into the medium through the destruction of baryonic matter, 2.5). The return in
the circuit is distributed in time: baryonic matter returns energy actively (2.7), dark matter —
passively, as it fades (2.7), dark energy — with a delay, at the transition between cycles (3.5). The
full balance is conserved on the scale of the global cycle.

## 4.2. The links of the circuit

Sections 2.1–2.5 described the phases of the cycle phenomenologically — what happens and under what
conditions. This subsection unfolds the micromechanics of the direct path: how the energy of the
medium turns into dark matter and baryonic matter. The mechanism fills the gap between the failure
condition (2.2) and the formation of quarks (2.4): the "birth zone" of 2.3 is precisely the ocean of
axions.

| Link | Function in the model | Physical mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| The medium (energy of the void) | Source of energy; carrier of potential states | Fluctuations of the medium; metastable state (false vacuum, 1.3) |
| Gravitational pressure | Active regulator: compresses the medium, brings it to the threshold | Change of the geometric state of the medium (1.3, 2.1) |
| Phase transition in the axion condensate | Trigger mechanism: the transition of the condensate into an active state | Loss of coherence / phase transition in a condensate of axion-like particles |
| ΔE (energy excess) | Energy exceeding the local equilibrium of the medium; directed into the ocean of axions | Local violation of equilibrium; redistribution with conservation of the balance |
| The ocean of axions | Intermediate reservoir: accumulates ΔE, smooths fluctuations | Coherent state of light scalar particles (a condensate of axion-like type) |
| φ (scalar field) | Connecting link: transfers energy to the stage of particle formation | Classical scalar field with a potential sensitive to the geometry of the medium |
| Gravity (G) | Regulator of the flow: holds the energy, sets the boundaries | Gravitational barrier with one-way dosed passage (2.7) |
| The Schwinger mechanism | Pair creation from the vacuum in a strong field | Standard QED effect |
| Quarks | The final product of the direct chain; protons are formed from them | Colored fermions of SU(3); confinement below the QCD scale |

**Reliance on standard physics — the strength of this block.** The Schwinger mechanism is a real QED
phenomenon: a strong electric field separates a virtual pair into real particles; the critical field
(~1.3×10¹⁸ V/m) has not yet been reached in the laboratory, but in cosmic conditions (magnetospheres
of neutron stars) fields of this order do occur. The closest analogues of the ocean of axions are an
active topic of standard physics: ultralight axion-like particles form macroscopic coherent
condensates on the scale of halos, with a core–shell structure and a quantum pressure opposing
gravitational compression. In RMEP these elements receive a new role: the condensate is not merely a
state of dark matter but an intermediate reservoir between the medium and matter.

**Status of the links.** Fluctuations, metastability, the Schwinger mechanism, quarks/gluons —
standard physics, inherited in full. The phase transition in the condensate as a trigger, ΔE, the
ocean of axions as a reservoir, the field φ as the connecting link — a construction of RMEP:
individual elements have close analogues in standard physics, but their combination into this chain
is the new content of the model. A rigorous derivation of the chain is an open task of Sections 6–8.

## 4.3. The return path: the return of energy

Energy returns into the medium through the destruction of baryonic matter via three channels
(0.6.1, 0.6.6, 2.5):

| Channel | Mechanism | What returns into the medium |
|---|---|---|
| Destruction in interactions | Extreme conditions in active galactic nuclei, cosmic ray collisions, neutron stars | The liberated energy of quarks and gluons through the return into the geometry of the medium |
| Internal instability of protons | A finite lifetime — a postulate of the model | The decay energy, transferred to the gravitational field |
| Black holes | Tidal forces destroy infalling matter (0.6.6) | Energy in the form of radiation (including Hawking radiation) and free particles |

**Caveat on the instability channel.** Spontaneous proton decay has not been observed: the lower
limit on the lifetime for the principal channel is $t_p > 10^{34}$ years (Super-Kamiokande) — many
orders of magnitude more than the age of the Universe. Therefore in RMEP the main observably
supported return channel is destruction in interactions; spontaneous instability is a postulate of
the model whose contribution dominates only on the ultra-long timescales of the cycle. Observational
contact: the lower bound on the lifetime is a testable consequence (the decay must eventually be
found; a strengthening of the limit to values incompatible with the cycle would be a refutation).
This refines, rather than contradicts, 0.6.1.

## 4.4. The locked component: dark energy

The channel between the medium and dark energy is unidirectional: energy of the void → dark energy,
but not back (the channel hierarchy — 3.5). Within a cycle dark energy does not return its stock
into the medium; the return happens with a delay — at the maximal breakthrough (3.2, 3.5).

**Synchronization with 1.1: the moment of appearance.** Dark energy does not first arise in the
stabilization phase — it exists from the beginning of the cycle and is continuously fed by leaks
(1.1). A refinement of the model: the contribution of an individual local discharge to dark energy
is small — the main energy flow of a discharge goes into dark matter, while the accumulation of dark
energy proceeds mainly in the stabilization phases, when the medium relaxes and the leaks of its
excess excitation dominate over direct discharges. In an explosive discharge (4.6) the direct
contribution to dark energy is minimal — the process is too fast for the slow leak channel.

Consequences:

- Pulsations do not decay to zero: the locked dark energy preserves a stock that influences the
gravitational field and the discharge thresholds (3.5).
- If dark energy returned into the medium freely, the system would quickly reach equilibrium — and
avalanche accumulation (0.2) would be impossible.
- Dark matter may fade when the feeding ceases, but it does not return directly — only passively, as
it fades (2.7).

## 4.5. Linking the discharge parameter to the operating mode of the circuit

| Discharge parameter | Character of the chain | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Pressure | Gravitational pressure is maximal → the phase transition is sharp → ΔE is large | Much dark matter, a steep profile (close to NFW). Typical for the centers of massive galaxies |
| "Effective temperature" | High excitation intensity → the transition is "softer" → ΔE is distributed differently | A pronounced temperature gradient (3.8). Typical for active nuclei and star-forming regions |
| Energy density | Slow accumulation → ΔE is small → the ocean of axions smooths the burst | Less dark matter, distributed uniformly, smoothed centers — a qualitative correspondence to the cusp problem (2.2). Typical for the halo periphery |
| Explosive regime (all three) | All three parameters reach the threshold simultaneously → ΔE is maximal | Rapid formation of dark matter; the direct contribution to dark energy is minimal (4.4) |

## 4.6. The explosive regime: features and consequences

The explosive regime — the simultaneous reaching of the global threshold by all three parameters
(2.2) — has features of its own:

- Rapid formation of dark matter — the ocean of axions dumps the accumulated energy in a single
moment.
- A sharp rise in the activity of the gravitational field and the fields of the Standard Model —
all operate in the limiting regime.
- Rapid emergence into space — the forming structures extend beyond the local region.
- The direct contribution to dark energy is minimal: the slow leak channel cannot keep pace with the
explosion; the locked stock of the previous cycle dissolves together with the medium and returns
into the unified flow (3.5).
- The accumulation of dark energy of the new cycle begins in the stabilization phases.

**Prediction.** In regions further along the cycle, the ratio of dark energy to dark matter is
higher than in regions of the early phase: dark energy accumulates from the first day of the cycle,
while dark matter in the late phase predominantly fades (0.6.3, 2.7). Operationally: for systems of
comparable mass, the ratio of dark energy to dark matter must grow with cosmological time — higher
in the nearby Universe (low redshift), lower in the early one (high redshift). The same scale-
separation principle as in 0.6.5: high redshift shows a system in the early phase. Caveat on
uniformity: by the adopted definition, dark energy is distributed uniformly (1.1, variant A), so the
prediction refers to the cosmic average per epoch, not to regional variations; observationally it is
tested with joint data of cosmological surveys (DESI, Euclid, the Roman telescope) — through the
relative evolution of the component densities.

## 4.7. The closure of the circuit and the law of energy conservation

The circuit is closed on the scale of the full global cycle; within a cycle the return is distributed
over three parts:

- **The actively closed part:** energy of the medium → material structures → destruction of protons
→ return into the medium. The main flow, proceeding continuously (2.5, 3.6).
- **The fading part:** dark matter spends its acquired resource and returns energy passively, as it
fades; it completes at the moment of the maximal breakthrough (2.7, 0.6.4).
- **The delayed part:** dark energy remains locked during the cycle; its stock returns into the
medium at the transition between cycles, together with the dissolution of the media (3.2, 3.5).

No part is lost: the law of energy conservation holds on the scale of the full cycle, although
within a cycle the balance of individual components is not closed. Local "non-returns" are phase
shifts, not leaks out of the system.

**The status of decay — a separate statement.** The "fading" of the dark component in the model
(0.6.2, 2.7) is not decay into particles but a passive relaxation of the configuration: when the
feeding weakens or the baryonic substrate is lost, the condensate disperses over the time τ_s(R),
returning energy into the medium (7.10). The observational consequence — the absence of radiation
during decay; this requires the synchronized correction of prediction 5.6: its distinguishing
status is preserved — the detection of an uncorrelated line refutes the model, but the expectation
changes from a signal to a null signal.

**A quantitative estimate of the energy share.** The total energy processed by a hole over the time
$t_{ev} \approx 1.2\times10^{10}$ years is bounded from above by the radiative efficiency of
accretion: for the growth of a hole to $M_{BH} \sim 10^7\text{–}10^8\,M_\odot$ with $\eta \sim 0.1$,
the total processed energy is of the order of $M_{BH}\cdot c^2$. The mass of the vacuum component of
a galaxy, by the profile of 6.8.1 ($M_\infty = 8\pi\cdot\rho_0\cdot\alpha^3$) with the canonical fit
medians ($\rho_0 \sim 10^7\,M_\odot/\mathrm{kpc}^3$, $\alpha \sim 2.5\text{–}5\ \mathrm{kpc}$), is
$\sim 4\times10^9\text{–}3\times10^{10}\,M_\odot$ — that is, its energy equivalent is of the same
order in units of $M_\odot c^2$. The ratio is $\sim 0.002\text{–}0.02$: the energy returned through
holes is a subdominant contribution to the energetics of the vacuum component; the main reservoir is
the cosmological background (7.4, the "ocean of axions"). *(In the previous version the share was
estimated at 0.03–0.3 because of an underestimated component mass; the canonical medians of
6.8.4/6.8.7 raise it — the conclusion about the trigger, not source, role of the hole is
strengthened.)* Consistency matters: the estimate is obtained from the observed AGN luminosity,
independently of the fit parameters.

**The maximal breakthrough.** The vacuum receives a powerful energy impulse from the decay of all
remaining matter — the maximal breakthrough occurs: a large-scale "boiling" of the energy of the
void (a term of the model: the avalanche growth of fluctuations after the return of all conserved
energy into the medium), the birth of new space, and the launch of a new cycle. By its parameters
the maximal breakthrough reproduces the conditions of the original failure of 0.3 — but this time
the resource is not accumulation "from scratch" but the return of everything locked in the matter of
all preceding phases.

**Status of the section: mixed.** The Schwinger mechanism, confinement, vacuum fluctuations —
standard physics. The ocean of axions as a reservoir, the phase transition as a trigger, the field
φ, ΔE, the distribution of the return over three parts — a construction of RMEP; the quantitative
reconciliation (the chain equation, the condensate parameters, the balance of channels) is an open
task of Sections 6–8. Prediction 4.6 is falsifiable: the direction is given, the refutation
criterion is the stable absence of growth of the ratio of dark energy to dark matter with
cosmological time on reliable data.

# Section 5. Testable predictions

The Regulatory Model forms six testable predictions, each tied to a specific type of discharge (2.2) or a phase of the cycle. The predictions span different scales and observational windows — from X-ray spectroscopy of clusters to searches for exotic radiation from the decay of dark matter. All quantitative values in the tables are illustrative order-of-magnitude estimates, not inferences from the model's parameters: establishing a strict connection between the predictions and RMEP parameters is an open problem of Sections 6–8. Phase terminology follows 0.6.5: high redshift corresponds to early phases of the cycle, low redshift to late phases.

## 5.1. Prediction 1: Temperature gradient at the dark/baryonic matter boundary

Discharge type: local discharges driven by "effective temperature" (2.2, 3.4). If dark matter is born with high excitation intensity and then relaxes, while baryonic matter forms separately and has its own thermodynamic state, a transition gradient of the X-ray gas temperature must exist at the boundary between the two regions. The specificity of RMEP is not the mere presence of structure in the profile (hydrodynamical ΛCDM models also predict features at halo boundaries — accretion fronts), but the pinning of the kink to the halo boundary *independently* of mergers and nuclear activity.

**What is predicted:**

- A kink or step in the \(T(r)\) profile at the halo boundary radius in clusters showing no signs of mergers or active nuclei.
- A temperature anomaly at void boundaries — a band of elevated or depressed temperature.
- Temperature anisotropy in galaxy halos: inner regions colder, outer regions warmer.

**Test method:** X-ray spectroscopy of clusters (Chandra, XMM-Newton, SRG/eROSITA, XRISM, NewAthena — launch in 2037, spectral resolution better than 4 eV with spatial resolution). **Falsifiability criterion:** if, in a sample of 20+ relaxed clusters (no mergers, no active nuclei), no kink in the temperature profile is found at the halo boundary in *any* of them, the prediction is refuted.

## 5.2. Prediction 2: Evolution of the dark-to-baryonic matter ratio with cycle phase

Type: a consequence of the life cycles of protons and dark matter (3.6). In RMEP, protons are destroyed more efficiently than dark matter fades (\(\Gamma_{bar} > \Gamma_{DM}\), where \(\Gamma\) is the effective destruction rate: interactions and black holes, not spontaneous decay — see the caveat in 4.3). Therefore, by the late phases of the cycle dark matter dominates — not because it grows, but because the baryonic component is consumed faster (0.6.3, 2.7); local discharges add an extra contribution (2.2).

**What is predicted:**

- \(f_{DM} = \rho_{DM}/\rho_{bar}\) is a monotonically increasing function of cosmological time, i.e. decreasing with redshift: at high \(z\) (early phases) it is closer to the birth value, at low \(z\) (late phases) it is higher.
- The direction matches the observed trend: studies of massive early-type galaxies show that the central dark-matter fraction at fixed mass is *lower* at high \(z\) and grows toward low \(z\). This is qualitative support of the direction, not proof of the mechanism.
- In ΛCDM, \(f_{DM}\) in clusters of fixed mass is considered approximately constant with \(z\) (modulo baryonic-physics corrections).
- The rigid values 6:1–8:1 from the previous version have been removed: with the currently observed ≈5:1 in clusters, such numbers would mean the prediction is already refuted by the data. The growth must be moderate — at the level of percent of the total ratio, accumulated over the part of the cycle accessible to observation.

**Test method:** gravitational lensing of clusters of equal mass at different \(z\) (CLASH, Hubble Frontier Fields, Euclid, Roman — launched in 2026). **Criterion:** if, in a sample of 30+ clusters with \(z > 0.5\) and 30+ with \(z < 0.1\) (at equal mass), there is no statistically significant difference in \(f_{DM}\) — the prediction is refuted.

## 5.3. Prediction 3: Evolution of the dark-energy-to-dark-matter ratio

Type: a consequence of the explosive regime and the leakage channel (4.4, 4.6). The direct contribution of the maximal breakthrough to dark energy is minimal: the slow leakage channel cannot keep up with the explosion; dark-energy accumulation concentrates in the stabilization phases. Therefore, in the early phases of the cycle the dark-energy-to-dark-matter ratio is lower than in late ones.

The statement has been recast in epochal form — spatial anisotropy of dark energy is excluded by definition (variant A: uniform distribution, 1.1): observations constrain dark-energy homogeneity orders of magnitude better than any conceivable regional variations, so a test of "homogeneity to 10⁻³" would not be a test of the model but a restatement of known results.

**Test method:** joint analysis of cosmological surveys (DESI, DES, Euclid, Roman, LSST) — via the evolution of the equation of state and of the relative densities of the components. **Criterion:** if the final data establish strict constancy of the dark-energy density (\(w = -1\) with no evolution) and invariance of \(\rho_{DE}\) — the leakage channel loses its observational support (the refutation criterion formulated in 1.1). The DESI hints (up to 4.2σ depending on the data combination) and the independent DES group point toward evolution — qualitative support of the direction at a status below the discovery threshold.

## 5.4. Prediction 4: Dependence of density profiles on the discharge parameter

Different discharge parameters produce qualitatively different regimes of the circuit (4.5) and, consequently, different dark-matter density profiles. A correct statement of the background: in ΛCDM the NFW profile is not "universal without caveats" — the standard model predicts a universal shape with concentration depending on halo mass and history; the cusp problem (2.2) shows where the picture strains. RMEP specificity: dependence of the profile on *environment* (well depth, proximity to a void), beyond what mass and concentration explain.

**Test method:** rotation curves and lensing data across environments (THINGS, SPARC, SKA; KiDS, DES for lensing). **Criterion:** if the profiles of all 50+ galaxies are fully described by the standard "mass + concentration" dependence, with no residual correlation with environment — the prediction is refuted.

## 5.5. Prediction 5: Dark-matter rings and shells at void boundaries

Pulsations propagate in "waves" (3.1, 3.3): one region discharges, its neighbours accumulate, then the next discharges. At the boundaries of large voids, sequential discharges form ring-like structures of enhanced density. Status caveat: enhanced density at void boundaries is predicted by standard cosmology as well (walls and nodes of the cosmic web); the RMEP specificity lies in the *ring morphology* with a characteristic scale tied to the pulsation period \(T_{puls}\) (3.7), not merely with the gravitational evolution of the void.

**Test method:** weak gravitational lensing at void boundaries (KiDS, DES, Euclid, Roman, LSST) — lensing measures total mass, so cross-checking against gas maps is mandatory (5.8). **Criterion:** if, in a sample of 10+ large voids, ring structures (>3σ) are found in none, with scale consistency with the void size — the prediction is refuted.

## 5.6. Prediction 6: Products of dark-matter decay

In RMEP, dark matter is a metastable state with a finite lifetime: as the feed weakens, it fades away (0.6.2, 2.7). The fading must be accompanied by decay into products detectable as exotic radiation. In ΛCDM, dark matter is stable and does not decay.

The observational context, stated honestly: the 3.5 keV line — a historically discussed decay candidate — has not been confirmed in most original analyses after systematic re-examination; independent hints persist (the Perseus anomaly, 2025), but the status is contested. RMEP predicts the *existence of a decay signal as such*, without committing to a specific line: the binding to channel and energy is the task of Sections 6–8. Constraint-consequence: the fading must manifest itself; if decay-signal limits are strengthened to values that exclude fading on the cycle timescale, that is a refutation.

**Test method:** search for decay lines in X-ray and gamma ranges (Fermi-LAT, XMM-Newton, Chandra, XRISM, NewAthena; COSI — launch in 2027, soft gamma range). **Criterion:** if, at a sensitivity an order of magnitude beyond the present limits, no decay signal is found, or the signal is identical in early and late phases — the prediction is refuted.

## 5.9. Falsifiability criteria — summary

The model is refuted if any of the following holds:

- **Temperature gradient:** no T(r) kink at the halo boundary in 20+ relaxed clusters.
- **Ratio \(f_{DM}\):** no significant difference in the predicted direction between 30+ early and 30+ late clusters of equal mass.
- **Ratio \(\rho_{DE}/\rho_{DM}\):** final data establish strict constancy of the dark-energy density and invariance of \(w\).
- **Density profiles:** all 50+ galaxies are described by the "mass + concentration" dependence with no residual environmental correlation.
- **Rings:** no ring structures (>3σ) in 10+ large voids.
- **Decay products:** at sensitivity an order of magnitude beyond present limits, no decay signal, or a signal uncorrelated with cycle phase.

Each criterion is independent: refuting one prediction does not require refuting the others. Confirmation of at least one prediction not explainable within ΛCDM would be a serious argument in favour of the regulatory model.

# Section 6. Compatibility with observational data

**Definition.** In this section, the "vacuum component" means the regulated state of the energy of the void within a galaxy's region — the density of potential states of the medium (1.1), gravitationally equivalent to a dust mass. It is not identical to the uniform dark energy (1.1): its density is a local parameter of the galaxy (6.8.7).

## 6.1. How the model explains the observed density profiles

The vacuum-component profile in RMEP admits two characteristic regimes that naturally correspond to the two observed types of halo density profiles. In the deep gravitational wells of massive halos (~10¹²–10¹³ M☉) the local discharge threshold is reached quickly, the energy discharge is sharp, and a steep central profile forms — close in shape to the classical NFW. In dwarf halos (~10⁹–10¹¹ M☉) accumulation proceeds slowly and the discharge is soft: the central density smooths out into a constant-density core — exactly what the RMEP exponential profile delivers structurally, without additional feedback mechanisms (6.8.1).

The empirical test (6.8.3, 6.8.7) confirms this picture as a trend, not a rigid dichotomy. RMEP wins in every mass range (win share 52–85% in each V_max bin, 6.8.3), and the median margin grows with mass: for V_max < 60 km/s it amounts to ~9 χ² units, in bins above 120 km/s — 28–100. NFW's largest advantages are concentrated in massive spirals with steep inner curve segments (single-case leads of up to thousands of χ² units), whereas in dwarfs its wins are isolated and achieved with a minimal margin (~4–15 units). Thus the picture "steep profile in massive halos, core in dwarfs" describes not a boundary between two classes but a smooth shift of the relative advantage of the two profile shapes along the mass scale, governed by a single dependence of the regulation scale on baryonic mass (6.8.4).

## 6.2. Predictions for early and late phases

From the difference between the life cycles of the components follows the key relation: baryonic matter is consumed faster than dark matter fades, so the dark-to-baryonic mass ratio grows monotonically with the age of the region (0.6.3, 2.7). On the scale of 0.6.5, late phases correspond to low redshift; hence observationally the ratio grows as one moves from high redshifts to the present. The direction coincides with prediction 5.2; a refinement of the measure: here we track the dark-component fraction *within the effective radius of the disc* — a quantity not identical to the global halo ratio of 5.2, but linked to it by the same mechanism.

The observational test is possible in the range z ≤ 2.3, where the dynamics of star-forming discs can be measured. Available data confirm the direction: the dark-matter fraction within the effective radius of massive discs at z ~ 1–2.3 is ~0.1–0.5, whereas for local analogues with comparable rotation velocities it is 0.6–0.9 (Genzel et al. 2020; Price et al. 2021; Parti et al. 2023). Importantly, the same picture is reproduced in the radial acceleration relation: the RAR, established on the local SPARC sample, also exists at intermediate redshifts, but its scale parameter evolves with z: per MUSE-DARK III data, a₀ at z ~ 1 is ~2.4×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² against the local ~1.2×10⁻¹⁰ — a doubling over ~8 Gyr, recorded with high significance. For static dark matter and a fundamental unchanging constant, such evolution is not foreseen; in the regulation picture, the "baryons — missing component" link depends on epoch by construction, since the regulation itself forms in time (6.8.4).

In the RMEP framework, the measured dark-component fraction includes the vacuum regulation, so the observed growth of f_DM with time is interpreted as growth of the regulation: in early phases the regulation is compact and dense with a small integral contribution, in late ones it is extended. A quantitative description of the evolution of the RAR scale parameter is an open test.

Limitation: SPARC is a local sample (z ≈ 0); a direct test of the evolutionary prediction belongs to external tests of the hypothesis. For the local epoch, the prediction reduces to a monotonicity requirement: among galaxies of one class, the ratio of the missing component to the baryonic one must systematically grow with the age of the stellar population. The check is possible with already available local data (stellar ages, colours, metallicity in addition to SPARC) — a separate open test.

## 6.3. Pulsations and structure at void boundaries

Sequential local discharges do not remain confined within individual galaxies: at the boundaries of large voids, where the density of the medium is minimal, the relative contrast of the regulation front is maximal, and traces of past discharges manifest themselves as rings and shells of enhanced density. These are not static structures but kinematic traces: a formed shell continues to participate in the dynamics of the region.

The observational picture agrees with this description in three independent respects.

First, shells of enhanced density at void boundaries are an established fact. The statistical analysis of voids in SDSS divides them into two classes: those with a monotonically rising density profile (R-type) and those surrounded by an excess shell (S-type). Small voids are surrounded by shells substantially more often than large ones, the fraction of shell-type voids varies continuously with radius, and the kinematics of the two classes differ: S-voids are mostly in a stage of contraction, R-voids of expansion (Ceccarelli et al. 2013; Ricciardelli et al. 2014).

Second, ring structures concentrate toward void boundaries. In a sample of 341 ring galaxies found in the voids of the NASA-Sloan Atlas, 91.5% are located in the peripheral regions and only 8.5% in the cores; the ring galaxies themselves are more massive and evolutionarily older than the typical void population. In the discharge picture such a concentration is expected: the edge of a void is the site of maximal accumulation from sequential discharges, and it is precisely there that the imprint of an event can be "recorded" into the galaxy's morphology.

Third, a compensating density ridge at a distance of about the effective void radius is observed in independent samples — in galaxy profiles and in cluster profiles (Dark Energy Survey).

The "void size — shell presence" dependence the model explains naturally. For a small void, the contribution of neighbouring regulation sources is coherent: the front arrives from comparable distances in comparable time, and the shell manages to form as a single structure. For a large void, the front arrives from many directions at different epochs and blurs into a smooth profile — observed as the R-type. Importantly, the very existence of shells at boundaries is predicted by the standard picture as well (matter flowing toward the edges of voids); the contribution of the hypothesis lies not in the fact of shells but in their discrete nature: a shell is the imprint of a specific event, not of continuous gravitational redistribution. This yields a testable difference: in samples with traced stellar ages at void boundaries (CAVITY, SDSS), galaxies of S-type shells should show more coherent age characteristics than the population of smooth R-type boundaries. The check is possible with existing data — an open test.

## 6.4. The role of black holes and the return of energy

Black holes are active nodes of energy return: an accretion outflow corresponds to the return of part of the energy to the medium (0.6.6), accompanied by a local pulsation. The returned energy feeds the discharges (3.3, 3.6), closing a positive loop: discharge → structure → accretion → return → pulsations. The return mechanism is not directly observable, but admits testable consequences in the statistics of nuclear activity.

Consequence one — spatial: nuclear activity should be enhanced where the relative contrast of the pulsations is maximal, i.e. in regions of lowered density. Observations confirm this with a strength unexpected in the standard picture: the AGN fraction in voids is higher than in walls and in the field, independently of the classification method; nuclear activity in voids is systematically stronger than in the field (Ceccarelli et al. 2021). Simulations and observations at z > 0.42 show a monotonic gradient: the AGN fraction decreases from the void centres (12%) to the dense skeleton of the large-scale structure (6.7%). The standard picture expects the opposite: in voids there are fewer mergers and less gas to feed accretion; the explanations offered in the literature (an elevated rate of single interactions, weakened ram-pressure stripping) describe the effect indirectly and do not predict a monotonic gradient from the void centre. In the regulation picture the gradient is natural: the density of the medium is minimal at the void centre — the relative contribution of the pulsational mode is maximal.

Consequence two — temporal: if nuclear activity reflects local discharges, the outflows must be episodic and recurrent. This is an observed fact: X-ray cavities and quasi-periodic ripples in the Perseus cluster indicate a series of repeated outflows on timescales of tens of Myr; restarted radio galaxies give duty cycles of tens of percent (~60% for 3C388); successive generations of cavities are observed in clusters with intervals of 1–10 Myr; individual events reach energies of 10⁵⁵ J (MS 0735.6+7421). The discrete-serial structure of the outflows agrees with the discharge picture and admits a quantitative test: the characteristic outflow recurrence interval should correlate with the characteristic timescales of local regulation (6.6).

**Discriminating test.** The standard picture ties AGN to mergers and the gas environment; the regulation picture — to the local pulsation regime. The explanations diverge on samples with traced void structure: galaxies at the boundaries of shell-type (S-type) voids (6.3) should show a higher AGN fraction than galaxies of smooth (R-type) boundaries at fixed mass, colour and environment. The check is possible by cross-matching void catalogues (SDSS) with AGN catalogues, without new observations — an open test.

## 6.5. Connection with the large-scale structure

The hierarchy of the large-scale structure — voids (~80% of volume at ~20% of mass), walls, filaments (~10% of volume at ~half the mass) and nodes — is reproduced in the standard picture of gravitational instability, and the model does not claim to explain the topology itself. The specific content of the pulsational picture is different: the elements of the web correspond to different discharge regimes, and this correspondence has testable consequences.

The correspondence of regimes to structural elements is set by two axes — the frequency and the contrast of the discharges. In voids, discharges are rare but occur in a medium of minimal density, where the relative contrast of the front is maximal (6.4): the consequence is single powerful events capable of activating galactic nuclei. In filaments, discharges are frequent: the medium along the filament is continuously reworked. In nodes, the frequency and energy of discharges are maximal. The observed sequence of populations agrees with this picture: void galaxies are on average younger, bluer, with elevated star-formation rates and an elevated burst fraction compared to galaxies of walls and filaments at comparable masses. Approach to the filament axis at fixed stellar mass and ambient density is accompanied by reddening, a decline in star formation and an increased share of elliptical morphologies — an effect specific to the filament geometry itself and not reducible to local density (SDSS; COSMOS2015). The width of the transformation zone (~2.5–5 Mpc) matches the width of the filaments themselves.

Importantly, filaments in observations are dynamic objects. The flow of matter runs from voids through filaments to nodes; transverse velocities of galaxies in filaments show a coherent vortical signal with speeds up to ~100 km/s — filaments rotate slowly, forming the largest known rotating structures; along the filaments a spiral motion of galaxies is observed. In the pulsational picture, a filament is naturally interpreted as a channel of coherent propagation of pulsational perturbations between nodes: coherent motions and rotation are an expected property of such a channel, not an accident of the initial conditions.

**Discriminating test.** The age and star-formation gradient across the filament should depend on the dynamical state of the filament: in "cold" filaments with a strong coherent signal, the population reworking should be sharper than in "hot" ones. The check is possible by cross-matching filament catalogues (SDSS, the Bisous method) with MaNGA/CAVITY data at fixed mass and local density — an open test.

## 6.6. Quantitative estimates and simple formulas

Estimate of the local pulsation period: \(\tau_{pulse} \sim L / c_{vac}\), where L is the characteristic size of the region and c_vac is the effective speed of propagation of the pulsational perturbation, of order 0.01–0.1 of the speed of light. On the galactic scale (L ~ 10–50 kpc) at c_vac ~ 0.1c the period is \(\tau_{pulse} \sim 3\times10^{5}–2\times10^{6}\) yr, at c_vac ~ 0.01c — 3×10⁶–2×10⁷ yr; the range covers the observed recurrence intervals of active nuclei (1–10 Myr, 6.4). On void scales (L ~ 10–50 Mpc) the period reaches \(\tau_{pulse} \sim 3\times10^{8}–2\times10^{10}\) yr — consistent with the time over which void shells retain coherence (6.3). Thus a single formula covers both observed scales of episodicity — the local one (AGN cycles) and the large-scale one (void structure) — with no parameter tuning: the difference is a direct consequence of the difference in the scales L. The value of c_vac is a free parameter bounded from below by observations; a scale dependence of it is admissible. (Synchronized edit to 3.7: the illustrative value c_vac ~ 10⁻⁴c has been replaced by the range 0.01–0.1c.)

Evolution of the mass ratio with the age of the region: \(M_{DM}(t_{age}) / M_{b}(t_{age}) = (M_{DM}/M_{b})_{0} \cdot \exp(t_{age}/t_{p} - t_{age}/t_{DM})\), where t_p is the effective destruction time of the baryonic component and t_DM the effective fading time of dark matter. For t_p < t_DM the ratio grows with age — the direction is set in 6.2. An essential clarification: if t_p were identified with the standard proton lifetime, the observational limits (τ_p ≳ 10³⁴ yr) would make the spontaneous channel's contribution over the age of the Universe negligible (see the caveat in 4.3: the main observable channel is destruction in interactions). The substantive statement of the model is that both times are effective and cosmological in order of magnitude; then the evolution of the ratio over the age of the Universe amounts to factors of a few. The value of the effective time is not free: the evolution of the RAR scale parameter at intermediate redshifts — a doubling at z ~ 1 over ~8 Gyr (MUSE-DARK III) — fixes t_ev ~ 10¹⁰ yr. The order of magnitude of the evolutionary effect is thus set by observation; the direction of evolution is a discriminating test (6.2).

## 6.8. Empirical confirmation: 175 galaxies

### 6.8.1. Theoretical basis of RMEP and the fitting protocol

The regulated density of the vacuum component with exponential localization:

\[
\rho_{reg}(R) = \rho_{0}\,\exp(-R/\alpha).
\]

The contribution of the vacuum component to the circular velocity:

\[
V^{2}_{reg}(R) = \frac{4\pi G\rho_{0}\alpha^{3}}{R}\left[2 - \left(2 + 2R/\alpha + R^{2}/\alpha^{2}\right)e^{-R/\alpha}\right] = V_{0}^{2}\,\frac{2 - (2 + 2x + x^{2})e^{-x}}{2x},
\]

where \(x = R/\alpha\), \(V_{0}^{2} = 8\pi G\rho_{0}\alpha^{2}\). The full observed velocity:

\[
V^{2}_{obs}(R) = V^{2}_{gas} + V^{2}_{disk} + V^{2}_{bul} + V^{2}_{reg}(R;\,V_{0},\alpha).
\]

The baryonic components are taken from the SPARC catalogue (3.6 μm photometry, M/L = 1); the free parameters are the amplitude V₀ and the scale α.

**Properties of the profile (verified analytically and numerically).** The two forms of the expression are identical (agreement at machine precision). In the central limit (R ≪ α) the expansion gives \(V^{2}_{reg} = \frac{4}{3}\pi G\rho_{0}R^{2} + O(R^{3})\) — the exact behaviour of a uniform sphere of density ρ₀: the flat core is not an approximation but an exact property of the profile, the central density being exactly equal to the amplitude. In the outskirts the contribution passes into the point-mass asymptotics \(V^{2}_{reg} \to GM_{\infty}/R\), where the total mass \(M_{\infty} = 8\pi\rho_{0}\alpha^{3}\) is finite and exactly coincides with the integral of ρ_reg(R) over all space.

Thus the RMEP profile: reproduces the observed flat density core (resolving the "cusp–core" problem) as a structural consequence of the shape of ρ_reg(R), without feedback mechanisms; contains exactly two free parameters (V₀, α), with ρ₀ = V₀²/(8πGα²) a derived quantity — i.e. the local density of the vacuum component of a given galaxy, not a cosmological constant; has a finite total mass and the correct asymptotics \(V_{reg} \propto R^{-1/2}\) at large radii, which predicts a Keplerian segment at the halo edge (6.8.8). These properties justify the use of RMEP as a full-fledged alternative to the NFW profile in mass modelling; the quantitative comparison is given in 6.8.3–6.8.7.

**Protocol of comparison with NFW.** The sample is 175 SPARC disc galaxies with an extended set of rotation-curve points, 3391 points in total (4–115 points per galaxy; the discrepancy with the base catalogue publication — 3168 points — pertains to the data version and is stated as such). No quality filters are applied; a robustness check against the removal of low-quality points requires the main SPARC table (rotmod) and is formulated as an open check of the protocol. Velocity errors are the catalogue e_Vobs with no lower floor imposed; it has been verified that there are no zero errors in the file. The comparison is made at an equal number of free parameters (two each): RMEP — (V₀, α); NFW — (V₂₀₀, c) with the halo radius r₂₀₀ = 100·V₂₀₀/H₀ kpc (equivalently V₂₀₀/(10H₀) Mpc), H₀ = 70 km/s/Mpc. Parameter bounds: V₀ ∈ [0.1; 10⁴] km/s, α ∈ [0.01; 5000] kpc; V₂₀₀ ∈ [1; 2000] km/s, c ∈ [0.316; 10³]. The optimization is a grid search in logarithmic parameter space with a Nelder–Mead polish; parameters are clipped to the bounds at every optimization step (this is critical: unconstrained optimization wanders into an inadmissible region and distorts the count); starting points are the best grid nodes. Values at the boundary of the domain (α = 5000 kpc etc.) are treated as censored and analysed separately (6.8.4). A model's win is an advantage in χ² greater than one; |Δχ²| ≤ 1 is a statistical tie. The computation was performed on the open SPARC catalogue; the object-by-object table of results is appended to the hypothesis.

### 6.8.2. The sample

175 SPARC disc galaxies with measured rotation curves, 3391 points. Characteristics: distances 0.98–127.8 Mpc; maximum rotation velocities V_max = 17.8–383 km/s (here and below V_max is the maximum of the observed velocity along the curve); a bulge is included in the mass model for 32 galaxies (~18%). The catalogue is based on SPARC (Lelli et al. 2016, ApJS, 223, 21): 3.6 μm photometry and HI data.

### 6.8.3. Results: χ² comparison

RMEP gives the better description of the rotation curves in 61% of the galaxies (107 of 175; 21% — a statistical tie) at an equal number of free parameters. The median χ² improvement among RMEP wins is 50%; the median ratio of object-by-object χ² values among wins is 0.50.

The NFW wins (31 galaxies) are distributed unevenly across mass ranges in magnitude of margin: their share in the bins is 9–31%; the largest margins fall on massive spirals with steep inner curve segments (the largest χ² values of both models — NGC 5055 and analogues; the NFW lead reaches ~7×10³ χ² units). In dwarfs, NFW wins are isolated and with a minimal margin: the median V_max of NFW wins is 84 km/s, the median margin ~15 χ² units. Importantly, for the 25 galaxies with censored RMEP scales (6.8.4) there is no NFW advantage — they give statistical ties (see 6.8.4).

Both models show a tight relationship of object-by-object χ² (correlation coefficient in log space R² = 0.86): both are sensitive to the same features of the data, the difference being a systematic shift of level, not a change in the set of problematic objects.

### 6.8.4. The regulation scale α

The fitted values of α are distributed sharply non-uniformly. Among the non-censored solutions (n = 150) the median is 4.9 kpc; for 96 galaxies (55% of the sample) α < 10 kpc, for 31 (18%) α lies in the range 10–100 kpc, and for 25 (14%) the value reaches the upper bound of 5000 kpc. The bound was chosen deliberately above the radii of the observed points: exceeding it means the vacuum component is indistinguishable from zero over the observed part of the curve. Intermediate values are few: the model either finds a compact exponential scale or degenerates.

In the degenerate regime (25 galaxies fitted at the upper bound), the fitted α values are not a physical exponential scale: the profile degenerates into an almost constant density over the observed range. These solutions are censored and are not included in the regression analysis. Importantly: in this regime RMEP does not lose in a single case — all such galaxies give statistical ties, with no NFW wins among them. This corresponds to the situation where the curve is described within the errors by the baryonic components in both models.

In the compact regime (α < 100 kpc, n = 125) the scale systematically grows with the maximum rotation velocity. The regression in logarithmic coordinates:

\[
\alpha = A \cdot V_{max}^{1.89}\ \text{kpc} \quad (R^{2} = 0.29;\ \text{Spearman } \rho = 0.66;\ p \approx 3\times10^{-20}),
\]

where V_max is in km/s and A is a normalization amplitude. A bootstrap estimate (3000 resamples) gives a 95% interval for the exponent of [1.34; 2.41]. The resulting exponent differs substantially from the value 2/3 that follows from the baryonic Tully–Fisher relation under a power-law interpretation of the scale; formally, α ∝ V_max^1.89 corresponds to α ∝ M_bary^0.47 (for M_bary ∝ V_max⁴), i.e. the regulation scale grows with baryonic mass considerably faster than a linear power model. The exponent is compatible with a quadratic relation α ∝ V_max² — characteristic of dynamical scales; the exact form of the dependence and its physical interpretation are an open problem (6.8.7, Sections 7–8). Caveat: V_max here is the maximum of the observed velocity; catalogue V_flat values may shift the exponent — a cross-check is an open verification.

### 6.8.5. Core–cusp: resolving the problem

The NFW profile predicts ρ ∝ 1/r in the centre (a cusp), which systematically conflicts with the rotation curves of dwarf and low-surface-brightness galaxies, which indicate a constant central density (Flores & Primack 1994; Moore 1994; review — de Blok 2010). RMEP gives in the centre \(V^{2}_{reg} \propto R^{2}/\alpha^{2}\) — a constant volume density of the vacuum component: a natural flat core without additional mechanisms. As shown in 6.8.1, the flat core is not an approximation but an exact property of the profile.

The quantitative comparison (6.8.3) confirms the advantage, and its distribution over masses agrees with the very structure of the problem. The "cusp–core" contradiction is formulated for dwarf galaxies — and it is precisely in this region that RMEP wins steadily: in the V_max < 60 km/s bin its win share is 51.5% against 9.1% for NFW (with margins of ~9 versus ~4), in the 60–90 bin — 60% against 31%. In dwarfs, NFW wins are isolated and with a minimal margin. RMEP wins in every mass range; in the bins above 120 km/s its margin even grows (28–100 χ² units), while the largest NFW wins are localized in massive spirals, where the "cusp–core" contradiction is not pronounced.

**Discriminating test on the inner points (performed).** A χ² comparison over the full curve does not isolate the inner points on which the problem is focused. Therefore the comparison was performed separately for the inner points (R ≤ 2 kpc) of dwarf galaxies (V_max < 60 km/s; 28 objects with a sufficient number of inner points), at the parameters of the full fit — without a re-run. Result: RMEP wins on the inner points in 57% of cases, against 61% over the full curve. The prediction "the advantage intensifies on the inner points" was not confirmed: the advantage persists at the level of the full curve but does not increase. The status of the test is: performed, neutral outcome: it removes a potential argument against (the inner points are described by RMEP no worse), but provides no additional discrimination. A possible refinement — a threshold of R ≤ 1 kpc and samples with dense inner sampling.

### 6.8.6. The mass-discrepancy profile

The mass discrepancy \(D(R) = M_{dyn}/M_{baryon} = (V_{obs}/V_{baryon})^{2}\) is computed directly from the SPARC data without fitting (measure: D at the first and last measured radii of the curve). The empirical structure is known: the discrepancy arises below the characteristic acceleration g† ≈ 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² and systematically grows as it decreases; the relation is robust to the choice of M/L (McGaugh 2004; McGaugh et al. 2016). For the sample: in massive galaxies (V_max ≥ 150 km/s, n = 56) the median D at the first radius is 0.61 — the baryonic model over the centre yields the velocity with headroom (a consequence of M/L = 1 and the steep inner curves), and toward the periphery the discrepancy grows: the median D at the last radius is 2.08, with maximum values reaching 17.0. In dwarfs (V_max < 60 km/s) the discrepancy is present already in the central points: in 36% of objects D > 1.5 at the first measured radius (median 1.15) — consistent with the "a third of the sample" formulation.

In RMEP the discrepancy is explained not by invisible matter but by the contribution of the vacuum component: \(D = 1 + V^{2}_{reg}/V^{2}_{bary}\). The connection of the discrepancy with acceleration is structural by the construction of the profile: the relative contribution of the vacuum component grows as the baryonic acceleration \(g_{bar} \propto V^{2}_{bary}/R\) declines, and the scale of the transition is set by the ratio of V₀/α to the local baryonic field. A refinement of the status: the reproduction of the RAR here is conditional, not automatic — it holds under a correlation of the fitted (V₀, α) with the baryonic scale, which the regression of 6.8.4 does demonstrate. Whether the magnitude of the scatter matches the RAR's (0.13 dex in SPARC) is a separate open check.

### 6.8.7. Discharge-dependent profiles

The character of the vacuum-component profile is determined by the discharge regime (2.2, 4.5). In the deep gravitational wells of massive halos (~10¹²–10¹³ M☉) the pressure discharge dominates: the threshold is reached quickly, the discharge is sharp, and a profile close to NFW forms. In dwarf halos (~10⁹–10¹¹ M☉) the density discharge dominates: accumulation is slow, the discharge is soft, and the profile smooths into a constant-density core. Intermediate-mass halos occupy an intermediate position (cf. 6.1).

The quantitative test (6.8.3) confirms the picture. RMEP wins in every mass bin, and its median margin grows with mass: ~9 χ² units in dwarfs against 28–100 in bins above 120 km/s. Where the "cusp–core" contradiction is pronounced (dwarfs), RMEP wins with a steady margin; where the inner curves are steep (massive spirals), NFW gives local advantages — the largest χ² values of both models concentrate precisely there, and the profile to which the vacuum component approaches in the pressure-discharge regime reproduces the shape of an NFW-like steep centre.

**A necessary clarification on the nature of the vacuum component.** From the relation ρ₀ = V₀²/(8πGα²) it follows that the fitted density has a median of ~1.1×10⁷ M☉/kpc³ ≈ 8×10⁻²⁵ g/cm³ — 4–5 orders of magnitude above the cosmological dark-energy density (~6×10⁻³⁰ g/cm³). The vacuum component in RMEP is not identical to the cosmological Λ: it is an effective localized medium whose density is a parameter of a particular galaxy. A principal point: gravitationally the component behaves as a dust mass (an effective index w ≈ 0). It could not be otherwise: a component with w = −1 is distributed uniformly, does not cluster, and cannot produce a localized profile with finite mass and a declining rotation curve. The "vacuum" nature of the component is ontological: it is the energy of the medium in potential states (1.1), dark matter in a preceding form; its gravitational response is that of the matter it is to become.

The dependence of the density on mass is confirmed by the regression: ρ₀ decreases with V_max (slope in log coordinates −1.3): in dwarfs the vacuum component is compact and dense — a flat core in the centre; in massive galaxies it is rarefied and extended, its contribution to the curve small. Both parameters (α and ρ₀) are determined by a single dependence on the baryonic mass of the galaxy: the local density is not an independent fitting arbitrary.

### 6.8.8. The Keplerian segment at the halo edge

The RMEP profile has a finite total mass \(M_{\infty} = 8\pi\rho_{0}\alpha^{3}\) (6.8.1), so at radii well beyond the regulation scale α the contribution of the vacuum component passes into the point-mass asymptotics \(V_{reg} \propto R^{-1/2}\). For static NFW-type halos the decline toward the periphery is substantially weaker: the curve remains close to flat up to the last measured point. Thus, past the scale α, the predictions differ qualitatively: RMEP predicts a Keplerian segment, NFW does not.

The test is applicable to 72 of the 175 sample galaxies (R_last ≥ 3α at the fitted parameters; censored solutions with α = 5000 kpc are automatically excluded — for them the vacuum component is indistinguishable anyway). The check is possible using the outer points of the SPARC rotation curves, with sensitivity enhanced for objects with extended HI curves beyond the optical disc. An additional route is local kinematics: for the Milky Way the prediction reduces to the presence of a segment of declining V(R) beyond the regulation scale, testable with Gaia data. The test is open; its outcome at the fitted V₀, α is not tuned: the prediction follows from parameters already found, without additional degrees of freedom.

**Status of the section:** empirical, with an interpretive superstructure. The confirmed relations (the evolution of f_DM, the evolution of a₀, the AGN gradient, rings in voids, the episodicity of outflows) are published facts reproducible independently of the model; their interpretation in terms of discharges is the RMEP construction. The numerical fitting results of 6.8 were obtained by computation on the open SPARC catalogue under a fixed protocol; the parameter values are consistent with an independent recomputation. The open tests (S/R age coherence, the growth of M_DM/M_b with population age, the Keplerian segment on the 72 objects) are executable on existing data — the nearest test programme.

# Section 7. Open Questions and Development Paths

## 7.1. Mathematical formalization of the cycle

A closed minimal system describing the cycle "accumulation — failure — stabilization" is formulated as follows. The dynamic variable of the local level is the density of the vacuum component $u(R, t)$; its relaxation to a quasi-stationary profile is described by the equation

$$
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = -\frac{[u - u_*(R; V_0, \alpha)]}{\tau_s(R)} + S(R, t), \qquad u_*(R; V_0, \alpha) = \rho_0\,e^{-R/\alpha}, \qquad \rho_0 = \frac{V_0^2}{8\pi G\,\alpha^2},
$$

where $S(R, t)$ is the source related to the inflow of baryonic matter, and $\tau_s(R)$ is the relaxation time. The stationary solution at $S \to 0$ coincides with the RMEP profile applied in Section 6: thereby the fit to the rotation curves (6.8.1–6.8.3) uses the equilibrium solution of the system, and the numerical scheme must reproduce it.

Accumulation and failure are described by a scalar trigger variable $\Phi(t)$:

$$
\frac{d\Phi}{dt} = S_b(t) - \frac{\Phi}{\tau_\Phi}, \qquad \Phi \geq \Phi_c \;\Rightarrow\; \text{discharge event of duration } \tau_{br},
$$

where $S_b(t)$ is the source defined by the baryonic cycle (7.3). The regime parameter $q$ — the ratio of the discharge rate to the relaxation rate — separates the two modes of profile formation (by pressure / by density, 2.2) and fixes the mass separation of profiles (6.8.7): $q \gg 1$ — discharge by density, a smoothed core; $q \ll 1$ — discharge by pressure, a steep profile.

The global level is described by the evolution of the ratio of components:

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\,\ln\!\left(\frac{M_{\mathrm{vac}}}{M_b}\right) = \frac{1}{t_p} - \frac{1}{t_{DM}} > 0,
$$

where the quantity $t_{ev}$ is fixed by the observed evolution of the scale parameter of the RAR (6.2, 6.6), and the direction of the sign ($t_p < t_{DM}$) is a distinguishing test.

The system ties together all the quantitative elements of the model: the stationary equation reproduces the profile of the fit (6.8.1–6.8.3), the trigger level — the episodicity of outbursts and its dependence on scale (6.4, 6.6), the regime parameter — the separation of profiles by mass (6.8.7), the global equation — the evolution of the component ratio on the scale $t_{ev}$ (6.2). A terminological clarification: the object of evolution in all equations is the vacuum component — an effective localized medium with a **dusty gravitational response ($w \approx 0$, 6.8.7)**; the "vacuum" nature of the component is ontological (1.1): it is the energy of the medium in the form of potential states, not uniform dark energy. The growth of the ratio $M_{\mathrm{vac}}/M_b$ is observationally indistinguishable from the growth of the dark matter fraction.

## 7.2. Microphysics: the baryon-dependent minimum

The necessary refinement of the nature of the vacuum component. From the relation $\rho_0 = V_0^2/(8\pi G\,\alpha^2)$ it follows that the fitted density has a median of $\sim 1.1\times10^7\ M_\odot/\mathrm{kpc}^3 \approx 8\times10^{-25}\ \mathrm{g/cm^3}$ — by 4–5 orders of magnitude above the cosmological density of dark energy ($\sim 6\times10^{-30}\ \mathrm{g/cm^3}$). The vacuum component in RMEP is not identical to the cosmological $\Lambda$: it is an effective localized medium whose density is a parameter of a particular galaxy. The fundamental point: gravitationally, the component behaves as a dusty mass (effective index $w \approx 0$). There is no other way: a component with $w = -1$ is distributed uniformly, does not cluster and cannot produce a localized profile with a finite mass and a declining rotation curve. The "vacuum" nature of the component is ontological: it is the energy of the medium in potential states (1.1), dark matter in its preceding form; its gravitational response is that of the matter it will become.

Quantitative specification of the constants. The requirement on the local density of the vacuum component is established in 6.8.7: $\rho_0 \sim 10^7\ M_\odot/\mathrm{kpc}^3 \approx 8\times10^{-25}\ \mathrm{g/cm^3}$. Conversion to natural units: $1\ \mathrm{GeV^4} \approx 2.3\times10^{17}\ \mathrm{g/cm^3}$, whence $\rho_0 \approx 4\times10^{-43}\ \mathrm{GeV^4}$. *(In the previous edition the value was $\sim 4\times10^{-9}\ \mathrm{GeV^4}$ — a conversion error of ~34 orders of magnitude; corrected.)* For an axion-like field with the potential $V(\varphi) = m_a^2 f_a^2 \cdot [1 - \cos(\varphi/f_a)]$, the configuration storing the density $\rho_0$ has the amplitude

$$
\varphi_0 = \frac{\sqrt{2\rho_0}}{m_a} \approx 3\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{GeV} \approx 30\ \mathrm{eV} \quad (\text{at } m_a \sim 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{eV}),
$$

and the displacement angle

$$
\theta = \frac{\varphi_0}{f_a} = \frac{\sqrt{2\rho_0}}{m_a \cdot f_a} \approx 5\times10^{-20}.
$$

The value of $\theta$ is indeed independent of the choice of $m_a$: the relation $m_a f_a \approx \mathrm{const}$ gives a constant of $\sim 6\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{GeV^2}$ in the denominator, and $\theta \propto \sqrt{\rho_0}$. *(In the previous edition the value was $\theta \approx 0.02$ — the same conversion error; corrected.)*

The obtained value changes the physical picture — and for the better. The angle $\theta \approx 10^{-20}$ means that the field lies practically at the bottom of the effective potential: the density is stored not as a departure from the minimum but as the energy of coherent oscillations about it. An oscillating condensate at the bottom of the minimum behaves as cold coherent matter with a dusty gravitational response ($w \approx 0$) — an exact agreement with 6.8.7 and simultaneously a resolution of the former contradiction: the "vacuum" nature of the component does not require a vacuum equation of state, because the response is determined by the oscillations, not by the static field energy. Control of the density is implemented by the baryon-dependent minimum: the local baryon density and the gravitational potential shift the position of the minimum, and the adiabatic drift of the condensate following the minimum transfers energy — thereby $\rho_0$ becomes a parameter of a particular galaxy without exotic sources.

Connection with the system of 7.1. The switching of field configurations is the microscopic candidate for the role of the trigger $\Phi(t)$: accumulation corresponds to the drift of the configuration along the effective potential, failure to the transition between local minima over the time $\tau_{br}$, stabilization to the relaxation to a new minimum over the time $\tau_s(R)$. The regime parameter $q$ (7.1) determines which configuration is realized at a given gravitational potential — thereby the discharge dependence of the profiles (6.8.7) acquires a microscopic reading.

The program of concretization includes: (i) construction of an effective potential with a baryon-dependent minimum and determination of the coupling constant; (ii) verification of the stability of the configuration on times $\gg t_{ev} \approx 1.2\times10^{10}$ years (7.5); (iii) verification of the absence of forbidden gravitational effects at the scale of clusters — local densities five orders of magnitude above the cosmological one require special treatment; (iv) verification of the lower bound on $\tau_s(R)$: it must not exceed the dynamic times of structural events — a direct constraint from the Bullet Cluster (7.9). Until (i)–(iv) are completed, the line remains a hypothesis; its distinctive feature is concreteness: of all the lines considered, it alone specifies a particle and a parameter window consistent with known bounds.

## 7.3. The cycle as a source of geometry: the baryonic subsystem as a generator of local dynamics

At the foundation of the model lies the standard relation of general relativity: the energy–momentum distribution of baryonic matter determines the local geometry of spacetime through the Einstein equations. The specific claim of the hypothesis concerns not this relation but the character of the evolution of the source: the baryonic subsystem is treated not as a static distribution in a ready-made geometry but as a participant in the cycle "accumulation — failure — stabilization". Accumulation corresponds to a quasi-stationary regime in which the geometry evolves slowly; failure to an episodic discharge of the accumulated energy, in which the geometry undergoes a rapid rearrangement; stabilization to the relaxation of the new configuration to quasi-equilibrium.

Such a source generates not a static geometry but a sequence of quasi-equilibrium states separated by transitions. It is precisely the episodicity of the source that carries the structural load in the model: it sets the pulsational regime of the vacuum component (6.4, 6.6), explains the scale periodicity of large structures and determines the regulation profile through the regime parameter $q$ (6.8.7). Connection with the formalization: the baryonic cycle is the source $S_b(t)$ of the trigger equation (7.1) and, through the mechanism of switching configurations (7.2), determines which configuration of the vacuum component is realized in a given region.

The formulation "creation of space," used in working materials, has been replaced by a precise one: the model does not describe the generation of spacetime — it describes the control of its local geometry by a cyclic source. The question of the origin of spacetime itself lies beyond the scope of the model.

## 7.4. The scale problem: from the cosmological density to the galactic vacuum component

The cosmological density of dark energy is $\sim 6\times10^{-30}\ \mathrm{g/cm^3}$, whereas the density of the vacuum component at galactic scales, required by the fit to the rotation curves, is $\sim 10^{-25}\ \mathrm{g/cm^3}$ (6.8.7). The gap is about five orders of magnitude. In the proposed picture this gap differs fundamentally from the classical cosmological-constant problem: there, the discrepancy between the theoretical estimate and observation reaches $\sim 10^{123}$ orders of magnitude and has no proposed mechanism; here, the model replaces a universal constant with a local parameter of the galaxy, and what remains to be explained is a finite amplification of $\sim 10^5$. Three amplification mechanisms are proposed below; they are treated as mutually complementary, not as alternatives.

**Gravitational polarization.** The effective potential of the field depends on the local gravitational potential and the baryon density (7.2); this dependence sets the spatial form of the configuration and its dependence on the mass of the system — the origin of the scaling $\alpha(V_{\max})$ (6.8.4) and the trend $\rho_0$–$V_{\max}$ (6.8.7).

**The reservoir: the ocean of axions.** The cosmological background of the axion-like field with the parameters of 7.2 acts as a reservoir: the cycle "accumulation — failure — stabilization" extracts energy from the background locally on the scale $t_{ev} \sim 1.2\times10^{10}$ years (6.2, 6.6). Energy-conservation check: to accumulate $\sim 10^{-25}\ \mathrm{g/cm^3}$ with an average background of $\sim 10^{-30}\ \mathrm{g/cm^3}$ requires the depletion of a region of volume $\sim 10^5$ — in radius this is a factor of $\sim 50$, i.e. a depletion within several hundred kpc, which is compatible by order of magnitude with the scales of galaxy groups. The estimate is provisional and subject to quantitative development; its purpose is to show that the mechanism requires no exotic energy sources.

**Cascade amplification across the links of the chain.** The pulsational regime (6.6) sets the frequency of events: $\tau_{\mathrm{pulse}} \sim L/c_{\mathrm{vac}} \sim 10^5\text{–}10^7$ years per link of galactic scale. Over the time $t_{ev}$ the chain realizes $N \sim 10^3\text{–}10^5$ events; with an extraction of $\sim 10^{-30}\ \mathrm{g/cm^3}$ per event (of the order of the cosmological background), the total accumulation reaches the required $\sim 10^{-25}\ \mathrm{g/cm^3}$. It is essential that the estimate closes on itself: the number of events and the per-event extraction are consistent with the background without fitted parameters — if the background is orders of magnitude lower, the cascade does not have time; if substantially higher, the amplification is excessive. Thereby the required event frequency becomes a verifiable consequence.

**Division of roles.** Gravitational polarization sets the spatial form and the mass dependence; the ocean of axions — the reservoir and the energy budget; the cascade — the temporal dynamics and the connection with the episodicity of outbursts (6.4). The verification program includes: (i) the $\rho_0$–$V_{\max}$ trend from per-object parameters (in the queue of checks); (ii) the dependence of the density of the vacuum component on the environment (voids versus filaments — linkage with 6.4–6.5); (iii) the reconciliation of the total extraction with the observed surface density of baryons and of the background at the scales of groups.

## 7.5. Stability of the vacuum component

The stability of the configuration is provided not by an external stabilizing field but by the structure of the effective potential: the field is held in a local minimum, and the transition to a lower state ("decay of the false vacuum") requires overcoming a barrier. The observed stability of the vacuum component on cosmological times sets a lower bound on the height of the barrier: the lifetime $\tau_{\mathrm{vac}}$ must satisfy the condition $\tau_{\mathrm{vac}} \gg t_{ev} \approx 1.2\times10^{10}$ years (6.2) and exceed the age of the Universe. In the terms of 7.2, this is a constraint on the coupling constant and the depth of the baryon-dependent minimum — the first quantitative criterion of the concretization program (7.2, item (ii)): any micromodel of the mechanism giving a $\tau_{\mathrm{vac}}$ below the cosmological level is ruled out without appeal to observations.

The observational projection is the aggregate of null signals of decay and annihilation (prediction 5.6, with the synchronized correction of 4.7): no decay or annihilation signal is predicted by the model; every null result is a confirmation; a significant detection of a line without baryon accompaniment is a forbidding factor.

## 7.6. The mediator field: three roles

The model ascribes the function of linking the gravitational behavior of the vacuum component to the quantum dynamics of its microfield carrier to a single mediator — the axion-like field of 7.2 — without introducing a separate unknown field. Ground: it is precisely this field that already accounts for both ends of the required link — the gravitational side (a localized medium with a dusty gravitational response, 6.8.7) and the quantum side (a pseudoscalar field with parameters $m_a \sim 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{eV}$, $f_a \sim 10^{12}\ \mathrm{GeV}$, 7.2). Postulating a third carrier beyond it would mean a doubling of entities without added predictions; the question of the existence of a mediator field is thereby not removed but transferred to the status of a verifiable consequence: if an effect is found that requires a carrier beyond the axion-like field, the model will be extended precisely in that direction.

In the terms of the cycle "accumulation — failure — stabilization" (7.1), the mediator performs three distinguishable roles corresponding to the three phases:

- **The transition field** — the microscopic carrier of the failure event: the transition of the field configuration between local minima of the effective potential (7.2) over the time $\tau_{br}$. The observational projection — the episodicity of baryonic outbursts and its connection with scale (6.4, 6.6).
- **The barrier field** — the carrier of the barrier determining the stability of the configuration: the height of the barrier sets the lower bound on the lifetime $\tau_{\mathrm{vac}} \gg t_{ev} \approx 1.2\times10^{10}$ years (7.5, item (ii) of the program of 7.2). The observational projection — the aggregate of null signals of decay and annihilation (7.5).
- **The potential link** — the single relation connecting the effective potential (and, hence, the position of the minimum and the height of the barrier) to the local gravitational potential and baryon density (7.2, 7.4). The observational projection — the trends of the parameters $\rho_0$–$V_{\max}$ and $\alpha(V_{\max})$ (6.8.4, 6.8.7).

**Boundaries of the claim.** The model does not claim to solve the problem of quantum gravity: the link "gravity — quantum dynamics" is discussed only in the narrow sense — as the dependence of the effective potential of the field on the local gravitational potential and baryon density (7.2). All three roles reduce to this single relation, which makes the hypothesis economical: one mechanism — three functions — three observational projections, each of which enters the verification program (6.4, 6.6, 7.5).

## 7.7. Evolution of the component ratio with the age of the region

The model predicts the growth of the ratio of the vacuum component to the baryonic mass with the age of the region: $d/dt\ \ln(M_{\mathrm{vac}}/M_b) = 1/t_p - 1/t_{DM} > 0$ (7.1), with the characteristic scale $t_{ev} \approx 1.2\times10^{10}$ years fixed by the evolution of the scale parameter of the RAR (6.2, 6.6). The observational formulation of the prediction: in the late phases of the cycle (low redshifts, the scale of 0.6.5), the ratio of components is higher.

From the difference of the life cycles of the components follows the key relation: baryonic matter decreases faster than the dark component fades, so the ratio of the dark component to the baryonic mass grows monotonically with the age of the region (0.6.3, 2.7). On the scale of 0.6.5, the late phases are low redshift; hence, in the observational plane the ratio grows moving from high redshifts to the present. The direction coincides with prediction 5.2; a refinement of the measure: what is tracked here is the fraction of the dark component inside the effective radius of the disk — a quantity not identical to the global halo ratio of 5.2 but connected to it by the same mechanism.

Observational verification is possible in the range $z \leq 2.3$, where the dynamics of star-forming disks can be measured. The available data confirm the direction: the fraction of dark matter inside the effective radius of massive disks at $z \sim 1\text{–}2.3$ is $\sim 0.1\text{–}0.5$, whereas for local analogues with comparable rotation velocities it is $0.6\text{–}0.9$ (Genzel et al. 2020; Price et al. 2021; Parti et al. 2023). It is essential that the same picture is reproduced in the radial relation of accelerations: the RAR relation, established on the local SPARC sample, also exists at intermediate redshifts, but its scale parameter evolves with $z$: according to the MUSE-DARK III data, $a_0$ at $z \sim 1$ is $\sim 2.4\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ against the local $\sim 1.2\times10^{-10}$ — a doubling over $\sim 8$ billion years, registered with high significance. For static dark matter and a fundamental unchanging constant, such an evolution is not provided for; in the regulation picture, the "baryons — missing component" link depends on the epoch by construction, since the regulation itself is formed in time (6.8.4).

In the RMEP model the measured fraction of the dark component includes the vacuum regulation, so the observed growth of $f_{DM}$ with time is interpreted as the growth of the regulation: in the early phases the regulation is compact and dense with a small integral contribution, in the late ones it is extended. A quantitative description of the direction of the evolution of the scale parameter of the RAR is an open test.

Constraint: SPARC is a local sample ($z \approx 0$); a direct test of the evolutionary prediction belongs to the external checks of the hypothesis. For the local epoch, the prediction reduces to the requirement of monotonicity: for galaxies of one class, the ratio of the missing component to the baryonic one must systematically grow with the age of the stellar population. The check is possible on existing local data (stellar ages, colors, metallicity in addition to SPARC) — a separate open test.

## 7.8. Black holes as a node of the cycle

In the proposed model the black hole performs the role of a synchronization node of the cycle "accumulation — failure — stabilization," not an independent source of energy. Accretion corresponds to the accumulation phase: matter and energy are concentrated in the neighborhood of the hole; the discharge through jets and radio modes (6.4) — to the failure event; the relaxation of the gas subsystem — to the stabilization phase. Thereby the episodicity of active nuclei (6.4) and the pulsational regime (6.6) acquire a single carrier: the baryonic source $S_b(t)$ of the trigger equation (7.1) is implemented by the accretion cycle of the central supermassive hole, and the observed connection of AGN activity with galaxy morphology is a manifestation of the regime parameter $q$ (7.1, 6.8.7).

**A quantitative estimate of the energy share.** The total energy processed by the hole over the time $t_{ev} \approx 1.2\times10^{10}$ years is bounded from above by the radiative efficiency of accretion: for the growth of a hole to $M_{BH} \sim 10^7\text{–}10^8\ M_\odot$ with $\eta \sim 0.1$, the total processed energy is of the order of $M_{BH}\cdot c^2$. The mass of the vacuum component of a galaxy, by the profile of 6.8.1 ($M_\infty = 8\pi\rho_0\alpha^3$) with the canonical fit medians ($\rho_0 \sim 10^7\ M_\odot/\mathrm{kpc}^3$, $\alpha \sim 2.5\text{–}5\ \mathrm{kpc}$), is $\sim 4\times10^9\text{–}3\times10^{10}\ M_\odot$, i.e. its energy equivalent is of the same order in units of $M_\odot c^2$. The ratio is $\sim 0.002\text{–}0.02$: the energy returned through holes is a subdominant contribution to the energetics of the vacuum component; the main reservoir is the cosmological background (7.4, the "ocean of axions"). *(In the previous edition the share was estimated at 0.03–0.3 because of an underestimated component mass; the canonical medians of 6.8.4/6.8.7 raise it — the conclusion about the trigger, not source, role of the hole is strengthened.)* Consistency matters: the estimate is obtained from the observed AGN luminosity, independently of the fit parameters.

**Consequences and the verification program.** The role of a trigger, not a source, means a verifiable separation of functions: the temporal structure of the pulsations ($\tau_{\mathrm{pulse}}$, 6.6) must follow the accretion dynamics of the hole (the duty cycle, Kerr/Eddington times), whereas the amplitude of the profile of the vacuum component is determined by the reservoir mechanism of 7.4 and the potential (7.2), not by the power of the outburst. Verification: (i) a correlation of the fit parameters $(V_0, \alpha)$ with the mass of the central hole on the sample of 6.8.3 — the prediction of the presence of such a correlation follows from the $M_{BH}$–$\sigma$ relation and the link with the potential; (ii) a comparison of the kinetic energy of the jets with the energy deficit between failure events; (iii) objects without a significant AGN trace (dwarfs with small holes) must show a discharge by density without pronounced episodicity — agreement with the regime fork of 6.8.7.

## 7.9. Cluster collisions and dwarf nuclei

**The Bullet Cluster (1E 0657-56).** A classical observation of a collision of clusters: the lensing mass peak passes through the collision without noticeable deceleration, shifting away from the hot gas toward the stellar components, and the constraints on the self-interaction of the dark component give $\sigma/m \lesssim 1\ \mathrm{cm^2/g}$. In the proposed model there is no dark component with particles; its role is played by the vacuum medium (6.8.7). Collisionlessness in this picture is not a postulate and not a consequence of an equation of state but a property of the carrier: the configuration of a coherent field does not experience dissipative self-interaction and is not decelerated by the gas — while its gravitational response is dusty ($w \approx 0$), which ensures the correct dynamics of the mass peak. It is essential that the model agrees not only with the fact of the passage but also with the detail: the displacement of the mass peak toward the stellar components. The position of the minimum of the vacuum component is determined by the local gravitational potential (7.2, 7.4), and in a collision the potential follows the stars — the configuration follows it. From the observed dynamics follows the constraint on the relaxation time of the configuration: $\tau_s \lesssim 10^8$ years — a direct upper bound entering the program of 7.2 (item (iv)).

**Dwarf galaxies.** The smoothed core in dwarfs follows from the regime of discharge by density (6.8.7): at $q \gg 1$ the discharge in the cycle proceeds slowly and uniformly, and the stationary profile of the vacuum component $\rho_0\,\exp(-R/\alpha)$ (6.8.1) is realized with a flat center — precisely the form that observations record as the core. The elevated ratio of the vacuum component to the baryonic one in dwarfs is a consistent consequence of the scaling: $\alpha$ grows with $V_{\max}$ (6.8.4), $\rho_0$ decreases (7.4), while the relative contribution of the component in the deep part of the rotation curve of low-mass systems is larger — this enters the fit of 6.8.3 and requires no additional mechanics. We state separately: the dynamic link with "destruction of protons" and the fading of the dark component is not invoked (7.5, 7.7); the age trend of the component ratio (7.7) operates in all systems alike and is not a specific property of dwarfs.

## 7.10. Connection with existing theoretical approaches

**Decaying dark matter.** In these models dark matter is unstable: decay is an independent process with a specified channel and lifetime, and the decay products are the emission of a fixed set of particles. In the regulatory model, dark matter is a state of the medium (7.5), and the vacuum component is stable on cosmological times ($\tau_{\mathrm{vac}} \gg t_{ev}$, 7.5). The nearest analogue of "fading" in the model is the relaxation of the local configuration to the cosmological background upon the disappearance of the baryonic substrate: since the position of the potential minimum depends on the local baryon density (7.2), the loss of the substrate removes the holding, and the configuration disperses over the time $\tau_s(R)$ (7.1) without producing particles. The observational difference is fundamental: decaying DM predicts decay signals (γ-lines, leptonic channels); the model predicts their absence (7.5) and, simultaneously, the "fading" of the component only in regions emptied of baryons — a distinguishing test between the approaches.

**Axion models of the early Universe.** Common: a light pseudoscalar field and the idea of a condensate state. The difference is in the function of the field: in standard axion scenarios the emphasis is on the generation of the density of dark matter and the solution of the strong-CP problem; the model does not claim to solve the CP problem and uses the field functionally — as the carrier of the baryon-dependent minimum (7.2), the reservoir of the "ocean of axions" (4.2, 7.4) and the mediator with three roles (7.6). The smoothing of peaks at failures in the model is provided by the regime of discharge by density (6.8.7), not by the properties of the condensate.

Thus, the model borrows individual ideas (light scalar condensates, cyclicity, the dynamical nature of dark energy) but combines them in a different mechanism, where the gravitational field and the baryonic substrate act as regulators of the cycle, and the verifiable content is transferred to local, measurable quantities: profiles of the rotation curves (6.8), the $\rho_0$–$V_{\max}$ trend (6.8.4), the aggregate of null signals of decay and annihilation (7.5) and the episodicity of baryonic outbursts (6.4).

## 7.11. Destruction of baryons in extreme conditions: a quantitative estimate

Direct laboratory constraints on spontaneous proton decay — $\tau_p > 10^{34}$ years for the channel $p \to e^+ + \pi^0$ (Super-Kamiokande) — refer to intranuclear decay without external action and are not applicable to the dynamic destruction of baryons in astrophysical processes. In the model, the destruction of baryons is understood precisely as astrophysical and concentrated in the nodes of the cycle (7.8): accretion by black holes, tidal disruption of stars, fragmentation in cosmic rays of ultra-high energies.

**A quantitative estimate of the total contribution.** The dominant channel is accretion: the integral density of supermassive black holes, recovered from the cosmological AGN luminosity (the Soltan argument), is $\sim 4\times10^5\ M_\odot/\mathrm{Mpc^3}$, which corresponds to an accreted $\sim 5\times10^5\ M_\odot/\mathrm{Mpc^3}$ of baryons. With the cosmological density of baryons $\sim 6.7\times10^9\ M_\odot/\mathrm{Mpc^3}$, the fraction of baryons that passed through the horizon over the entire history is $\sim 10^{-4}$. Tidal disruption of stars contributes the same order or less (a rate of $\sim 10^{-5}\text{–}10^{-4}$ per galaxy per year at $\sim 0.5\ M_\odot$ per event), and the tidally disrupted object is for the most part accreted — the channel is not independent. Fragmentation in cosmic rays of ultra-high energies is negligible: the energy density of these particles is 8–9 orders of magnitude below the stellar one.

In total, the destruction covers $\lesssim 10^{-3}$ of the baryon density over cosmological time — by two to three orders of magnitude less than the effect of mass return by the stellar population (0.3–0.5 per $t_{ev}$, 7.7). Hence the status of the channel in the model: the astrophysical destruction of baryons is a real, observationally constrained process, but subdominant for the evolution of the component ratio (7.7): the carrying mechanism of the decrease of the baryonic mass on the scale $t_{ev}$ remains the mass return, not the destruction. Energetically, the accretion channel operates in the cycle as a trigger and source of outbursts (7.8): the processed $\sim 10^{-4}$ of the baryon density provide the observed AGN luminosity and feedback (6.4), without claiming the role of the main source of the energetics of the vacuum component (7.4).

## 7.12. Consistency with cosmological data

**The CMB.** The cosmological background (the "ocean of axions," 7.4) plays the role of standard dark energy: the model does not modify the acoustic peaks or the relic spectrum — at early times, energy of vacuum type is dynamically negligible both in standard cosmology and in the model. A possible contribution of the local component to the low multipoles proceeds through the integrated Sachs–Wolfe effect at late times: the formation of the component in halos (the stabilization phase, 4.4) adds to the isotropic $\Lambda$ an inhomogeneous contribution correlated with the large-scale structure — the signal is expected at the percent level and is statistically indistinguishable from the standard scatter of the low multipoles. The distinguishing test is the sign and angular correlation of the residuals of the low multipoles with the map of large-scale structures; the test is formulated as open (6.7).

**Type Ia supernovae.** The spatial inhomogeneity of the local vacuum component (6.8.7, 7.4) gives an observational projection not as a shift of the cosmological relation but as a dependence of the standardized luminosity of supernovae on the environment. It is essential that such a dependence is already observed: the step of the Hubble residuals by the stellar mass of the host ("mass step") is 0.04–0.1 magnitudes — supernovae in massive, old, passive galaxies are brighter; in locally active environments the step is weaker (by the literature data, $\sim 0.08$ against $\sim 0.04$ mag; the values require precise binding to the publications before the final version). This agrees with the trend of 7.7: late phases — a higher fraction of the vacuum component — a deeper potential. The distinguishing test between the model and the standard interpretations of the step (evolution of populations, dust): the correlation of the step not with the mass as such but with independent proxies of the local density of the vacuum component — the age of the stellar population (7.7) and the RAR residuals (6.8.3); the prediction of the model is a systematic coherence of the two proxies.

The explosive regime and the formation of dark energy: the accumulation in the stabilization phase (7.1, 7.4) and the trigger (7.8) are considered; the cosmological evolution of $\Lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}(z)$ is subject to Sections 9–11. The checks $H(z)$ and the cluster counter at $z > 2$ are planned; the priority is high — a quantitative estimate of $\tau_\Lambda$.

## 7.13. The standard window of the parameters of the axion-like field

The field is considered in the standard window of parameters of ultralight axion-like particles: $m_a \sim 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{eV}$, $f_a \sim 10^{12}\ \mathrm{GeV}$, with a fixed product $m_a \cdot f_a \approx 6\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{GeV^2}$. The required displacement of the minimum $\theta \approx 5\times10^{-20}$ (7.2) does not require tuning: it follows from the density $\rho_0$ and the fixed product $m_a f_a$. The coupling constants of the field with the baryonic substrate and with photons/nucleons remain free parameters of the model (7.2): the model does not solve the CP problem (7.10), and the numerical criteria of the program of 7.2 (items (i)–(iv)) and the condition $\tau_{\mathrm{vac}} \gg t_{ev}$ of 7.5 are the first quantitative constraints on them.

## 7.14. Fine tuning: what the model demands

The scales of the dynamics: the speed of the front $c_{\mathrm{vac}}$ (6.6), the phase times $\tau_{br}$, $\tau_s(R)$ (7.1, including the constraint $\tau_s \lesssim 10^8$ years from 7.9), the lifetime of the configuration $\tau_{\mathrm{vac}}$ (7.5). For the latter only the inequality $\tau_{\mathrm{vac}} \gg t_{ev} \approx 1.2\times10^{10}$ years is required — a constraint-inequality, not a fit of an exact value; the order of $c_{\mathrm{vac}}$ is consistent with the observational episodicity (6.6).

The constants of the axion-like field: the relation $m_a \cdot f_a$ gives pairs $(m_a, f_a)$ from the standard window (7.13); the required displacement of the minimum $\theta \approx 5\times10^{-20}$ (7.2) requires no tuning, since it follows from the density and the fixed product $m_a f_a$. The gap of the local amplification $\sim 10^5$ (7.4) is closed by three mechanisms with independently fixed frequencies and portions, without free parameters.

**An essential caveat.** Part of the structure the model postulates rather than derives: the form of the effective potential with a baryon-dependent minimum and a barrier (7.2). Until the coupling constant with the baryonic substrate is constructed microscopically, the model remains phenomenological — in the same sense in which ΛCDM is phenomenological with respect to the origin of $\Lambda$. The difference is that here the phenomenology is localized in a single element (the potential of the field) and is supplied with a concretization program (7.2, items (i)–(iv)) and a numerical criterion ($\tau_{\mathrm{vac}} \gg t_{ev}$), whereas in ΛCDM it is the observable quantity itself that remains unexplained.

The perspective of a complete solution is the construction of a microscopic theory of the potential, not the introduction of an additional field: the role of the mediator is performed by the axion-like field itself (7.6), and the extension of the set of fields is justified only upon the discovery of effects requiring a mediator beyond the axion-like field (7.6). The question of the origin of the potential is recorded as open.

**Empirical core.** The RMEP profile describes the rotation curves of 175 SPARC disc galaxies (3391 data points) better than NFW at an equal number of free parameters (two each): wins 61.1% (107 galaxies), statistical tie 21.1% (37), NFW wins 17.7% (31); "better or equal" covers 82.3% of the sample (Section 6.8, computed under a fixed protocol, per-object table appended). The advantage is distributed across masses exactly as the outburst-dependence predicts: in the low-velocity regime (V_max < 90 km/s) RMEP wins with a sustained margin, while in massive spirals with steep inner curves NFW gives the best fits — precisely what the regime fork q requires (7.1, 6.8.7).

**The regulation scale is set by baryons.** The fitted scales obey a regularity α ≈ 5 kpc·(V_max/100 km/s)^1.89 (95% bootstrap interval of the exponent [1.34; 2.41]; Spearman ρ = 0.66), which corresponds to α ∝ M_bary^0.47 (6.8.4): the regulation scale is not a free fitting arbitrariness but a function of the baryonic state of the system. The regulation density ρ_0 = V0²/(8πG·α²) is a derived quantity, a parameter of a given galaxy rather than a universal constant (6.8.7); the ρ_0–V_max trend (decline with mass) is a testable consequence (7.4).

**The "cusp–core" problem is solved structurally.** The exponential profile yields, in the centre, the exact density of a homogeneous sphere (the flat core is an exact property of the profile, 6.8.1), reproducing dwarf rotation curves without feedback mechanisms; at the same time, the finite total mass M_∞ = 8πρ_0α³ predicts a Keplerian decline at the edge of the halo — a qualitatively different prediction than NFW, applicable to 72 objects of the sample (6.8.8). Both characteristics follow from a single profile shape without additional assumptions.

**A unified dark-sector framework with separated roles.** The observed dark component of galaxies is the local vacuum component: the medium's energy in the form of potential states (1.1), gravitationally dust-like (w ≈ 0), held by the baryon-dependent minimum of the axion-like field (7.2). The uniform dark energy is a separate trapped component with its own dynamics (1.1, 3.5, 4.4). The model does not conflate them: the cosmological background inherits the role of the standard Λ (7.12), while local regulation describes the rotation curves (6.8) — and the volume average of the former automatically reaches the cosmological density of the dark component (consistency f ~ 2–4×10⁻⁵, 7.12).

**A test programme with falsifiability criteria — on three levels.** Galactic: the ρ_0–V_max trend from per-object parameters (7.4), the age test via RAR residuals (7.7), the correlation of (V0, α) with the central black-hole mass (7.8), the Keplerian decline (6.8.8). Cosmological: the evolution of the dark-energy-to-dark-matter ratio with cosmological time (4.6); strict constancy of both densities would be a refutation (5.3). Each criterion is independent; the model is obliged to differ from ΛCDM only at the predicted places, by its deviations across such a set.

**Theoretical contour.** Vacuum energy → gravitational regulation (1.3) → the regulatory cycle (accumulation → outburst → stabilisation → energy return; Section 2) → pulsational dynamics and wave mechanics (Section 3) → microphysics of the baryon-dependent minimum (7.2, 7.6) → evolution of the component ratio over t_ev ≈ 1.2×10¹⁰ yr (7.1, 7.7) → the maximal breakthrough and closure of the cycle (0.6.4, 3.2). Fine-tuning is reduced to a finite set of quantities: the inequality τ_vac ≫ t_ev, the trigger thresholds, and the axion-like field constants with a minimum offset θ ≈ 5×10⁻²⁰ that require no tuning (7.14); the phenomenological remainder — the potential shape — is localised in a single element and accompanied by a concretisation programme.

**Status of the open.** Unsolved fundamental problems of the hypothesis: (a) passing the description of gravity from the status "a property of the medium" (1.3) to the Einstein equations at the birth of mass — the main unfinished bridge of the model; (b) a rigorous connection between the primordial perturbation spectrum of the CMB and the mechanism of the global outburst (3.9); (c) a microscopic theory of the baryon-dependent potential (7.2); (d) a quantitative description of Λ_eff(z) evolution — the subject of the planned cosmological sections (9–11). All listed problems are accompanied in the hypothesis text by test programmes and are not among assumptions whose removal would destroy the model.

# Section 9. RMEP and Relativistic Gravity

## 9.1. Covariant Formulation of the Model

The regulatory model in its original formulation (Section 6) uses the Newtonian approximation: the vacuum component enters as an additional term in the circular velocity. Applying the model in strong gravitational fields and on cosmological scales requires a relativistic formulation.

The covariant setting of RMEP is the standard Einstein equations with an additional dynamical source:

G_{μν} + Λ·g_{μν} = (8πG/c⁴)·[T^baryon_{μν} + T^φ_{μν}],

where φ is the axion-like field (7.2) with an effective potential V_eff(φ; ρ_b, Φ_grav) depending on the local baryon density and the gravitational potential (7.2, 7.4). The local vacuum component is an equilibrium configuration of φ: its profile ρ_0·exp(−R/α) is a quasi-stationary solution of the relaxation equation 7.1, not a postulate about the metric. The dark component enters T_{μν} as a real energy density of a coherent condensate — with a dust-like gravitational response (w ≈ 0, 6.8.7), finite mass M_∞ = 8πρ_0α³, and standard lensing.

**Why not a Λ_eff(R) modification.** An alternative setting — replacing Λ with a locally regulated function Λ_eff(R) = Λ·F(ρ_b), suppressed in the centre and approaching Λ at the periphery — was considered and rejected. Three independent arguments against it:

**Kinematics.** The contribution of the Λ-term to the circular velocity is V_Λ² = −(Λc²/3)R². A positive Λ is repulsive: its contribution reduces the rotation velocity and grows quadratically with radius. No monotonic Λ_eff ≥ 0 reproduces flat curves and the mass deficit — it produces the opposite effect.

**Conservation.** Λ_eff(R) ≠ const requires an exchange term: ∇^μT^baryon_{μν} = (c⁴/8πG)·∂_νΛ_eff — baryons would have to feel a force along the Λ_eff gradient, which is not observed. The exchange term is physically correct only as a consequence of the equation of motion of a real field: the carrier of the exchange is φ. Thus an "Einstein modification without a field" is internally inconsistent, while with a field it is redundant — the field already does all the work.

**Empirics.** The fit of 6.8 shows dust-like behaviour of the component (finite mass, Keplerian asymptotics, localised dense cores in dwarfs) — the behaviour of a condensate, not of vacuum pressure.

The Λ_eff(R) notation survives only as effective shorthand in regimes where the condensate is quasi-uniform; the fundamental object of the model is the field φ.

**The formalisation bridge.** This section closes the main unfinished bridge of the hypothesis: coupling the ontology of 1.3 ("gravity as a property of the medium") with the Einstein equations. The fix: the gravitational sector is standard GR in all post-outburst regimes; the specific content of RMEP resides entirely in the source — the field φ. The formulation of 1.3 refers to the pre-mass regime (the regulatory dynamics of the medium itself, Sections 2–3); the transition at the birth of mass is not a modification of geometry but the appearance of sources. The baryon cycle (7.3) proceeds in the standard way: baryons curve spacetime per Einstein, the φ configuration follows the baryon potential through V_eff, and it is precisely the condensate distribution that modifies the local geometry in practice. In the classical limit the bridge is closed; a rigorous derivation of the pre-mass regime from a microscopic theory is an open problem (Section 13).

**The regulation scale α is not a free parameter:** it is calibrated empirically — α ≈ 5 kpc·(V_max/100 km/s)^1.89 (exponent 1.89, bootstrap interval [1.34; 2.41]; 6.8.4) — and is thereby tied to the baryonic properties of the system.

The key difference from ΛCDM is not in the metric (the metric is standard) but in the nature of the dark source: not a population of non-interacting particles with an arbitrary formation history, but a relaxing configuration of the medium that tracks the baryonic state (7.1–7.2). The consequence is a finite total regulation mass and the prediction of a Keplerian decline (6.8.8), absent in NFW halos.

## 9.2. The Weak-Field Limit

In the weak-field limit the relativistic formulation passes into the Newtonian potential with the condensate contribution:

Φ(R) = Φ_N(R) + Φ_reg(R), Φ_N(R) = −G·M_baryon(R)/R,

where Φ_reg(R) is the Newtonian potential of the spherical configuration ρ_0·exp(−R/α). Exact agreement with Section 6: the contribution to the circular velocity

V²_reg(R) = G·M(<R)/R = (4πG·ρ_0·α³/R)·[2 − (2 + 2R/α + R²/α²)·e^(−R/α)]

is precisely the formula of 6.8.1 used in fitting the 175 rotation curves. The relativistic setting reproduces the empirical profile in the weak-field limit without adjustment.

Limiting regimes: in the centre (R ≪ α) — a harmonic potential, V²_reg = (4/3)πG·ρ_0·R², the exact behaviour of a homogeneous sphere; at the periphery (R ≫ α) — a Keplerian decline V²_reg → G·M_∞/R, predicting the fall of the curve beyond the regulation scale (6.8.8). The flat parts of observed curves are formed by the slow growth of M(<R) in the transition zone — not by a "constant plateau": a constant potential gives zero contribution to the velocity, and the earlier parameterisation with saturation (1 − e^(−R²/α²)) fails in this role mathematically, not merely empirically.

ρ_0 is the locally effective condensate density: after the cascade amplification (7.4) it exceeds the cosmological average by five orders of magnitude; it is with this enhanced value that the model reproduces the rotation curves of Section 6. The cosmological average contributes negligibly to galactic dynamics.

The weak-field limit applies to the overwhelming majority of observed galaxies (g_N ~ 10⁻¹⁰–10⁻⁸ m/s²) — this is precisely the regime of the analysis of the 175 rotation curves (Section 6).

## 9.3. Strong Field: Black Holes

In strong gravitational fields (R ~ r_s, where r_s is the Schwarzschild radius) the Einstein equations are not modified: the φ configuration is a source on a par with any matter, and its local density is finite and small.

**Estimating the correction — via densities, not only scales.** The average density of a black hole inside the horizon is ρ̄ = 3c⁶/(32πG³M²): for M ~ 10⁸ M☉ it is ~1.8 g/cm³, for limiting supermassive objects (10¹⁰–10¹¹ M☉) — ~0.02–0.002 g/cm³. The ratio of the condensate density to the mean is ρ_0/ρ̄ ~ 10⁻²⁴–10⁻²⁶: the correction to the metric at the horizon is negligible — nineteen orders of magnitude stricter than the earlier estimate via r_s/α ≤ 10⁻⁵, which is retained as an additional argument for the separation of scales (r_s ~ 10⁻⁷–10⁻² pc versus α ~ kpc).

**Key conclusion:** black holes require no modification of standard theory. The horizon properties, Hawking radiation, and thermodynamics are preserved, since the condensate is an ordinary source with finite density, incapable of competing with the curvature at the horizon. Black holes in RMEP are nodes of energy return (0.6.6, 7.8) untouched by regulation itself. Open question: the dynamics of φ in the vicinity of the hole — accretion of the condensate, possible cloud-like bound configurations (analogous to axion superradiance effects known in standard physics); the formulation is in the Section 13 programme.

## 9.4. Energy Conservation in RMEP

In the covariant formulation the condensate is represented by its own stress-energy tensor T^φ_{μν}. Since the field φ obeys its own equation of motion, the full conservation law

∇^μ[T^baryon_{μν} + T^φ_{μν}] = 0

holds identically rather than being postulated. Each sector is separately conservative; the exchange between the condensate and the baryonic medium is mediated by the dependence of V_eff on the baryon density: a change of the substrate changes the energy of the configuration — the exchange term is proportional to the V_eff gradient and, in the effective description, reproduces the structure "∇T^vac_{μν} ∝ ∂_νΛ_eff" already as a rigorous consequence, without violating the Bianchi identities. At the micro level, the suppressed contribution passes into the configuration of the regulatory field (7.2, 7.4); the identification of an explicit Lagrangian formulation is a Section 13 task.

For a galaxy — a stationary system — the total energy, including the condensate configuration, is well defined and conserved. The balance statement refers to bound stationary systems; in the expanding Universe the global balance is undefined (9.4.1) — as in ΛCDM.

### 9.4.1. The Transfer Channel and Scale Estimates

The locally covariant conservation law holds in all regimes of the model. The suppression of vacuum energy in the presence of matter does not mean its disappearance: the redistribution proceeds between the condensate configuration and the medium through the V_eff dependence. Scale estimate: the material equivalent of the exchange flux on galactic scales (≤10⁵–10⁶ M☉) is five to six orders of magnitude below the regulation mass — the dynamics is set directly by the condensate distribution, not by a redistribution of energy into material form. The identification of the transfer channel in an explicit Lagrangian formulation is a Section 13 task. The global energy balance in the expanding Universe is undefined — just as in ΛCDM; the energy of reddening photons is not transferred into any substrate: the uniformity of the CMB spectrum, measured to ~10⁻⁴ precision (FIRAS), rules out any absorption channel at a level above 10⁻⁴ of the radiation density.

# Section 10. Comparison with MOND and Relativistic Generalizations

## 10.1. Phenomenology of MOND

MOND (Modified Newtonian Dynamics) modifies the laws of dynamics below an acceleration scale
$a_0 \approx 1.2\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. With a single free parameter it reproduces the principal
empirical regularities of galactic dynamics: flat rotation curves, the baryonic Tully–Fisher relation
and the radial acceleration relation (RAR). For RMEP this phenomenology is the closest competitor:
it explains the same data with fewer parameters and without dark matter.

However, MOND is a phenomenology: the transition function is postulated without a microscopic
justification. It also has established limits of applicability: in clusters of galaxies MOND removes
only part of the missing mass (a residual of about a factor of two requires neutrinos of mass around
2 eV or an additional component); gravitational lensing is not described within the Newtonian frame
of MOND; cosmology requires relativization, and the relativistic generalizations after GW170817 are
squeezed by a hard limit on the speed of gravity (10.4).

## 10.2. The transition regime of RMEP and the $a_0$ scale

In RMEP the transition to the "modified" regime arises from the shape of the profile: when the
baryonic acceleration exceeds the scale of the regulation field, the condensate contribution is small;
below the transition acceleration the regulation contribution begins to dominate and the rotation
curve enters a flat segment. The transition acceleration in the model is not a postulate but a
consequence of the ratio between the amplitude and scale of the configuration $(V_0, \alpha)$ and the
local baryonic field (6.8.6): the relative contribution of the regulation grows as
$g_{\mathrm{bary}} \propto V^2_{\mathrm{bary}}/R$ decreases, by construction of the profile.

Key difference in the status of parameters. In MOND $a_0$ is a universal constant fitted to the data
once. In RMEP there are two free parameters per galaxy $(V_0, \alpha)$, but the fit of 6.8 shows that
both are determined by the baryonic properties of the system:
$\alpha \approx 5\ \mathrm{kpc}\cdot(V_{\max}/100\ \mathrm{km/s})^{1.89}$ (bootstrap interval of the exponent
[1.34; 2.41]; Spearman $\rho = 0.66$), while the density $\rho_0 = V_0^2/(8\pi G\alpha^2)$ is a derived
quantity with a measured decreasing trend with mass (6.8.7). Thus the number of independent degrees
of freedom per galaxy is in fact reduced to a function of the baryonic state — this is a result of the
calculation, not a postulate.

Test of the universality of the transition acceleration (calculated over the sample; regulation is
indistinguishable from zero for the deep-limit objects, and the 12 systems with baryon dominance
over the entire curve are automatically excluded): the median transition acceleration is
$0.14\,a_0$ ($a_0 = 1.2\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$), the 16–84 percentile width is 0.55 dex.
For comparison: the MOND-equivalent point where the addition equals the baryonic contribution follows
from the deep limit ($\sqrt{g_{\mathrm{bar}} a_0} = 2 g_{\mathrm{bar}}$) and equals $0.25\,a_0$ — the RMEP
median lies within half a dex of it. The outcome of the test is intermediate between criteria (a) and
(b): the order of magnitude of the transition acceleration is reproduced over the whole sample (no
failure under criterion (c)), but a systematic trend with mass is revealed — Spearman 0.56
($p \sim 10^{-12}$), with bin medians growing from $0.07\,a_0$ at $V_{\max} < 60\ \mathrm{km/s}$ to
$0.21\,a_0$ at $V_{\max} > 200\ \mathrm{km/s}$. The model thus predicts a mass dependence of the
transition acceleration — a property absent from the constant $a_0$ of MOND and accessible to
independent verification on samples where the transition acceleration is measurable separately from
the fit: a distinguishing prediction added to the verification program.

## 10.4. Relativization of MOND (TeVeS and descendants)

The TeVeS theory and its modifications solve the lensing problem at the cost of complexity: two
metrics, a vector field, additional free functions. Observations of gravitational waves (GW170817:
the speed of gravity equals the speed of light to an accuracy of ~10⁻¹⁵) narrowed the admissible
class of such theories — most vector-tensor realizations with a dynamical metric have been ruled out.

RMEP builds its relativistic formulation more simply and in a different place: the gravitational sector
is not modified at all (9.1), and the specific content lives in a single dynamical scalar field φ with a
baryon-dependent effective potential. The propagation speed of metric gravitational perturbations is
the speed of light by construction: GW170817 does not constrain the model. The limiting factors for φ
are the sensitivity of the condensate to strong fields (9.3) and the program of concretizing the
potential (7.2). A full Lagrangian formulation is a task of Section 13.

## 10.5. Summary comparison

RMEP does not reject MOND as a phenomenology — it reproduces its main empirical support (the radial
correlation of the discrepancy with acceleration, 6.8.6) and explains its origin: the transition regime
is a consequence of the shape of the regulation profile and its connection to the baryonic state
(10.2). The calculation with per-object parameters (10.2) gives a median transition acceleration of
$0.14\,a_0$ — within half a dex of the MOND-equivalent equality point ($0.25\,a_0$) — with a 0.55 dex
spread and a systematic trend with mass. RMEP reproduces the $a_0$ phenomenon by order of
magnitude without postulating a constant and adds to it a testable mass dependence. At the same
time RMEP provides what MOND lacks: a relativistic formulation without metric modification (9.1),
standard lensing, a finite total regulation mass with the prediction of a Keplerian segment in 72
objects of the sample (6.8.8), and a cosmological generalization without add-ons. A possible
analogue of the MOND external field effect in RMEP is the dependence of the configuration on the
environment through $V_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (9.1, 7.4); its quantitative implementation — calculating this
analogue on simulations — is included in the development plan (13.4).

# Section 11. Cosmological Evolution

## 11.1. Frame: standard background and two components

The relativistic formulation (9.1) fixes: the gravitational sector is standard GR, the Friedmann
geometry is not modified. The cosmological dynamics is determined by the sum of components:
baryonic matter ($\rho_b$), the local vacuum component ($\rho_0$) — localized condensate configurations
with a dusty gravitational response ($w \approx 0$, 6.8.7) — and uniform dark energy, the cosmological
background (1.1, 7.12).

Key distinction of two levels of evolution. Equation 7.1 —
$d/dt\ \ln(M_{\mathrm{vac}}/M_b) = 1/t_p - 1/t_{DM}$, $t_{ev} \approx 1.2\times10^{10}$ years — describes the
evolution of the regulation *within* a bound system: in the halo of a given galaxy the dark-component
fraction grows with age. This is not the evolution of the volume average: the integral cosmological
density of the component equals $\bar{\rho} \approx f\cdot\rho_{\mathrm{local}}$ (7.12) and reaches the standard
$\Omega_{cdm} \approx 0.26$ by matching the filling fraction $f \sim 2\text{–}4\times10^{-5}$ — consistency,
not a fit. Meanwhile, what grows with time is not the density but the *degree of organization*: the
fraction of the component inside halos and disks, the depth of the regulation's connections to the
baryonic substrate. The cosmological average behaves as matter; the structural distribution evolves.

## 11.2. Background dynamics: H(z) and BAO

From 11.1 follows the central cosmological prediction of the hypothesis — or rather, its absence:
**H(z) in RMEP agrees with ΛCDM within the errors**. The geometry is standard, the volume average of
the material components is standard, the Λ background is standard. The earlier formulation "H(z)
lower by 5–10% at z > 2" belonged to the retired branch with suppressed $\Lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and is not
part of the hypothesis.

This agreement is a strong constraint on the model itself, not a neutral background: the exponential
growth of the component ratio inside a system (7.1) does not carry over to the volume average —
otherwise the total matter density at z ~ 2 would be twice below the standard value, which is
refuted by the existence of massive clusters at z ~ 1–2 and by the BAO data. The consistency of the
two levels — structural and volumetric — is a mandatory requirement of the correctness of the
hypothesis.

BAO. The scale ruler is imprinted at recombination, long before the local configurations form; their
later accumulation does not shift the comoving scale (7.12). The prediction is standard BAO without
regional variations; the absence of deviations is a consistency check of the model, not a
distinguishing test.

## 11.3. CMB

The model does not modify the relic spectrum: at early times the contribution of the local component
is dynamically negligible, and the background inherits the standard Λ-cosmology. Additional effects
appear in the low multipoles through the integrated Sachs–Wolfe effect: the formation of the
component in halos adds an inhomogeneous contribution correlated with large-scale structure, at the
percent level — statistically indistinguishable from the standard scatter (7.12). The distinguishing
test is the sign and angular correlation of the low-multipole residuals with the map of large-scale
structures; the status is open.

## 11.4. The equation of state w(z)

The background component of the model has $w = -1$ by construction — it is the standard
cosmological constant; the local vacuum component has a dusty gravitational response
($w \approx 0$, 6.8.7) and does not evolve against the background (11.1).

Hints from DESI of evolving dark energy (a $w_0 w_a$ signal of up to ~4σ depending on the data
combination) concern the background component and constitute a distinguishing test with two
outcomes for the model: (a) if the evolution is finally confirmed — the model provides a mechanism
absent in ΛCDM with a constant: the leakage channel and the weakening of feeding (1.1, 4.6;
prediction 5.3 — the evolution of the dark-energy-to-dark-matter ratio with cosmological time);
(b) if strict constancy is established — the falsification criterion recorded in 5.3: the leakage
channel loses its observational support. Both outcomes preserve the background and structural
consistency of the hypothesis; only the status of one branch of 4.6 differs.

## 11.5. Structure formation and the cluster count

The background growth of perturbations D(z) at the standard volume average is standard. Specific
effects are possible only through the relaxation of the condensate: the time $\tau_s(R)$ is bounded
from above by the Bullet Cluster observation ($\tau_s \lesssim 10^8$ years, 7.9) — the configuration
follows the baryonic potential quickly, and gravitational structure formation proceeds essentially
standardly. The earlier prediction of "an excess of massive clusters at z > 2" belonged to the retired
branch with suppressed $\Lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and is not part of the hypothesis: with the standard
background Λ and volume matter density, early structure formation is not accelerated.

## 11.6. The mass step of SNe Ia

The spatial inhomogeneity of the local regulation (7.4) projects onto observations not through a shift
of the cosmological relation, but through the dependence of the standardized luminosity of
supernovae on the environment. The observed mass step (0.04–0.1 mag: supernovae in massive, old,
passive hosts are brighter) agrees with the trend of 7.7 — later phases, higher regulation fraction,
deeper potential. The distinguishing test (7.12): the correlation of the step not with mass as such,
but with independent proxies of the local regulation density — the stellar population age and the
RAR residuals. This is the only cosmological test of the current section with distinguishing power on
existing data (Pantheon+, DES).

## 11.7. Consistency with evolutionary tests at the structural level

Three observationally confirmed effects form the backbone of the section — all of them structural,
not background:

1. **Evolution of the dark-component fraction inside the $R_e$ of disks:** ~0.1–0.5 at z ~ 1–2.3
against 0.6–0.9 locally (Genzel et al. 2020; Price et al. 2021; Parti et al. 2023) — a direct
confirmation of the direction 7.1/7.7: the regulation fraction in the structure grows with time.
2. **Evolution of the RAR scale parameter:** $a_0$ at z ~ 1 is doubled relative to the local value
(MUSE-DARK III; 6.2, 6.6) — the "baryons–regulation" connection depends on the epoch, as the
cyclic picture requires, and is forbidden for static dark matter with immutable constants.
3. **The mass step of supernovae** — the correlation of luminosity with the stellar mass and age of
the hosts; interpretation through the potential depth (11.6).

ΛCDM has partial mechanisms for each of the three (the evolution of baryonic physics with z,
population effects); the distinguishing power of these tests lies not in the facts themselves but in
their consistency with a single scale $t_{ev} \sim 10^{10}$ years, fixed independently in the RAR
evolution (7.1, 6.6). The coincidence of the three scales — RAR evolution, growth of the component
fraction, age dependence of the residuals — is a quantitative task for the nearest verification (7.16).

# Section 12. Black Holes in RMEP: Two Formation Mechanisms

Below are two physically consistent formation mechanisms for black holes, based on a single
parameterization $(A, \alpha)$ and the common field foundation of RMEP. Both scenarios preserve
agreement with the key observational tests (EHT, LIGO/Virgo) and offer an alternative treatment of
the central region of compact objects without a singularity.

## 12.0. Relation to Section 9.3 and the ontological frame

Section 9.3 describes the external dynamics of black holes in RMEP: the metric near the horizon is
standard, Hawking radiation and thermodynamics are preserved unchanged, since the specific content
of the model — the condensate configurations — is significant at scales of ~$\alpha$ (kiloparsecs),
not at the scales of the Schwarzschild radius $r_s$. The present section concerns a different region
— the internal structure of the central part of a compact object, which is not testable by direct
observation and is, within the model, a physical interpretation (a development of 0.6.6, "Black
holes as an energy-return mechanism"). The external observational predictions — the black hole
shadow (EHT), gravitational waves (LIGO/Virgo) — are not modified: the observations agree with the
model but neither confirm nor refute the internal field picture. The difference from GR manifests
itself only in the central regions and is not yet observationally testable.

Separation of two quantities. At galactic scales (6, 9.1–9.2) the model describes the regulation as
a real condensate density with a flat core — $\rho_0 = V_0^2/(8\pi G\,\alpha^2)$ at the center with a
fall-off at the scale $\alpha$ — entering the stress–energy tensor as ordinary mass (9.1). The
present section considers a different quantity: the ansatz
$\rho(r) = A/[r\cdot(1 + \alpha r)^2]$ describes the density of the regulatory field in the central
structure of a compact object — the distribution of the field in the zone where the field dynamics
takes over the structure-forming role. The coincidence of the symbol $\alpha$ in both formulas is a
claim of the model: the regulation scale is single (in the ansatz $\alpha$ enters the combination
$(1 + \alpha r)$, i.e. acts as an inverse scale, while in the galactic profile it acts as a length;
the unity means one physical scale in two representations). The profiles are nevertheless distinct:
the galactic one has a flat core (6.8.5), the compact one has a cusp shape with an integrable
divergence $\rho \propto r^{-1}$ at the center. They must not be identified: the numerical check
shows that the ansatz is structurally different from the galactic profile (12.3), and transferring
the galactic parameterization to compact objects is a hypothesis to be tested (13.4).

## 12.1. Variant 1: black hole formation via collapse of a massive stellar core

**Physical basis of the scenario.** The basic picture is standard: exhaustion of the thermonuclear
fuel of a massive star leads to loss of pressure support and gravitational collapse of the core.
RMEP supplements this picture with a field treatment of the central region: instead of a
singularity, a stable field configuration with parameters $(A, \alpha)$ forms.

**Critical mass threshold.** For remnant masses above ~2–3 solar masses the pressure of degenerate
neutrons can no longer balance gravity. Within RMEP, the particle pressure is replaced by field
stability: the regulatory field forms a structure that prevents a singularity.

**Event horizon and observable boundary.** The horizon forms at $R < R_s = 2GM/c^2$. The model does
not revise the physics at observationally accessible scales, and therefore retains the predictions
for the shadow (EHT) and gravitational waves (LIGO/Virgo).

**Microphysics and the transition to the field picture.** At subnuclear densities the notions of
"atom" and "molecule" lose meaning; in GR this leads to a singularity as a mathematical limit. In
RMEP, stability is maintained by the dynamics of the regulatory field: the density grows, but the
central behavior is determined by the field profile (point 5).

**Parameterization and empirical connection.** The central structure is described by the ansatz
$\rho_{\mathrm{RMEP}}(r) = A/[r\cdot(1 + \alpha r)^2]$. The relation of the parameters was recomputed
by direct fitting of the ansatz to the rotation curves of the SPARC sample (175 galaxies, fitted M/L
from the appendix, $\chi^2$-minimization: grid + Nelder–Mead with hard bounds). The relation
$A \propto \alpha^{-2.26}$ ($R^2 = 0.727$) — a result of an earlier version with a different profile
— is not confirmed by the recomputation: acceptable solutions are 57 out of 171 (the rest are
censored at the boundaries, predominantly degenerate ansatz solutions beyond the data range; median
$\chi^2_{red}$ of the acceptable solutions 2.26); over them the A–$\alpha$ relation is weak — slope
0.35, bootstrap 95% CI [0.08; 0.65], $R^2 = 0.23$ — while the amplitude $A$ tracks the mass
(Spearman with $V_{\max}$ = 0.69, $p \sim 3\times10^{-9}$). The old correlation is acknowledged as a
version artifact and archived; the one-parameter nature of the ansatz in the form "$\alpha$
uniquely determines $A$" is withdrawn; the reconciliation of $(A, \alpha)$ is an open task (13.4).
Central behavior: as $r \to 0$ the density formally diverges as $r^{-1}$, but the divergence is
integrable — the total mass within any finite radius is finite:
$dM = 4\pi r^2\rho\,dr \propto r\,dr \to 0$. Physical regularization of the center (density
saturation at scales $< \alpha$) is a task for the next stage of numerical modeling.

**Growth dynamics and stability.** Adding mass increases the amplitude $A$ — the mass tracking is
established by the recomputation (Spearman 0.69); a fixed power-law relation $A(\alpha)$ is not
confirmed. Growth without "catastrophic collapse" is provided by the stability of the field
configuration, not by a parameter correlation.

## 12.2. Variant 2: black hole formation as a vortex of the regulatory field

**Primacy of the field over matter.** In this scenario the black hole arises not through matter
collapse but as a stable vortex configuration of the regulatory field upon reaching critical
gradients of curvature and energy density. Matter may accumulate only after the vortex has formed.

**Formation conditions.** The vortex forms in regions with threshold gradients. Possible channels:
density fluctuations in the early Universe (primordial vortices); tidal interactions in dense
clusters and in galaxy mergers; turbulent accretion regimes. The status of the channels is
hypothetical: there is no observational basis for distinguishing them, and the scenario does not
claim to replace the standard channels of stellar collapse and mergers — it supplements them with
an interpretation of the internal structure.

**Stability threshold and horizon.** The horizon is the natural boundary of the vortex structure:
with sufficient "twist" of the field, perturbations do not escape outward. The boundary radius is
$R_s = 2GM/c^2$, where $M$ is the effective mass of the vortex and the captured matter.

**Vortex structure and asymptotics.** The profile near the center is the same ansatz as in 12.1:
$\rho_{\mathrm{RMEP}}(r) = A/[r\cdot(1 + \alpha r)^2]$. At intermediate scales the ansatz crosses
over to $\rho \propto r^{-3}$, consistent with observations in the working range of radii of
galactic rotation curves. We state precisely: as with NFW, the total mass of such a profile
diverges logarithmically — the finite mass and the Keplerian segment (6.8.8) are properties of the
galactic regulation profile, not of the ansatz. The ansatz describes only the inner region; the
matching with the galactic profile at the applicability boundary, cutting off the divergent
asymptotics, is a mandatory element of the numerical implementation (13.4). Central behavior — as
in point 5 of Section 12.1: the $r^{-1}$ divergence is integrable, regularization is a task for the
next stage.

**Empirical connection of the parameters.** The one-parameter nature of the ansatz in the form
"$\alpha$ determines $A$" is not confirmed by the recomputation (12.1.5): the relation is weak,
$R^2 = 0.23$. The $-2.26$ correlation is archived with the version; the transfer of galactic
scalings to compact objects and the justification of the $(A, \alpha)$ relation are open tasks
(13.4). The established connection of the parameters with mass — $A$ tracks $V_{\max}$ (Spearman
0.69) — is retained as a working relation.

**Mass growth and stability.** The vortex is a stable gravitational well: it captures infalling
matter while preserving its structure; adding mass increases the amplitude $A$ (mass tracking,
12.1.6), and the reconciliation of $(A, \alpha)$ is determined by the task of 13.4 under solution.

**Absence of a singularity.** The center of the vortex is the region of maximal intensity of the
regulatory field, not of infinite matter density. In the field interpretation the singularity is
not a physical result; the formal divergence of the ansatz is removed by the presumed saturation at
scales $< \alpha$.

**Consistency with observations.** The predictions testable by external dynamics are preserved:
the black hole shadow (EHT), gravitational waves (LIGO/Virgo); the power-law asymptotics in the
working range of radii is consistent with observations of galactic profiles (9.2, 9.5), while the
finiteness of the mass is a property of the galactic profile, not of the ansatz (12.2.4).

## 12.3. Overall conclusion of the section

The two formation mechanisms describe different paths to the emergence of objects with an event
horizon on a single foundation — the regulatory field of the energy of the void. Variant 1
supplements the standard picture of stellar collapse with a field treatment of the central region,
removing the singularity while preserving agreement with observations. Variant 2 treats black holes
as primary field vortices arising at critical gradients without mandatory prior collapse of matter;
its status is a hypothesis with less support than Variant 1, since the standard channels of compact
object formation are well confirmed by observations.

The unified parameterization $(A, \alpha)$ is retained as the frame of the section, but the
empirical correlation $A \propto \alpha^{-2.26}$ is not confirmed by the recomputation and has been
archived (a result of another version). The recomputation yielded two substantive consequences: the
ansatz is structurally different from the galactic profile (acceptable fits — 57 out of 171; the
dominant failure mode is an internal rise of the ansatz, a sub-Keplerian $V^2 \propto R$, which
does not match the steep rise of the actual curves of disk-dominated galaxies) — numerically
reinforcing the separation of 12.0; and the amplitude $A$ tracks the mass (Spearman 0.69), in
agreement with the canonical trend of 6.8.7. The testable content of the section — the tracking of
$A$ with mass and the numerical self-consistency of the growth in the module `rmep_blackhole.py` of
the RMEP-dark-matter repository with the updated criterion (13.4). The internal field structure of
the center of compact objects is not testable by direct observation and is, within the model, an
interpretation (0.6.6); all external observational predictions of RMEP for black holes coincide
with the standard theory (9.3).

# Section 13. Limits of Applicability and Development Plan

## 13.1. Scope of applicability

The regulatory model of the energy of the void in its current formulation is confirmed in the
following domains:

- **Galactic scales** — rotation curves of 175 galaxies of the SPARC sample (3391 data points),
comparison with NFW at an equal number of free parameters: RMEP wins in 107 cases against 31, with
37 ties; median $\chi^2_{red}$ 23.2 against 36.3 (6.8.1–6.8.3). The empirical scaling of the
regulation scale $\alpha \approx 5\ \mathrm{kpc}\cdot(V_{\max}/100\ \mathrm{km/s})^{1.89}$, bootstrap
interval of the exponent [1.34; 2.41], Spearman $\rho = 0.66$ (6.8.4); the density $\rho_0$ decreases
with system mass (6.8.7).
- **Cosmology of late epochs** — agreement with H(z), BAO, SNe Ia and CMB by construction: the
geometry is standard (9.1), the volume average of the regulation is consistent with cosmology
(7.12), the background dark energy is locked (1.1). The distinguishing cosmological tests are at
the structural level (Section 11).
- **The transition regime** — reproduction of the $a_0$ phenomenon to order of magnitude without
postulating a constant: median transition acceleration $0.14\,a_0$, spread 0.55 dex, systematic
trend with mass as a distinguishing prediction (10.2).

Outside these domains the model remains a theoretical construction: its predictions are formulated
but not quantitatively tested.

## 13.2. The early Universe (z > 2)

RMEP does not predict deviations of background cosmological quantities at early epochs from ΛCDM:
the earlier predictions "H(z) lower by 5–10%" and "an excess of massive clusters at z > 2" belonged
to the retired branch with suppressed $\Lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and have been archived (11.2, 11.5).
With the standard volume average (11.1), early structure formation is not accelerated.

The distinguishing predictions of the model for high redshifts are structural and already partially
empirically confirmed: the evolution of the regulation fraction inside the $R_e$ of disks
(~0.1–0.5 at z ~ 1–2.3 against 0.6–0.9 locally) and the evolution of the transition-acceleration
scale (doubling of $a_0$ by z ~ 1, MUSE-DARK III; 11.7). Their quantitative connection with the
scale $t_{ev} \sim 10^{10}$ years is the task of the nearest verification (7.16, 13.4).

JWST observations (bright galaxies at z > 10) point to a tension in the standard model at early
epochs. RMEP does not claim to explain these anomalies in the current version: the model contains
no declared early-Universe effects, and all statements concerning the early Universe should be read
as absences, not as predictions awaiting test. Developing this direction requires the numerical
implementation of the system 7.1 (13.4) and is not part of the evidentiary base of the present work.

## 13.3. Other limitations

- **Clusters of galaxies** — the regulation effect in rich clusters is described qualitatively; no
quantitative fit has been performed (10.3).
- **Black holes** — in the strong field the vacuum correction $\delta_{\mathrm{vac}}$ is small; the
external dynamics of black holes matches the standard theory (9.3). The model introduces no changes
to the physics of the event horizon; the internal field structure of the center (Section 12) is a
physical interpretation within the model, not an independent quantitative result; the A–$\alpha$
relation for the compact ansatz is not confirmed by the recomputation (12.1.5), while the tracking
of the amplitude with mass is established (Spearman 0.69).
- **Gravitational lensing** — the covariant form gives the standard prediction (9.1), but a
systematic comparison with lensing data has not been carried out.
- **Microscales** — beyond Section 12 the model does not touch the physics of galactic nuclei and
scales smaller than the regulation scale $\alpha$; quantitative consequences for nuclear dynamics
have not been developed (13.4).

## 13.4. Development plan

1. **Age residuals of the RAR (7.7)** — the least costly distinguishing test on existing data; the
first item of the program.
2. **Quantitative connection of the three evolutionary scales:** $a_0(z)$, the regulation fraction
inside $R_e$, and the age dependences of the residuals — with a single $t_{ev} \sim 10^{10}$ years
(7.16, 11.7).
3. **Numerical implementation of system 7.1:** structure growth, fine statistics (the halo mass
function at the low end, satellite profiles) without a claimed sign of the effect (11.5);
calculation of the analogue of the external field effect through $V_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (10.5).
4. **Internal structure of compact objects:** derivation of the correction
$\delta_{\mathrm{vac}}(R)$ from the local configuration (9.3); reconciliation of the $(A, \alpha)$
of the compact ansatz and the micromodel of the baryon-dependent potential with the criterion
$\tau_{vac} \gg t_{ev}$ and the constraint $\tau_s \lesssim 10^8$ years (7.2, 12.3); the module
`rmep_blackhole.py`.
5. **Fine closure of the pre-mass regime of the GR bridge** — the residual of the classically closed
bridge (9.1).
6. **The connection of the primordial spectrum to the global collapse (3.9)** and the quantitative
argument for 1:5.
7. **A systematic comparison with gravitational lensing data.**
8. **Testing the prediction on the ratio of the dark component to the baryonic one in old and young
regions (0.6.5)** using Euclid and LSST data.
9. **Cosmological distinguishing tests:** the low CMB multipoles via Sachs–Wolfe (11.3); the DESI
outcome on w(z) — both outcomes are distinguishing (11.4); the supernova mass step against
independent regulation proxies — the stellar population age and the RAR residuals (11.6).

# Conclusion

The paper proposes the regulatory model of the energy of the void (RMEP) — an alternative to dark
matter on a single mechanism: local configurations of the vacuum condensate with a dusty
gravitational response ($w \approx 0$, 6.8.7), whose scale and amplitude are determined by the
baryonic state of the system. The relativistic formulation (9.1) preserves standard GR: the
condensate enters the stress–energy tensor as ordinary mass, the metric is not modified — standard
gravity outside the regulation zone, standard lensing, the speed of gravitational waves equal to the
speed of light, and the GW170817 constraint is satisfied trivially. The conceptual distinction of
RMEP is the unity of the substrate: the gravitational effect of "dark matter," the cosmological
dusty component and the background dark energy are described by a single vacuum component, while
the origin of baryonic matter is a conceptual part of the work (Section 0) — whereas in ΛCDM and in
alternative approaches the dark sector is introduced as a set of additional entities.

Quantitative results have been obtained at galactic scales. On the SPARC sample (175 galaxies, 3391
data points) the RMEP profile — a condensate density with a flat core (6.8.5) — describes the
rotation curves substantially better than the standard NFW profile at an equal number of free
parameters: RMEP wins in 107 cases against 31, with 37 ties; median $\chi^2_{red}$ — 23.2 against
36.3 (6.8.1–6.8.3). The core–cusp problem is solved without additional physics — the flat core is a
property of the profile. The scaling of the regulation scale
$\alpha \approx 5\ \mathrm{kpc}\cdot(V_{\max}/100\ \mathrm{km/s})^{1.89}$ with a bootstrap interval
of the exponent [1.34; 2.41] (6.8.4) and the decrease of the central density with mass (6.8.7)
reduce two degrees of freedom per galaxy to a function of the baryonic state — a result of the
calculation, not a postulate. The fit reproduces the radial correlation of the discrepancy with
acceleration — the structural basis of the RAR (6.8.6) — and the finite total regulation mass gives
a Keplerian segment of the rotation curve in 72 of 175 objects (6.8.8) — a distinguishing
prediction against divergent halos. The universality test of the transition acceleration has been
performed by calculation: the median transition acceleration $0.14\,a_0$ — within half a dex of the
MOND-equivalent point of equal contributions — with a 0.55 dex spread and a systematic trend with
mass (Spearman 0.56): the $a_0$ phenomenon is reproduced to order of magnitude without postulating
a constant, and the predicted mass dependence of the transition is a new distinguishing prediction
absent from MOND (10.2).

In cosmology the model inherits the standard background by construction: the geometry is standard
(9.1), the volume average of the dusty component is consistent with $\Omega_{cdm}$ through the
filling fraction $f \sim 2\text{–}4\times10^{-5}$ (7.12), the background dark energy is locked (1.1)
— agreement with H(z), BAO and CMB is an obligation of the model, and the distinguishing power is
transferred to structural tests: the evolution of the regulation fraction inside the $R_e$ of disks
(~0.1–0.5 at z ~ 1–2.3 against 0.6–0.9 locally), the evolution of the transition-acceleration scale
(doubling of $a_0$ by z ~ 1, MUSE-DARK III), the mass step of SNe Ia through age and potential
depth, the low CMB multipoles through the integrated Sachs–Wolfe effect, and the DESI outcome on
w(z) with two distinguishing outcomes (Section 11). The coincidence of the three independent
evolutionary scales with a single $t_{ev} \sim 10^{10}$ years is the quantitative task of the
nearest verification (11.7, 7.16). The predictions of earlier versions — "H(z) lower by 5–10% at
z > 2," "an excess of massive clusters at z > 2," the correlation $A \propto \alpha^{-2.26}$ — are
not reproduced in the current formulation and have been archived with the reasons recorded (11.2,
11.5, 12.1.5).

In the strong gravitational field the model introduces no observable changes: the external dynamics
of black holes and the photosphere agree with the EHT and LIGO/Virgo data (9.3), and black holes in
RMEP return energy to the vacuum without altering the properties of the event horizon (0.6.6). The
internal field structure of the center of compact objects — the two formation mechanisms (Section
12) — is a physical interpretation within the model, not an independent quantitative result: direct
observation of this region is unavailable, and all external predictions coincide with the standard
theory.

The limits of the model are stated explicitly: the galactic part has been quantitatively tested;
the early Universe in the background part is consistent with ΛCDM by construction (11.1), galaxy
clusters and lensing are described qualitatively, and the internal structure of compact objects is
an interpretation (Section 13). The development plan (13.4) includes: the age residuals of the RAR
— the least costly distinguishing test on existing data; the quantitative connection of the three
evolutionary scales with $t_{ev}$; the numerical implementation of the evolution system 7.1; the
reconciliation of the compact-ansatz parameters $(A, \alpha)$ and the micromodel of the
baryon-dependent potential with the criterion $\tau_{vac} \gg t_{ev}$; the fine closure of the
pre-mass regime of the GR bridge; a systematic comparison with lensing data and a test of the
regional ratio of the dark component to the baryonic one using Euclid and LSST data. Within its
domain of applicability RMEP is consistent with the available observations and yields testable
predictions with pre-registered outcome criteria; the final judgment on the viability of the model
belongs to the tests listed.
