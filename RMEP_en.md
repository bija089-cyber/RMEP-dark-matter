# RMEP — Regulatory Model of Empty-Energy Vacuum. Version 5.0

Scientific hypothesis and its empirical test on the SPARC catalog (175 galaxies).

Anonymous work (for peer review). Canonical version, November 2026.

## Summary

The Regulatory Model of Empty-Energy Vacuum (RMEP) is proposed as an alternative to the dark-matter hypothesis, built on a single mechanism: local configurations of the vacuum condensate with a dust-like gravitational response, whose scale and amplitude are tied to the baryonic state of the system (the scale–baryon link is a working assumption, sec. 6.8.4). The relativistic formulation keeps standard GR: the condensate enters the stress–energy tensor as ordinary mass, the metric is unmodified — standard gravity outside regulation zones, standard lensing, gravitational-wave speed equal to c.

## Empirical test (SPARC)

- Full SPARC sample: 175 galaxies, 3391 data points.
- At equal numbers of free parameters, the RMEP profile (condensate density with a flat core) fits rotation curves substantially better than the standard NFW profile: RMEP wins 107 vs 31, ties 37; median χ²_red 23.2 vs 36.3.
- The core–cusp problem is resolved without extra physics — the flat core is a property of the profile.
- The scale–baryon scaling reads α ≈ 5 kpc · (V_max / 100 km/s)^1.89 with a bootstrap exponent interval [1.34; 2.41]; an independent check does not reproduce this specific form, so its empirical status remains open (sec. 6.8.4), as does the direction of the central-density–mass trend (sec. 6.8.7).
- The model reproduces the radial discrepancy–acceleration correlation (the structural basis of RAR; conditional reproduction, sec. 6.8.6) and predicts a finite total regulation mass, giving a Keplerian rise in the rotation curve for 72 of 175 objects — a discriminating prediction against divergent halos.
- Transition-acceleration universality test computed: median transition acceleration 0.14·a₀ reproduces the MOND a₀ phenomenon by order of magnitude without postulating a constant; the revealed systematic mass trend is a new discriminating prediction.

## Cosmology

The model inherits the standard cosmological background by construction (consistency with H(z), BAO and CMB); its discriminating power moves to structural tests: evolution of the regulation fraction within effective disk radii at z ~ 1–2.3, evolution of the transition-acceleration scale, a SN Ia mass step via stellar-population age and potential depth, low CMB multipoles via the integrated Sachs–Wolfe effect, and the outcome of DESI measurements of w(z) with two discriminating scenarios. In strong gravity the model introduces no observable changes: exterior black-hole dynamics and the photosphere are consistent with EHT and LIGO/Virgo data; the internal field structure of compact-object centers is treated as a physical interpretation within the model, not as an independent quantitative result.

## Boundaries and status

The galactic part is quantitatively tested; clusters and lensing are described qualitatively; predictions of earlier versions not reproduced in the current formulation are archived with recorded reasons. A program of discriminating checks with pre-registered outcome criteria is formulated — from RAR age remnants on existing data to a numerical implementation of the regulation-evolution system. Final judgment on the viability of the model rests with these checks.
