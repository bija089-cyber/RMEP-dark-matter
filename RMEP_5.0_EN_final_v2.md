Regulatory Model of Empty-Space Energy (RMEP) and Its Connection with Dark Matter and Dark Energy

A scientific hypothesis and its empirical test on the SPARC catalog (175 galaxies)

Anonymous work (for peer review)

Version 5.0. Canonical version, consistent with the per-object runs. Updated as of November 2026.

Keywords: dark matter, dark energy, vacuum energy, galaxy rotation curves, cosmological constant, NFW profile, metastable (false) vacuum, SPARC catalog

## Abstract

We propose the Regulatory Model of Empty-Space Energy (RMEP) — an alternative to the dark-matter hypothesis on a single mechanism: local configurations of a vacuum condensate with a dust-like gravitational response, whose scale and amplitude are tied to the baryonic state of the system (the scale link — a working assumption, 6.8.4). The relativistic formulation preserves standard GR: the condensate enters the energy–momentum tensor as ordinary mass, the metric is not modified — standard gravity outside the regulation zone, standard lensing, the speed of gravitational waves equal to the speed of light.

The empirical test is performed on the full sample of the SPARC catalog (175 galaxies, 3391 data points). With an equal number of free parameters, the RMEP profile — a condensate density with a flat core — describes the rotation curves substantially better than the standard NFW profile: the RMEP wins in 107 cases against 31, with 37 ties; the median χ²_red values are 23.2 against 36.3. The core–cusp problem is solved without introducing additional physics — the flat core is a property of the profile. The link between the regulation scale and the baryonic state of the system is formulated as a working assumption: the main run gives the scaling α ≈ 5 kpc·(V_max/100 km/s)^1.89 with a bootstrap interval of the exponent [1.34; 2.41]; however, an independent check does not reproduce the specific form of the dependence — its empirical status remains open (6.8.4), as does the direction of the trend of the central density with mass (6.8.7). The model reproduces the radial correlation of the discrepancy with acceleration — the structural basis of the RAR relation (the reproduction is conditional, 6.8.6) — and predicts a finite total regulation mass, which yields a Keplerian segment of the rotation curve in 72 of 175 objects (a discriminating prediction against divergent halos). The test of the universality of the transition acceleration is performed by computation: the median transition acceleration of 0.14·a₀ reproduces the a₀ phenomenon of MOND in order of magnitude without postulating a constant, and the revealed systematic trend with mass is a new discriminating prediction.

In cosmology, the model inherits the standard background by construction (agreement with H(z), BAO and the CMB), while the discriminating power is transferred to structural tests: the evolution of the fraction of regulation within the effective radii of disks at redshifts z ~ 1–2.3, the evolution of the scale of the transition acceleration, the mass step of SNe Ia through the age of the stellar population and the depth of the potential, the low CMB multipoles through the integrated Sachs–Wolfe effect, and the outcome of the DESI measurements of w(z) with two discriminating outcomes. In strong gravitational fields the model introduces no observable changes: the external dynamics of black holes and the photosphere are consistent with the EHT and LIGO/Virgo data; the internal field structure of the center of compact objects is treated as a physical interpretation within the model, not as an independent quantitative result.

The limits of the model are delineated explicitly: the galactic part is quantitatively verified; clusters and lensing are described qualitatively; the predictions of earlier versions, not reproduced in the current formulation, are transferred to the archive with a record of the reasons. A program of discriminating checks is formulated with outcome criteria fixed in advance — from the age residuals of the RAR on existing data to a numerical realization of the regulation-evolution system; the final judgment on the viability of the model belongs to the indicated checks.





Section 0 describes the physical picture (interpretation) of the model; the quantitative results are moved to Sections 6–8.

## 0.1. What empty-space energy is

Empty-space (vacuum) energy is not a "creator" of the Universe and not a source striving to generate matter or space. Its fundamental properties are the striving toward equilibrium, the observance of the law of conservation of energy, and the second law of thermodynamics.

Empty-space energy is a dynamic medium with its own thermodynamic characteristics (pressure, temperature, energy density; their role as triggers is considered in Section 2). Fluctuations occur in it, and local states can accumulate energy without passing into observable matter. The gravitational field is present in the medium as an intrinsic property of the medium itself.

**The status of the assumptions.** (1) The thesis of a medium with accumulation is an assumption of the model, not a consequence of established physics. In standard theory, the vacuum is a state of fields with minimal energy; fluctuations in it are interpreted as virtual particles. The RMEP relies on the general idea of the non-zero activity of the vacuum, but introduces its own interpretation of this activity, not reducible to standard QFT. (2) The application of the second law of thermodynamics to the vacuum medium is part of the same assumption: in standard physics, the vacuum is not described as a thermodynamic system with entropy. The RMEP attributes pressure, temperature and density to the medium, and within this frame the second law sets the direction of the processes — from less probable states toward equilibrium. It is precisely for this reason that the "striving toward equilibrium" in the model is not an intuition but a consequence of the accepted thermodynamic picture of the medium.

## 0.2. Why the fields of the Standard Model do not exist in empty-space energy

Empty-space energy is a special medium whose conditions differ from the conditions inside the domains of dark matter. Different regimes operate in it: the density, the temperature regime and the character of the curvature of spacetime are not comparable with the intra-matter ones.

**The role of the Heisenberg uncertainty principle.** In the model it is considered not as a limiter of the rate of accumulation but as a structural regulator of the states of the medium: fluctuations have no precise localization and definite energy — they exist as a set of potential states. It is precisely this uncertainty that allows the medium to accumulate energy without launching premature processes of particle formation.

**Status: an interpretation of the model.** In standard physics, the uncertainty principle is a property of quantum systems, not a separate "regulator"; such a usage is an extension of the concept and is accepted in the RMEP as a postulate.

**The difference from standard QFT — a clarification of the terminology.** In standard quantum field theory, the vacuum is also an active medium with fluctuations; the RMEP does not deny this activity. The difference lies elsewhere: in the RMEP, before the breakdown, the fields of the Standard Model do not exist in the medium — neither the gauge ones nor the fermionic ones — and, consequently, there are no localized excitations with definite quantum numbers. The fields of the Standard Model describing particles arise only together with dark matter at the moment of the breakdown, when the local conditions of the medium reach a sufficient definiteness. The quantum nature of the medium itself is thereby preserved — its regime changes, not the absence of quantum character.

**Status: the central assumption of the RMEP.** In standard physics, fields exist everywhere, independently of the presence of matter; the thesis of the birth of the fields together with dark matter contradicts this proposition and therefore must be substantiated within the model, not from standard theory.

**A thought experiment: what would happen if the fields of the Standard Model were present in the medium.** If there existed fields in empty-space energy generating localized excitations, the medium would strive toward the formation of composite structures. This leads to one of two scenarios:

- **Collapse** — a local collapse of the medium. If the fields of the Standard Model existed in the medium before the breakdown, their excitations would begin to combine into bound states: pairs, composite particles, larger structures. Each combination concentrates energy in a limited volume, and all three thermodynamic parameters of the medium grow in a correlated way: the energy density increases, the temperature rises locally, the pressure on the gravitational field grows. The medium has no discharge mechanism in this regime: it does not yet contain those channels of energy withdrawal that in a working Universe are provided by gravity and stabilized matter. The growing pressure and density amplify the local curvature of spacetime, which contracts the surrounding excitations even more strongly — the process closes on itself in an avalanche-like (runaway) manner. The result is a local collapse: the domain of the medium collapses into a state with a density, temperature and pressure incompatible with the preservation of the structure of the medium itself.
- **A runaway of growth** — a rapid growth of energy leads to an uncontrolled production of new states exceeding the capacity of the system to maintain equilibrium.

**Status: intuitive argumentation, not a computation.** Both scenarios are treated in the model as violations of the energy balance — "collapse" as a concentration of energy above a threshold without compensation, "runaway" as a runaway process. A quantitative verification of this statement (the existence of a stable metastable state as a consequence of the absence of fields) is an open task of the model; in Sections 2–3 it is not solved but used as a premise.

Therefore, the fields of the Standard Model do not exist in empty-space energy. They appear only at the moment of the breakdown — together with dark matter, when the breakdown creates local conditions with a sufficient definiteness for their emergence. Where there is dark matter, there are quantum fields — they stabilize it under the control of the gravitational field.

## 0.3. How dark matter and the quantum fields of the Standard Model appear

The appearance of dark matter, of the fields of the Standard Model, of space and, ultimately, of the observable Universe is not a planned process but a consequence of an energy breakdown of empty-space energy (the vacuum). In the terms of a metastable medium, the breakdown is described as the nucleation of a bubble of the true vacuum: a local fluctuation in which one or a group of parameters (density, temperature, pressure) exceeds the critical threshold under the conditions of gravitational pressure. There is no external action — the system itself deviates from equilibrium.

**Status: randomness with a computable probability.** The randomness of the breakdown does not mean arbitrariness: in the theory of the metastable vacuum, the decay rate per unit volume is given by the expression Γ/V = A·exp(−S_E/ħ), where S_E is the instanton action (for a thin-wall bubble S_E = 27π²σ⁴/(2ε³), where σ is the surface tension of the bubble wall, ε is the difference of the energies of the phases). The RMEP inherits this apparatus: the breakdown is a rare but regular event, whose probability is determined by the parameters of the medium. Hence the first falsifiable consequence of the model: the characteristic waiting time of the breakdown is finite and depends on the energy density of the false vacuum (the quantitative formulation is an open task; the frame is an established apparatus, Coleman 1977).

Empty-space energy is in a metastable (false) state. The false vacuum is not a separate trigger but a characteristic of the whole medium: it is precisely in this metastable state that temperature, pressure and density can fluctuate up to the threshold. The breakdown is a transition from the false vacuum into a new state, accompanied by the release of energy, which becomes dark matter.

**Status:** the metastability rests on an established apparatus (the physics of vacuum phase transitions; the false vacuum is a commonly accepted concept). The identity "the released energy = dark matter" is an assumption of the model: standard physics does not identify the products of the decay of the vacuum with dark matter.

The law of conservation of energy is not violated globally. Locally a redistribution occurs: the energy from the vacuum passes into dark matter, and gravity locks it there, so that the overall balance is preserved. The breakdown is a local deviation, not a violation of a fundamental law.

At the moment of the breakdown, the following happens:

1. **Dark matter is born** — as a by-product of the exceeding of the threshold. In the explosive regime (when all three triggers — pressure, temperature, density — fire simultaneously), dark energy does not appear: the process is too rapid for its formation. Dark energy can arise only at the stage of stabilization, when the system calms down and the processes slow down (for details — 4.4 and 4.6).

2. **The fields of the Standard Model appear together with it** — they did not exist before the breakdown but emerge as an accompanying structure when the breakdown creates local conditions with a sufficient definiteness (see 0.2).

3. **The fields of the Standard Model stabilize dark matter under the control of the gravitational field.** Why this is necessary: after the birth of dark matter, complex structures begin to form (composite states) that absorb energy, raise the temperature locally and lower the pressure. Without the fields, this process would proceed uncontrollably — the dark matter would either disperse or collapse (the scenarios of 0.2). The fields hold it back from decay, while the gravitational field sets the boundaries within which the process proceeds.

4. **The gravitational field controls the entire process** — it defines the boundaries, holds the system, and does not let the energy back into the vacuum.

**The status of items 2–4:** the functional roles of the fields are an interpretation of the model. The mechanism by which quantum fields "stabilize" dark matter is not specified in the model; in standard physics, a field does not perform the function of "holding" as an agent. This is accepted as a working frame consistent with the self-consistency of the cycle, not derived from standard theory.

**The sequence:**

Empty-space energy (only the gravitational field, a superposition) → accumulation → breakdown (the nucleation of a bubble, the exceeding of the threshold) → the birth of dark matter + the appearance of the fields of the Standard Model → the formation of complex structures (the absorption of energy, the lowering of pressure) → the fields stabilize, gravity controls → stabilization → a new cycle of accumulation.

## 0.5. Why there is one Universe

In the RMEP, the observable Universe is the only one, and the model has a reason for this: the breakdown is not reproduced.

**The formulation of the problem.** In the standard theory of the metastable vacuum, the nucleation of bubbles is possible independently at any place: if the decay probability per unit volume is non-zero, over a long time there should be many such events. The RMEP is obliged to explain why one bubble fired, not many. The answer of the model is as follows: accumulation is a global process, not a set of independent local fluctuations. The medium accumulates energy as a whole; the threshold is exceeded once, and the accumulated resource is spent in full — on the birth of dark matter. After the breakdown, the energy is locked by gravity inside the domains of dark matter and does not return to the vacuum (0.3, item 4), and the medium itself returns to an equilibrium state with a new structure. A new breakdown would require a new full cycle of accumulation up to the threshold — and there are no conditions for it in the post-breakdown medium: the resource that could fluctuate up to the critical threshold is distributed among the structures of stabilized matter.

**Status: an assumption of the model.** The thesis of the global character of accumulation and of the uniqueness of the breakdown is an interpretation of the RMEP; standard theory does not require uniqueness and admits multiple acts of nucleation. A quantitative check (to show that the post-breakdown medium cannot again reach the threshold) is an open task of the model.

A separate caveat: the statement "there are no other Universes" in the strict sense is not observationally testable — the regions beyond the horizons of events are inaccessible. Therefore the position of this subsection should be understood not as an empirical prediction but as a consequence of the model: if its mechanism is accepted, a repeated breakdown does not arise. The distinction between "uniqueness as a consequence" and "uniqueness as an observable fact" is fundamental; the work under review claims only the former.

## 0.6. Cyclicity — not "creation" but "repair"

In the regulatory model of empty-space energy (RMEP), the pulsation cycle (accumulation → breakdown → stabilization → a new cycle of accumulation) is not a mechanism of the production of matter but an attempt of the system to return to equilibrium after each breakdown. Each breakdown is a deviation from equilibrium, each stabilization is a return to the conservation of energy. Dark matter, the fields of the Standard Model and space are not the goal of the process but stable by-products that gravity localized and "canned".

**The demarcation of the two levels of the cycle — the answer to the question about the second law of thermodynamics.** At each step of the cycle the second law operates, and it operates in one direction: accumulation is a drift — a slow displacement toward the threshold under the action of the thermodynamic pressure of the medium; the breakdown is a relaxation of the accumulated excess; stabilization is a transition of the system into a locally less strained state. None of the steps reverses entropy. Yet the cycle as a whole is capable of repeating itself — and there is a reason for this: the system is not isolated from its own past. The energy locked by gravity in the matter of past cycles returns into the medium through the destruction of structures (the decay of protons, black holes — Section 4), and the limiting case of the return is the maximal breakthrough, which restarts the medium as a whole (Section 4.7). Therefore, for the medium as a whole the cycle is not a "heat death" but a closed circulation: locally entropy grows at each step, globally — energy is redistributed between the medium and the conserved matter without complete equalization.

**Status: an interpretation of the model.** The application of the second law to the local steps of the cycle agrees with the general frame of 0.1; however, the thesis of the global circulation (the return of energy through the decay of structures and the restart of the medium) is an assumption of the RMEP; in standard physics, the cosmological evolution is not described as a closed cycle. A quantitative check is an open task.

The formula "repair, not creation" sets a criterion for the entire further structure of the model: if any element of reality looks like "created with a purpose", the RMEP is obliged to show it as a by-product of the regulatory process. Dark matter, the fields and space satisfy this criterion — each of them arises as a consequence of the return to equilibrium, not as its goal.

## 0.6.1. The life cycle of protons

Protons did not appear all at once — their formation proceeded at an increasing rate. Protons are not eternal objects but temporal structures formed in the process of the accumulation of quarks (the terminology of the model: by "accumulation" is meant the combination of fundamental components into bound states, see 0.2). They are stable under ordinary conditions but not eternal. The partial destruction of protons proceeds constantly along three channels:

- **Destruction in interactions** — in the nuclei of active galaxies, in collisions of cosmic rays, in the interiors of neutron stars. This is the only channel partially supported by standard physics: hard interactions destroy nuclei and change the composition of matter, although the proton as a particle is preserved in them.
- **Internal instability** — protons are unstable "by construction": they are formed of quarks, which are themselves a product of the cycle, and are not obliged to exist eternally.
- **Black holes** — extreme tidal forces destroy matter down to elementary components; the released energy returns into empty-space energy. It is precisely this channel that closes the global circulation on which 0.6 rests.

**Status and connection with observations.** The dependence on the channels: channel 1 is consistent with observed physics; channels 2–3 are assumptions of the model. At the same time, the thesis of a finite lifetime of the free proton is a falsifiable prediction: the proton-decay search experiments (Super-Kamiokande and analogs) have already established a lower bound on the lifetime exceeding 10³⁴ years, without detecting a decay. The RMEP, asserting the instability of protons "by construction", is obliged either to reconcile its lifetime with these bounds or to indicate a decay channel not covered by the experimental searches. The quantitative formulation is in Sections 6–8; the qualitative position here is as follows: the existing constraints do not refute the model but severely narrow the admissible parameters of its channel 2.

**The role in the cycle.** The totality of the channels ensures a continuous return of matter into the medium: breakdowns and interactions destroy structures, black holes disintegrate them into components, and the energy flows back into empty-space energy — this is the very mechanism of return that makes the cycle of 0.6 closed while preserving the second law of thermodynamics.

## 0.6.2. The life cycle of dark matter

Dark matter has its own "timer" — a life cycle distinct from the baryonic one. Dark matter does not sustain itself infinitely: its existence depends on the inflow of energy from empty-space energy. The feeding is not a free access to the source: gravity has cut dark matter off from the unlimited drain (0.3–0.4), and the inflow proceeds only in a dosed form, under the control of the gravitational field. While the feeding arrives, dark matter develops and becomes more complex. When the flow weakens or ceases, the decline of dark matter begins: the structures cease to be maintained and gradually lose organization, and the energy contained in them is subject to return into the medium (the mechanisms of return — Section 4, see also 0.6.1 for the baryonic channel).

**Status: an assumption of the model.** The dependence of dark matter on the feeding and its decline have no analog in standard cosmology, where dark matter is considered as a conserved component not requiring "nutrition". The RMEP accepts this thesis as a condition of the closedness of the energy cycle: without the feeding and the decline, the return of energy into the medium would be impossible, and the cycle of 0.6 would not close.

## 0.6.3. The interaction of the two cycles

| Phase | Protons | Dark Matter | What is observed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early cycle | Form at an increasing rate | Receives feeding from the vacuum | Baryonic matter dominates |
| Mature cycle | Partial destruction, energy → vacuum | Stable, feeding still arrives | Equilibrium of both components |
| Late cycle | Destruction exceeds reproduction | Feeding decreases, decline | Dark matter dominates |
| Cycle finale | Destruction of the overwhelming part of protons | Fully depleted, decay | Maximal breakthrough |

**The key conclusion of the table.** The baryonic cycle is consumed faster, while dark matter is depleted more slowly. Therefore, in the late phases of the cycle, dark matter dominates — not because it grows, but because it fades more slowly. This ratio agrees with the observed composition of matter in the Universe: dark matter accounts for about 85% of all matter. The RMEP interprets this not as a coincidence but as the natural state of the late phase of the cycle.

**Status: a qualitative correspondence, not a quantitative derivation.** The model explains the sign of the ratio (there is more dark matter) but does not derive the number 85% from its parameters — the quantitative agreement with the measurements of the cosmological parameters (the ΛCDM flatness) is an open task for Sections 6–8. The asymmetry of the decay rates of the two cycles is an assumption of the model that requires a stated reason (see below).

**The reason for the asymmetry.** In the RMEP, it follows from the difference in the structures of the cycles (0.6.1–0.6.2): baryonic matter is destroyed by active channels (interactions, black holes) from the moment of its formation, whereas dark matter fades only when the feeding weakens — it has no analog of “destruction in interactions”. Dark matter is consumed more slowly not because it is intrinsically stronger, but because its cycle contains fewer consumption channels.

## 0.6.4. The maximal breakthrough

When the feeding from the vacuum ceases, dark matter gradually fades (0.6.2), the fields of the Standard Model lose stability and disappear together with the structure that generated them. In the final stage, the massive decay of protons (0.6.1) completes the baryonic cycle. Space ceases to exist as a stable geometry — it "dissolves" together with the vanishing matter.

The vacuum receives a powerful energy impulse from the decay of all the remaining matter — the maximal breakthrough occurs: a large-scale "boiling" of empty-space energy (the term of the model: an avalanche-like growth of fluctuations after the return of all the conserved energy into the medium), the birth of a new space and the launch of a new cycle. In its parameters, the maximal breakthrough reproduces the conditions of the original breakdown of 0.3 — but this time its resource is not an accumulation "from scratch" but the return of everything that was locked in the matter of all the preceding phases.

**The resolution of the apparent contradiction with 0.5.** In 0.5 it was established that a repeated breakdown after a single one is impossible: the resource capable of fluctuating up to the threshold is distributed among the structures of matter. The maximal breakthrough removes precisely this condition: by the end of the cycle all the conserved energy has returned into the medium (0.6.1, 0.6.2), there is no distributed structure any more — and the condition of 0.5 removes itself. The cycle is thus self-completing: the breakdown does not repeat while matter exists, but becomes possible again after its complete return. The caveat of 0.5 on testability retains its force: the uniqueness of the Universe remains a consequence of the model for all phases except the transition between cycles.

**Status: an assumption of the model.** The dissolution of space, the disappearance of the fields together with matter and the restart of the medium have no analog in standard physics and are accepted as a condition of the closedness of the cycle. A quantitative check is an open task of Sections 6–8; the qualitative link with 4.7 — there the maximal breakthrough is considered as the limiting case of the return of energy.

## 0.6.5. A testable prediction

If dark matter fades more slowly than the baryonic one (0.6.3), then the ratio of dark matter to baryonic must grow with the time of the cycle: in regions at a later phase, the fraction of dark matter is higher.

**The operational formulation.** An "old region" in the model is a region evolved further along the cycle, not an object seen through a longer time span. The distinction is fundamental: an object with a high redshift is seen by us as it was at an early phase (the light traveled for billions of years) — this is a young system at the moment of observation. An old region is the evolved systems of the near Universe of the same class and mass. The correct formulation of the test: at a fixed mass and type of object, the ratio of dark matter to baryonic must be higher in the systems advanced further along the cycle (a low redshift) than in their early analogs (a high redshift). The test on "ancient clusters" in the original formulation mixed these two scales — corrected.

**The observational status.** The picture of the data is inhomogeneous, and the model is obliged to show this:

- **In favor:** the fraction of dark matter within the effective radius of star-forming disk galaxies decreases with increasing redshift — that is, the evolved (near) systems are richer in dark matter than their early analogs at the same mass. This qualitatively matches the prediction of the RMEP.
- **Against (tension 1):** in ΛCDM the global ratio of baryons to dark matter is fixed already by the CMB and the baryon acoustic oscillations — about 1:5 since the epoch of recombination without a noticeable change. If the fraction of dark matter systematically grew with the time of the cycle, the global ratio would have to evolve. The model is obliged to localize the effect: the prediction refers to the fractions in bound systems, not to the cosmological mean — but then an argument is needed for why the redistribution within the systems does not change the global ratio (an open task of Sections 6–8).
- **Against (tension 2):** the dependence is non-monotonic in mass. The JWST observations show that low-mass galaxies at z ~ 6–7 are already dark-matter-dominated within ~1 kpc, while for clusters the evolution of the baryonic fraction with redshift is weak or close to zero. Hence, the observed trend in disks may reflect baryonic physics (the growth of the stellar mass, the flow of gas) rather than the decline of the components — and the model cannot yet separate its contribution from this background.

**The criterion of refutation.** The prediction is refuted if, under the control of mass, type and the method of estimating the dark-matter fraction, it turns out that the evolved systems are systematically poorer in dark matter than the early analogs of the same class — and this is not removed by corrections for baryonic physics. A single persistent counterexample on reliable data suffices.

**Status: a falsifiable prediction.** It is formulated operationally, the direction of the effect is set, the criterion of refutation is stated. The current status is a partial consistency with the data on disk galaxies under two recorded tensions (the global 1:5 ratio since the epoch of recombination; the non-monotonicity in mass). The prediction is not refuted, but not unambiguously confirmed either; the quantitative reconciliation is an open task of Sections 6–8.

## 0.6.6. Black holes as a mechanism of energy return

A black hole is a domain of an extreme gravitational field in which tidal forces destroy the infalling matter down to elementary components. The return of energy into empty-space energy proceeds along two channels of different significance:

- **Channel A — radiation before crossing the horizon.** The main part of the energy of the infalling matter is released before the matter crosses the event horizon: the accretion disk is heated to enormous temperatures, and the energy escapes into radiation and jets. The observations of quasars and active galactic nuclei are a direct testimony of such a return: accretion can release up to tens of percent of the rest energy of the infalling matter, which is orders of magnitude more efficient than thermonuclear fusion. This channel is observably real and supports the thesis of a partial return.
- **Channel B — the return from behind the horizon.** The energy that has crossed the horizon is inaccessible from the point of view of an external observer; the only mechanism of its return admitted by standard physics is Hawking radiation, which for astrophysical black holes of stellar masses is so slow that the evaporation times exceed the current age of the Universe by tens of orders of magnitude. The RMEP accepts this channel as complete but extremely stretched in time: the final return of the conserved energy proceeds at scales much larger than the lifetimes of stars and galaxies.

**Status:** channel A is consistent with the observations and standard physics; channel B is an assumption of the model. The thesis of the complete return of the energy from behind the horizon has no confirmation: Hawking radiation has not been observed, and the information paradox remains unsolved. The RMEP is obliged to admit that the closedness of the cycle rests on an undiscovered mechanism, and its empirical check is an open task (including the fundamental question: is the information preserved upon the return). The metaphor of the "whirlwind"/"vortex" is figurative, not physical: the gravitational field of a black hole does not vorticate in the mechanical sense — the tidal stretching is described by the standard Schwarzschild/Kerr geometry.

**The role in the cycle.** The totality of the channels ensures a staged return: the disk gives away the energy fast and observably, the horizon conserves the remainder at super-long times. For the global circulation of 0.6 this means: the return proceeds continuously but asymmetrically — the larger part is conserved, and precisely this makes the maximal breakthrough (0.6.4) a rare event rather than a constant process.

# Section 1. The concept of empty-space energy

## 1.1. The excitation of empty-space energy

Empty-space energy is not a static background: processes of the birth and annihilation of particles proceed in it continuously. In the regulatory model of empty-space energy (RMEP), the medium has two regimes of excitation:

- **Partial excitation** — a local fluctuation with a small growth of the number of elementary particles. The energy remains within the system: the medium absorbs the perturbation and returns to equilibrium without an ejection outward. Functionally this is the accumulation channel: each such fluctuation leaves in the medium a contribution to the total pressure (0.3).
- **Strong excitation** — an exceeding of the local threshold at which a part of the energy goes beyond the vacuum and passes into dark energy. This is not a full-fledged breakdown but a "leakage" through the boundary. Functionally this is the safety channel: it discharges the excess pressure in places, without bringing the medium to the global threshold.

**Accumulation as a growth of potential states.** Accumulation proceeds not as "particles being piled up" but as a growth of the density of potential states — configurations of the medium which after the breakdown (0.3) are realized as real particles and fields. Coexisting in the medium are: virtual particles, constantly born and annihilating — this regime is confirmed by standard physics (the Casimir effect, the Lamb shift); exotic particles — yet undiscovered excitations of the medium; candidates for accumulation — electrons, photons, the progenitors of quarks, axions, majorons. The latter two are hypothetical particles: axions remain one of the main candidates for dark matter in standard physics as well, while majorons appear in the models of neutrino mass.

**Dark energy: the first definition in the document.** In the RMEP, dark energy is a uniformly distributed form of empty-space energy that has preserved a direct connection with the medium. Unlike dark matter, which gravity cut off from unlimited access to the source (0.4), dark energy accepts the leakages directly. In standard cosmology, dark energy is a component responsible for the accelerated expansion of the Universe, accounting for about 68% of its energy composition; the commonly accepted model (ΛCDM) assumes its density to be constant.

**The link with 0.6.2: the feeding mechanism.** The leakages of 1.1 give the RMEP a mechanism it did not previously have: the leakages feed dark energy, and it redistributes what it receives as a dosed feeding of dark matter. Thus the gap left in 0.6.2, where the feeding was introduced without indicating the path, is closed. The chain: partial excitation → accumulation; strong excitation → leakages → dark energy → the feeding of dark matter.

**The observational context.** The data of the DESI instrument for 2024–2025 gave hints that dark energy changes in time: its influence, judging by the joint analysis with the relic radiation, supernovae and weak lensing, weakens with time — a significance up to 4.2σ depending on the data combination, which is below the discovery threshold of 5σ. For the RMEP this qualitatively agrees with 0.6.3: the feeding in the late phases of the cycle decreases — and, if dark energy is fed by the leakages, its density in the late phase must decrease, that is, the weakening effect is the expected direction for a mature cycle. At the same time, the picture of the data is inhomogeneous: the 2026 analysis of the Lyman-alpha forest has shifted a part of the evidence back toward ΛCDM, so the status is "a qualitative coincidence of direction", not a confirmation.

**The criterion of refutation.** If the final data of DESI, Euclid and the Roman telescope establish a strict constancy of the density of dark energy, the leakage channel will lose observational support: the model will have to either revise the role of dark energy or admit the channel empirically empty. The prediction that arises here: the RMEP expects from dark energy an evolution in time, not a constancy.

**Status: mixed.** Virtual particles — consistent with standard physics. Partial excitation as the accumulation channel, the leakages, the connection of dark energy with the medium, and accumulation as potential states — assumptions and interpretations of the RMEP. A quantitative check (the equation of state of dark energy in the terms of the model, the rate of the leakages) is an open task of Sections 6–8.

## 1.2. The connection with quantum field theory

The standard formulation of quantum field theory (QFT) describes particles as excitations of the corresponding fields, and the vacuum — as the state of the fields with the lowest energy. Within the regulatory model of empty-space energy (RMEP), this picture is true after the breakdown (0.3), when the fields of the Standard Model are already separated out and stabilized by gravity. Before the breakdown, particles act as potential states of energy (1.1) — excitations of the medium not yet formed into fields.

**The ontological relation.** QFT and the RMEP do not compete: QFT describes the behavior of the fields after their emergence, the RMEP — the origin of the fields themselves and the medium preceding them. The divergence is only in the question of the status of the vacuum: for QFT the vacuum is the lowest state of the fields, for the RMEP — the substrate from which the fields are born and into which they return. QFT remains an exact effective theory within the cycle; the RMEP claims a description of its edges — the beginning (the breakdown) and the completion (the maximal breakthrough, 0.6.4).

**The caveat on the language.** The formulation "in a superposition" from the former version of the section has been removed deliberately: superposition is a concept of the quantum formalism, which in the model itself appears together with the fields after the breakdown. To speak of a superposition of states before the emergence of the fields means to apply an instrument beyond its domain of definition; this is the same error of scale that the model controls in 0.1 (the control of levels). The correct term is "potential states" in the sense of 1.1.

**Status: an interpretative extension, not a negation.** All the working predictions of QFT (the Lamb shifts, renormalizations, the structures of the interactions) the RMEP inherits fully: within the cycle the model offers nothing that would contradict QFT. The new content of the RMEP in this section is ontological, not computational: the medium is primary, the fields are its stable excitations. Checkable consequences from this statement are not generated by section 1.2 itself; its role is to fix the place of QFT in the structure of the model and to remove the potential objection "the theory contradicts quantum field theory".

## 1.3. The difference from the standard picture (ΛCDM)

ΛCDM (the standard cosmological model with dark energy and cold dark matter) is built on general relativity: gravity responds to an already existing mass and energy — matter curves spacetime, and the gravitational field is a consequence of the distribution of the sources. Gravity in this picture is passive: without a source it does not exist.

In the regulatory model of empty-space energy (RMEP), the gravitational field is primary and actively governs the medium before the appearance of mass:

- **Holding in the metastable state.** Gravity holds the vacuum in a metastable state (the false vacuum — a state locally stable but not absolutely stable). The concept of the false vacuum is itself standard: the metastability of the electroweak vacuum is discussed in standard physics on the basis of the measured mass of the Higgs boson. What is new in the RMEP is that gravity is considered as a factor maintaining the metastability, not simply as a field generated by sources.
- **Bringing to the threshold.** Accumulation (0.3, 1.1) proceeds under the control of gravity: it doses the leakages and does not let the medium discharge the excess prematurely — this is its regulatory function.
- **The separation at the breakdown.** At the moment of the breakdown (0.3), gravity cuts the nascent dark matter off from unlimited access to the source (0.4, with the account of the synchronous edit on the dosed feeding), controlling the energy balance between the medium and the conserved matter.

**In total:** gravity is not a passive response to mass but an active regulator of the cycle, governing the excitation of empty-space energy even before mass appears.

**The tension with general relativity — an honest fixation.** In GR, the gravitational field is determined by the distribution of energy–momentum: the theory knows of no source without mass or energy. The thesis of the RMEP on "gravity before mass" is not formulated in these terms without additional assumptions — for example, that the gravitational field exists as an independent geometric structure of the medium, not requiring a source in the pre-breakdown phase. The RMEP accepts this as the core of its ontology: gravity is a property of the medium itself, not a response to its content. The model inherits GR over the whole intra-cyclic range: where mass exists, the predictions coincide with the verified ones.

**Status: the core of the model, an assumption.** Active gravity before the appearance of mass is the central assumption of the RMEP, having no analog in standard physics; it is not refuted by the observations (there is nothing to observe the pre-breakdown phase with), but it is not supported by them either. The only indirect contact is the very fact of the existence of the Universe: standard cosmology starts from an already given hot initial state and does not answer where it came from — the RMEP occupies precisely this niche. The quantitative formulation (which field describes the pre-breakdown gravity, how it passes into GR at the birth of mass) is the key open task of Sections 2 and 6–8.

# Section 2. The mechanism of the regulator

The regulator is the gravitational field governing the cycle of transformations of empty-space energy (the ontological status — 1.3: gravity as a property of the medium itself, not a response to its content). The regulatory cycle consists of four phases:

1. **Accumulation** (2.1) — the growth of the density of potential states under the holding of metastability.
2. **The breakdown** (2.2–2.3) — the birth of dark matter; at a global breakdown — also of the fields of the Standard Model.
3. **Stabilization** (2.4) — the relaxation of the medium, the formation of quarks and baryonic matter.
4. **The return of energy** (2.5) — the staged return of the conserved energy into the medium (0.6.1, 0.6.6), completed by the maximal breakthrough (0.6.4).

The evolution of matter (0.4–0.6) unfolds within phases 3–4.

## 2.1. The accumulation phase

- In the medium, the birth and annihilation of virtual particles proceed continuously — a regime confirmed by standard physics and inherited by the RMEP (1.1, 1.2).
- Accumulation proceeds as a growth of the density of potential states (1.1) — configurations which after the breakdown are realized as real particles and fields.
- The growth of density and energy loads the medium itself: its geometric state ("curvature" as a state of the gravitational structure, 1.3) changes, the excitation becomes denser, the load on the gravitational field grows. "Pressure" and "effective temperature" here are characteristics of the intensity of the excitation, not thermodynamic quantities: before the appearance of particles there is no temperature in the strict sense.
- As long as the load has not reached the critical threshold, accumulation proceeds. The regulator is active: it doses the leakages through strong excitation (1.1) and holds the metastability (1.3) — the growth of pressure is consistent with the regime of regulation, not contradictory to it.

**Status: mixed.** Virtual particles — standard physics; the growth of the density of potential states — an interpretation of the RMEP; "curvature", "pressure", "effective temperature" in the pre-breakdown phase — working quantities of the model, their strict definitions and the transition to ordinary thermodynamics after the breakdown — an open task of Sections 6–8. The mechanism of the holding of the metastability by gravity is an additional open task.

## 2.2. The condition of the breakdown

The threshold of the breakdown is set by a group of parameters of the medium (2.1): pressure, "effective temperature" and energy density. The event is initiated by whichever parameter reaches the threshold first; the remaining parameters determine not "yes/no" but the character of the event. It is fundamentally necessary to demarcate two regimes:

- **The global breakdown** (0.3) — a one-time event of the cycle: dark matter and the fields of the Standard Model are born. The thesis of 0.5 on the impossibility of a repeated breakdown refers precisely to it.
- **A local discharge** — an exceeding of the local threshold in an individual domain of the medium: only dark matter is born. The fields exist by this moment from the time of the global breakdown, so their repeated birth is not required. Local discharges are multiple and continue throughout the cycle.

Thus a ladder of thresholds is built up: partial excitation (accumulation) → strong excitation (a leakage into dark energy, 1.1) → the local threshold (a discharge: the birth of dark matter) → the global threshold (the breakdown). Each level is its own form of the excitation of the medium, and none cancels the previous one.

**The character of the event depending on which parameter fired first:**

| What fired first | Where it is probable | Character of the event |
|---|---|---|
| Pressure | Deep gravitational wells (the centers of massive galaxies, clusters) | A massive discharge: much dark matter, a steep distribution close to the NFW profile |
| "Effective temperature" | Domains of intense particle birth (active galactic nuclei, star-forming regions) | A "hot" discharge: dark matter is born with a high intensity of excitation, relaxes longer |
| Energy density | Domains of slow prolonged accumulation (the periphery of halos, the boundaries of voids) | A "cold" moderate discharge: less dark matter, a more sparse distribution |

**The comparison with standard physics.** The Navarro–Frenk–White profile is a standard result of the simulations of cold dark matter: a steep (cusp-like) distribution of the density in the center of the halo; for massive galactic and cluster halos it is confirmed by observations (the Milky Way, M 31, clusters). In the RMEP this agrees with the first row of the table: a massive discharge under pressure in a deep well gives a compressed, steep distribution. A side benefit of the model: "cold" small discharges give more sparse, non-steep profiles — which qualitatively resonates with the cusp problem: observations of dwarf galaxies consistently show a "core" of nearly constant density instead of the predicted NFW cusp. The RMEP does not solve this problem here, but its spectrum of profiles (steep for massive, shallow for small ones) is directed in the same way.

**The caveat on the analogy.** The "hot" and "cold" discharge in the model is the state of dark matter at birth and the rate of its relaxation, not the same thing as hot and cold dark matter in standard cosmology (where the classification is set by the velocities of the particles in the early Universe, and hot candidates are rigidly constrained by observations — less than 1% of the composition). These notions must not be mixed; the correspondence is only an analogy of the type "a compressed/smeared distribution".

## 2.3. The breakdown phase

- Upon reaching the threshold, the breakdown occurs: a global one — the birth of dark matter and the fields; a local one — the discharge of dark matter (2.2).
- A part of the empty-space energy passes into the birth zone.
- The fields of the Standard Model appear at the global breakdown (1.2) and stabilize the newborn dark matter; at a local discharge, the stabilization is provided by the already existing fields.
- The gravitational field closes the direct channel of access to the source: it cuts dark matter off from unlimited access to empty-space energy, admitting a dosed feeding (0.4, 0.6.2).
- Dark matter retains the dosed feeding and relies on the accumulated resource.

## 2.4. The stabilization phase

- The excitation of the medium decays: the "effective temperature", pressure and energy density return to equilibrium values.
- The medium returns to equilibrium — accumulation continues already in the post-breakdown medium.
- Dark matter relaxes: it loses the excess excitation ("hot" discharges — longer).
- The formation of quarks within dark matter proceeds (0.6.1) — on the fields existing from the moment of the global breakdown; further — baryonic matter and protons.

## 2.5. The phase of the return of energy

- A part of the protons is destroyed (interactions, internal instability, black holes) — 0.6.1.
- The liberated energy returns into the medium — along the channels of 0.6.6: the radiation of accretion disks (channel A) and the super-long return from behind the horizon (channel B). The return into the geometry of the medium (1.3) is a working image; its strict mechanism is an open task of Sections 6–8.
- Black holes conserve a part of the energy at super-long times — this sets the staged character of the return and the rarity of the maximal breakthrough (0.6.6).
- The returned energy feeds the continuation of the cycle — accumulation and local discharges.

## 2.6. The full contour

- **The local contour** (repeated during the cycle): accumulation → local discharge → stabilization → accumulation.
- **The global cycle** (one-time): accumulation → global breakdown → stabilization → evolution and the return of energy (0.4–0.6) → the maximal breakthrough (0.6.4) → a new global cycle.

**The status of Sections 2.2–2.5: mixed.** The ladder of thresholds and the regimes of the breakdown are a construction of the RMEP; the correspondence of the profiles to the observed ones (NFW in massive halos, smeared cores in dwarfs) is qualitative, not quantitative: the model does not yet derive the parameters of the discharges. The multiplicity of local discharges is an assumption of the model; for 0.5 a synchronous caveat is required: the proposition on the impossibility of a repeated breakdown refers to the global breakdown, local discharges are a separate multiple regime, and the conditions of the removal of one-timeness are set by the maximal breakthrough (0.6.4). An observational consequence: the fraction of dark matter in a system grows with each local discharge — this refines the picture of 0.6.3 (the domination in the late phase is set primarily by the asymmetry of the rates; local discharges give an additional contribution), without contradicting it.

## 2.7. The key property of dark matter

Dark matter has no own channel of return into empty-space energy. This is a consequence of the structure of the barrier placed by the regulator at the breakdown (0.4, 2.3):

- **The barrier governs the flow in both directions — and doses both.** Gravity cut dark matter off from unlimited access to the source but preserved the dosed feeding (0.6.2). Hence, the channel between dark matter and the medium is not sealed shut but placed under the control of the regulator: the input (the feeding) is dosed, and the output (the return) is as well. The "cutting off" in the RMEP is not a wall but a transfer of the channel under governance.
- **Baryonic matter has return channels; dark matter does not.** Baryonic matter returns energy actively: through the destruction of protons and black holes (0.6.1, 0.6.6). Dark matter does not interact and has no active channels — its return is possible only passively, as it declines with the weakening of the feeding (0.6.2). It is precisely for this reason that its consumption is stretched over the whole cycle (0.6.3): not because it is stronger by nature, but because its cycle contains fewer consumption channels.
- **Not a deficit of energy but the structure of the channel.** The formulation "dark matter lacks sufficient energy to overcome the barrier" is imprecise: it implies that upon the accumulation of sufficient energy the return would become possible — and this would make the property quantitative and destructible. In the RMEP, the return is blocked not by a deficit of energy but by the structure of the channel: the regulator does not let dark matter back except dosed and as it declines. While the feeding arrives, dark matter does not decline and, consequently, does not return — it conserves the energy.

**The consequence for the cycle:** dark matter is a conservator of energy. While the cycle proceeds, the baryonic component actively pumps the energy back into the medium, while dark matter stores it and gives it away only at the very end (0.6.3, the final phase: the feeding reduced to zero — the decline completes, all the conserved energy is returned). This refines the mechanics of 0.6.4: the resource of the maximal breakthrough is precisely that dark matter could not return earlier — its energy is available to the medium only at the finish, when the feeding is exhausted. The property "does not return" and the property "provides the final impulse" are one and the same construction, not two separate assumptions.

**The reconciliation with the accepted text.** There is no contradiction with 0.6.2: there the return of energy by dark matter is described as a decline — a passive return as the feeding weakens, which is what is recorded here. With 0.6.4 it is agreed: "fully depleted, decays" — the moment when the passive return is complete; the full depletion of dark matter at the finale receives a qualitative justification (the mechanism — the decline), the quantitative side remains for Sections 6–8.

**Status: an assumption of the model.** The one-way-dosed character of the channel and the absence of active return channels in dark matter is a construction of the RMEP without an analog in standard physics. This block has no observational contact and must not claim one: its role is the internal mechanics of the cycle, explaining simultaneously the asymmetry of the rates (0.6.3) and the source of the final impulse (0.6.4).

# Section 3. The pulsation cycle

In the regulatory model, the system does not evolve monotonically but pulsates: local discharges (2.2) alternate with phases of accumulation and stabilization, creating a wave-like dynamics. The term pulsation here means the same as a full pass of the local contour (2.6): accumulation → local discharge → stabilization. Pulsations manifest themselves as local surges of the density and excitation of the medium, which together form the observed structure.

## 3.1. The nature of pulsations

Pulsations are not external impacts but internal oscillations of the system: different domains of the medium reach the local threshold (2.2) at different times. In some places the threshold by pressure fires first, in others — by density; therefore the pulsations proceed as "waves", not simultaneously over the whole volume.

Empty-space energy lives and pulsates on its own — this is a basic property of the medium (1.3), not requiring an external source. The pulsations are fed by two sources:

1. **The internal one** — the own dynamics of the medium, self-sufficient and independent.
2. **The return one** — the energy returned into the medium through the destruction of protons and black holes (0.6.1, 0.6.6, 2.5); it is not external with respect to the system (all the energy circulates within the cycle), but external with respect to the medium: it enters it from outside its equilibrium dynamics. The return energy amplifies the pulsations and accelerates the reaching of the thresholds in the domains of slow accumulation.

## 3.2. Local and global pulsations

**Local pulsations** are local discharges in individual domains (2.2), forming the structure of dark matter. This is a current, continuously proceeding process: one domain has discharged, its neighbors are accumulating, then the next one discharges. A local pulsation includes all four phases of the local contour (2.6).

**The global pulsation** is the maximal breakthrough (0.6.4), zeroing the entire cycle. This is an explosive regime in which all three parameters (2.2) reach the global threshold simultaneously. The process proceeds rapidly: dark matter is formed sharply, the gravitational field and the fields of the Standard Model operate in the limiting regime. In this case, the locked reserve of dark energy returns into the medium together with its dissolution (0.6.4, 3.5, see the statuses) — a new cycle begins from a clean state of the medium.

## 3.3. The mechanism of damping and renewed growth

After a local discharge, the domain loses the excess energy and passes into the stabilization phase (2.4) — the pulsation in this place temporarily "weakens", but the neighboring domains continue to accumulate. As a result, the pulsation shifts, creating the effect of a wave. At this moment the return energy joins the cycle: it feeds the medium in the domains of slow accumulation and accelerates the reaching of the threshold.

## 3.4. The amplitude and frequency of pulsations

The amplitude of a pulsation inherits the character of the discharge (2.2, the table): by pressure — a strong pulsation (much dark matter, a steep profile); by "effective temperature" — a medium one (a "hot" discharge, relaxing longer); by energy density — a weak one (a small discharge, a sparse distribution). The frequency of the pulsations is set by the time of the accumulation of a domain up to the local threshold and is accelerated by the return energy (3.3).

## 3.5. Dark energy as a locked component

The channel between the medium and dark energy is one-directional: empty-space energy → dark energy, but not back. Dark energy accepts the leakages directly (1.1) — its connection with the medium is direct on the input; on the output there is no channel: having separated from the equilibrium dynamics of the medium, this reserve has no path of return into it. Thus a hierarchy of channels is built up: the medium exchanges energy in both directions freely; dark energy — only accepts; dark matter — accepts in a dosed way, returns only passively, as it declines (2.7).

This is fundamentally important for the dynamics: the locked dark energy preserves a reserve that continues to influence the gravitational field and, consequently, the thresholds of the discharge — therefore the pulsations do not damp to zero during the cycle.

**The fate of the locked reserve at the finale of the cycle — a solution of the model.** The one-directionality of the channel operates within the cycle. At the maximal breakthrough (0.6.4), the medium dissolves together with the structures that cut it off — and the locked reserve of dark energy returns into the single flux together with all the conserved energy. Without this assumption the cycle is not closed: the return of "all the remaining matter" (0.6.4) would not cover dark energy. The property "does not return" in 3.5 holds for all the phases of the cycle except the transition between cycles — by the same scheme by which the one-timeness of the breakdown in 0.5 is removed by the maximal breakthrough.

## 3.6. The connection with the life cycles

The pulsations are connected with two parallel life cycles. **The proton cycle:** the destruction of protons releases energy that returns into the medium and feeds the pulsations (0.6.1, 2.5). **The cycle of dark matter:** while the dosed feeding arrives (0.6.2), dark matter develops and deepens the gravitational wells, accelerating the accumulation. The two cycles form a feedback that regulates the frequency and amplitude of the pulsations: more return — more frequent discharges; more dark matter — larger discharges in the deep wells.

## 3.7. The mathematical interpretation

Let rho_pot(t) be the density of the potential states in the medium, P — the pressure, T — the "effective temperature" (2.1). A local discharge occurs when at least one of the parameters reaches a critical value:

rho_pot ≥ rho_crit   or   P ≥ P_crit   or   T ≥ T_crit

For the wave-like dynamics of the pulsations, a phenomenological wave equation with damping and a source is introduced:

∂²ψ/∂t² = c_vac²·∇²ψ − γ·∂ψ/∂t + S_ret(t)

where c_vac is the effective velocity of the propagation of the pulsation in the medium, γ is the damping coefficient, S_ret(t) is the source of the return energy (the inflow from the destruction of protons and black holes, 3.1). The period of a local pulsation:

τ_pulse ~ L / c_vac

where L is the characteristic size of the domain of accumulation. The velocity c_vac is a free parameter of the model: the excitation propagates in the medium, not as a signal in a ready spacetime, and is not obliged to coincide with the speed of light. For the halos of a typical galaxy (L ~ 10–50 kpc) at c_vac ~ 0.01–0.1c the period amounts to ~3×10⁵–2×10⁷ years; the value is bounded from below by the observational data and remains an open parameter of Sections 6–8.

## 3.8. The connection with observed phenomena

The pulsation mechanism gives qualitative correspondences to several observed effects. The status of all three is an interpretation: standard physics explains these phenomena by its own means (the plasma dynamics of clusters, the gravitational evolution of voids), the RMEP offers an alternative treatment of the mechanism, not an explanation of the unexplained.

- **The temperature gradients in clusters of galaxies** — qualitatively consistent with the "hot" discharges (3.4): in standard physics, the temperature profiles of the hot cluster plasma are formed by its dynamics; the RMEP interprets them as traces of discharges by "effective temperature".
- **The shells and walls at the boundaries of voids** — qualitatively consistent with the wave of discharges: in standard physics, the boundaries of voids are regions of elevated density between the voids and the filaments; the RMEP sees in them traces of successive pulsations.
- **The local density peaks** — traces of past discharges; a direct analogy with the "cold" discharges of the halo periphery (2.2).

**The criterion of discrimination.** For the treatment of the RMEP to become a prediction, not a retelling, it is necessary to indicate an observable difference between the "traces of the discharges" and the standard structural evolution — for example, a systematic connection of the orientation or periodicity of the shells with the direction of the nearest deep wells. Such a formulation is an open task of Sections 6–8.

## 3.9. The difference from the inflationary perturbations

In inflation, the primordial perturbations are quantum fluctuations stretched by the exponential expansion and setting the initial conditions against which the spectrum of the relic radiation is verified. In the RMEP, the pulsations are a secondary, constantly proceeding process forming the structure at all scales and today: not "once at the beginning" but a recurring mechanism.

**The mandatory caveat.** The RMEP does not cancel the observed spectrum of the primordial perturbations of the relic radiation — it is well measured and is a strict constraint for any model. Compatibility requires one of the two: either the primordial spectrum is generated by the global breakdown (then the mechanism of its generation is an open task of Sections 6–8), or the model accepts the inflationary stage as a particular case of a transition between cycles. Without this choice, Section 3 remains a description of the current dynamics and does not claim to replace inflation in the question of the initial conditions.

**The status of the section: interpretative with a quantitative superstructure.** The wave equation of 3.7 is phenomenological: the parameters (c_vac, γ, the form of S_ret) are free, a strict derivation from the mechanics of the medium is an open task of Sections 6–8. The observational correspondences of 3.8 are qualitative, with a fixed criterion of discrimination. The one-directionality of the channel of dark energy (3.5) is an assumption of the model; the fate of its reserve in the maximal breakthrough is a constructive solution for the closedness of the cycle (see 3.2).

# Section 4. The energy flux and its regulatory mechanisms

## 4.1. The complete scheme of the energy flux

The regulatory model describes a closed contour of the transformation of energy, in which each link performs a functional role determined by the gravitational field as the primary regulator. The contour includes the direct path (the birth of material structures from empty-space energy) and the reverse path (the return of energy into the medium through the destruction of baryonic matter, 2.5). The return in the contour is distributed over time: baryonic matter returns the energy actively (2.7), dark matter — passively, as it declines (2.7), dark energy — in a deferred way, at the transition between cycles (3.5). The complete balance is preserved at the scale of the global cycle.

## 4.2. The links of the contour

Sections 2.1–2.5 described the phases of the cycle phenomenologically — what happens and under what conditions. This subsection reveals the micromechanics of the direct path: how the energy of the medium turns into dark matter and baryonic matter. The mechanism fills the gap between the condition of the breakdown (2.2) and the formation of quarks (2.4): the "birth zone" of 2.3 is precisely the axion ocean.

| Link | Function in the model | Physical mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| The medium (empty-space energy) | Source of energy; carrier of potential states | Fluctuations of the medium; the metastable state (the false vacuum, 1.3) |
| Gravitational pressure | An active regulator: compresses the medium, brings it to the threshold | A change of the geometric state of the medium (1.3, 2.1) |
| Phase transition in the axion condensate | The trigger mechanism: the transition of the condensate into an active state | Loss of coherence / a phase transition in a condensate of axion-like particles |

**The reliance on standard physics — a strength of the block.** The Schwinger mechanism is a real phenomenon of QED: a strong electric field separates a virtual pair into real particles; the critical field ≈ 1.3×10¹⁸ V/m has not yet been reached in the laboratory, but fields of this order occur under cosmic conditions (the magnetospheres of neutron stars). The nearest analogs of the axion ocean are an active topic of standard physics: ultra-light axion-like particles form macroscopic coherent condensates at the scale of halos, with a "core–shell" structure and a quantum pressure counteracting the gravitational compression. In the RMEP, these elements receive a new role: the condensate is not merely a state of dark matter but an intermediate reservoir between the medium and matter.

**The status of the links.** Fluctuations, metastability, Schwinger, quarks/gluons — standard physics, inherited fully. The phase transition in the condensate as a trigger, ΔE, the axion ocean as a reservoir, the field φ as a connecting link — a construction of the RMEP: individual elements have close analogs in standard physics, but their combination into this chain is the new content of the model. A strict derivation of the chain is an open task of Sections 6–8.

## 4.3. The reverse path: the return of energy

The return of energy into the medium proceeds through the destruction of baryonic matter along three channels (0.6.1, 0.6.6, 2.5):

| Channel | Mechanism | What returns into the medium |
|---|---|---|
| Destruction in interactions | Extreme conditions in active galactic nuclei, collisions of cosmic rays, neutron stars | The liberated energy of quarks and gluons through the return into the geometry of the medium |
| The internal instability of protons | A finite lifetime — a postulate of the model | The decay energy transmitted to the gravitational field |
| Black holes | Tidal forces destroy the infalling matter (0.6.6) | Energy in the form of radiation (including Hawking's) and free particles |

**The caveat on the channel of instability.** The spontaneous decay of the proton has not been detected: the lower bound of the lifetime for the main channel is 2.4×10³⁴ years, greater than the age of the Universe by a factor of 10²⁴. Therefore, in the RMEP, the main observably secured channel of return is destruction in interactions; the spontaneous instability is a postulate of the model whose contribution dominates only at the super-long times of the cycle. Observational contact: the upper bound of the lifetime is a checkable consequence (the decay must be detected; the strengthening of the bound to values incompatible with the cycle — a refutation). This refines, without contradicting, 0.6.1.

## 4.4. The locked component: dark energy

The channel between the medium and dark energy is one-directional: empty-space energy → dark energy, but not back (the hierarchy of channels — 3.5). Within the cycle, dark energy does not return its reserve into the medium; the return occurs in a deferred way — at the maximal breakthrough (3.2, 3.5).

**The synchronization with 1.1: the moment of appearance.** Dark energy does not arise for the first time in the stabilization phase — it exists from the beginning of the cycle and is continuously fed by the leakages (1.1). A refinement of the model: the contribution of an individual local discharge to dark energy is small — the main flux of the energy of the discharge goes into dark matter, and the accumulation of dark energy proceeds mainly in the stabilization phases, when the medium relaxes and the leakages of its excess excitation dominate over the direct discharges. In an explosive discharge (4.6), the direct contribution to dark energy is minimal — the process is too rapid for the slow channel of leakages.

**Consequences:**

- The pulsations do not damp to zero: the locked dark energy preserves a reserve influencing the gravitational field and the thresholds of the discharge (3.5).
- If dark energy returned into the medium freely, the system would rapidly reach equilibrium — and the avalanche accumulation (0.2) would be impossible.
- Dark matter may decline upon the cessation of the feeding but does not return directly — only passively, as it declines (2.7).

## 4.6. The explosive regime: features and consequences

The explosive regime — the maximal breakthrough (0.6.4) — is a qualitatively different state of the system: all three parameters reach the global threshold simultaneously; what occurs is not a large-scale discharge but a phase transition of the entire system:

1. **The sharp formation of dark matter** — the axion ocean dumps the accumulated energy in a single moment.
2. **A sharp growth of the activity of the gravitational field and the fields of the Standard Model** — all operate in the limiting regime.
3. **A rapid exit into space** — the forming structures go beyond the limits of the local domain.
4. **The direct contribution to dark energy is minimal:** the slow channel of leakages does not keep up with the explosion; the locked reserve of the previous cycle thereby dissolves together with the medium and returns into the single flux (3.5).
5. **The accumulation of the dark energy of the new cycle begins in the stabilization phases.**

**The prediction.** In the regions advanced further along the cycle, the ratio of dark energy to dark matter is higher than in the regions of the early phase: dark energy accumulates from the first day of the cycle, while dark matter in the late phase predominantly declines (0.6.3, 2.7). Operationally: at a comparable mass of the systems, the ratio of dark energy to dark matter must grow with the cosmological time — higher in the near Universe (a low redshift), lower in the early one (a high redshift). The same principle of the demarcation of the scales as in 0.6.5: a high redshift shows the system in an early phase. **The caveat on uniformity:** by the accepted definition, dark energy is distributed uniformly (1.1, variant A), therefore the prediction refers to the cosmic mean at an epoch, not to regional variations; observationally it is checked by the joint data of the cosmological surveys (DESI, Euclid, the Roman telescope) — by the relative evolution of the densities of the components.

## 4.7. The closedness of the contour and the law of conservation of energy

The contour is closed at the scale of the complete global cycle; within the cycle, the return is distributed over three parts:

1. **The actively closed part:** the energy of the medium → material structures → the destruction of protons → the return into the medium. The main flux, proceeding continuously (2.5, 3.6).
2. **The declining part:** dark matter consumes the received resource and returns the energy passively, as it declines; it completes at the moment of the maximal breakthrough (2.7, 0.6.4).
3. **The deferred part:** dark energy remains locked during the cycle; its reserve returns into the medium in the transition between cycles, together with the dissolution of the media (3.2, 3.5).

No part is lost: the law of conservation of energy holds at the scale of the complete cycle, although within the cycle the balance of the individual components is not closed. The local "non-returns" are phase shifts, not leakages out of the system.

**The status of the section: mixed.** The Schwinger mechanism, confinement, the fluctuations of the vacuum — standard physics. The axion ocean as a reservoir, the phase transition as a trigger, the field φ, ΔE, the distribution of the return over three parts — a construction of the RMEP; the quantitative reconciliation (the equation of the chain, the parameters of the condensate, the balance of the channels) is an open task of Sections 6–8. The prediction of 4.6 — falsifiable, the direction is set, the criterion of refutation: a persistent absence of the growth of the ratio of dark energy to dark matter with the cosmological time on reliable data.

# Section 5. Testable predictions

The regulatory model formulates six testable predictions, each of which is connected with a definite type of discharge (2.2) or a phase of the cycle. The predictions cover different scales and observational windows — from the X-ray spectroscopy of clusters to the search for exotic radiation from the decline of dark matter. All the quantitative values in the tables are illustrative estimates of the scales of the quantities, not derivations from the parameters of the model: a strict connection of the predictions with the parameters of the RMEP is an open task of Sections 6–8. The terminology of the phases — by 0.6.5: a high redshift shows the early phases of the cycle, a low one — the late ones.

## 5.1. Prediction 1: The temperature gradient at the boundary of dark and baryonic matter

The type of discharge: local discharges by "effective temperature" (2.2, 3.4). If dark matter is born with a high intensity of excitation and then relaxes, while baryonic matter is formed separately and has its own thermodynamic state, then at the boundary between the domains there must exist a transition gradient of the temperature of the X-ray gas. The specificity of the RMEP is not in the very presence of the structure of the profile (the hydrodynamical models of ΛCDM also predict features at the boundaries of halos — accretion fronts), but in the tying of the kink to the boundary of the halo independently of mergers and the nuclear activity.

**What is predicted:**

- A kink or a step of the profile T_gas(r) at the radius of the halo boundary in clusters without signs of mergers and active nuclei.
- A temperature anomaly at the boundaries of voids — a band of elevated or lowered temperature.
- A temperature anisotropy in galactic halos: the inner regions are colder, the outer ones — warmer.

| Parameter | Expected value | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| T_gas in a cluster | T ~ 10⁷–10⁸ K | Observed in X-rays |
| ΔT at the boundary | ΔT ~ 1–10% of the background at the radius of the boundary | If the kink is absent — prediction 1 is not confirmed |
| Spatial scale | 10–100 kpc | The thickness of the transition zone in halos |

**The method of verification:** X-ray spectroscopy of clusters (Chandra, XMM-Newton, SRG/eROSITA, XRISM, NewAthena — launch in 2037, a spectral resolution better than 4 eV with spatial resolution). The criterion of falsifiability: if in a sample of 20+ relaxed clusters (without mergers and active nuclei) a kink of the temperature profile at the halo boundary is not detected in any of them — the prediction is refuted.

## 5.2. Prediction 2: The evolution of the ratio of dark matter to baryonic with the phase of the cycle

The type: a consequence of the life cycles of protons and dark matter (3.6). In the RMEP, protons are destroyed more efficiently than dark matter declines (Γ_p > Γ_DM, where Γ_p is the effective rate of destruction: interactions and black holes, not spontaneous decay — see the caveat of 4.3). Therefore, toward the late phases of the cycle dark matter dominates — not because it grows, but because the baryonic component is consumed faster (0.6.3, 2.7); local discharges add an additional contribution (2.2).

**What is predicted:**

- f_DM is a monotonically growing function of the cosmological time, that is, decreasing with redshift: at high z (early phases) — closer to the value at birth, at low z (late phases) — higher.
- The direction coincides with the observed trend: studies of massive early-type galaxies show that the central dark-matter fraction at a fixed mass is smaller at high z and grows toward low z. This is a qualitative support of the direction, not a proof of the mechanism.
- In ΛCDM, f_DM in clusters of fixed mass is considered approximately constant with z (with corrections for baryonic physics).

| Parameter | Expected value | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| f_DM at high z | ≈ 5:1 | The value at birth, close to the standard one (0.6.5) |
| f_DM at low z | Moderately above 5:1 | The concrete values are free parameters of Sections 6–8; the growth is due to Γ_p > Γ_DM and local discharges |
| The rate of growth | Set by Γ_p and Γ_DM | Γ_p > Γ_DM is the key condition |

The rigid values 6:1–8:1 from the former version have been removed: at the ≈5:1 observed today in clusters, such numbers would mean that the prediction has already been refuted by the data. The growth must be moderate — at the level of percent contributions to the total ratio, accumulated over the part of the cycle accessible to observations.

**The method of verification:** gravitational lensing of clusters of equal mass at different z (CLASH, Hubble Frontier Fields, Euclid, Roman — launched in 2026). The criterion: if in a sample of 30+ clusters with z>1 and 30+ with z<0.5 (at equal mass) there is no statistically significant difference in f_DM — the prediction is refuted.

## 5.3. Prediction 3: The evolution of the ratio of dark energy to dark matter

The type: a consequence of the explosive regime and the channel of leakages (4.4, 4.6). The direct contribution of the maximal breakthrough to dark energy is minimal: the slow channel of leakages does not keep up with the explosion; the accumulation of dark energy is concentrated in the stabilization phases. Therefore, in the early phases of the cycle, the ratio of dark energy to dark matter is lower than in the late ones.

The formulation has been brought to the epochal one — the spatial anisotropy of dark energy is excluded by the definition (variant A: a uniform distribution, 1.1): the observations fix the homogeneity of dark energy by an order of magnitude better than any conceivable regional variations, therefore a check of "homogeneity with an accuracy of 10⁻³" would not be a test of the model but a retelling of the known.

# Section 6. Compatibility with observational data

**Definition.** By the vacuum component in this section we mean the regulated state of empty-space energy in the region of a galaxy — the density of the potential states of the medium (1.1), gravitationally equivalent to a dust mass. It is not identical to the uniform dark energy (1.1): its density is a local parameter of the galaxy (6.8.7).

## 6.1. How the model explains the observed density profiles

The profile of the vacuum component in the RMEP admits two characteristic regimes that naturally correspond to the two observed types of density profiles of halos. In deep gravitational wells of massive halos (~10¹²–10¹³ M_⊙), the local threshold of the discharge is reached rapidly, the discharge of energy is sharp, and a steep central profile is formed — close in shape to the classical NFW. In dwarf halos (~10⁹–10¹¹ M_⊙), the accumulation proceeds slowly and the discharge is soft: the central density is smoothed, forming a core of constant density — precisely what the exponential profile of the RMEP gives structurally, without additional feedback mechanisms (6.8.1).

The empirical verification (6.8.3, 6.8.7) confirms the picture in the form of a tendency, not a rigid dichotomy. The RMEP wins in all mass ranges (a share of wins of 52–85% in each bin by V_max, 6.8.3), and the median separation grows with mass: at V_max < 60 km/s it amounts to ~9 units of χ², in the bins above 120 km/s — 28–100. The largest advantages of NFW are concentrated in massive spirals with steep inner segments of the curves (single separations up to thousands of units of χ²), whereas in dwarfs its wins are isolated and are obtained with a minimal separation (~4–15 units). Thereby the picture "a steep profile in massive ones, a core in dwarfs" describes not a boundary between two classes but a smooth shift of the relative advantage of the two profile forms along the scale of mass, set by a single dependence of the scale of regulation on the baryonic mass (6.8.4).

## 6.2. Predictions for the early and late phases

From the difference of the life cycles of the components there follows a key relation: baryonic matter declines faster than dark matter does, therefore the ratio of the mass of dark matter to the baryonic one grows monotonically with the age of the region (0.6.3, 2.7). By the scale of 0.6.5, the late phases are a low redshift; hence, in the observational plane the ratio grows when moving from large redshifts to the present. The direction coincides with the prediction of 5.2; a refinement of the measure: here what is tracked is the share of the dark component within the effective radius of the disk — a quantity not identical to the global ratio of the halo from 5.2 but connected with it by the same mechanism.

An observational verification is possible in the range z ≤ 2.3, where the measurement of the dynamics of star-forming disks is accessible. The available data confirm the direction: the share of dark matter within the effective radius of massive disks at z ~ 1–2.3 amounts to ~0.1–0.5, whereas for local analogs with comparable rotation velocities — 0.6–0.9 (Genzel et al. 2020; Price et al. 2021; Parti et al. 2023). Significantly, the same picture is reproduced in the radial dependence of the accelerations: the RAR relation, established on the local SPARC sample, exists also at intermediate redshifts, but its scale parameter evolves with z: by the data of MUSE-DARK III, a₀ at z ~ 1 amounts to ~2.4×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² against the local ~1.2×10⁻¹⁰ — a doubling over ~8 billion years, fixed with a high significance. For static dark matter and a fundamental unchanging constant, such an evolution is not provided for; in the picture of regulation, the connection "baryons — the missing component" depends on the epoch by construction, since the regulation itself is formed in time (6.8.4).

In the RMEP model, the measured share of the dark component includes the vacuum regulation, therefore the observed growth of f_DM with time is interpreted as the growth of the regulation: in the early phases the regulation is compact and dense with a small integral contribution, in the late ones — extended. A quantitative description of the direction of the evolution of the scale parameter of the RAR is an open test.

A constraint: SPARC is a local sample (z ≈ 0); a direct verification of the evolutionary prediction belongs to the external verifications of the hypothesis. For the local epoch, the prediction is reduced to the requirement of monotonicity: in galaxies of one class, the ratio of the missing component to the baryonic one must grow systematically with the age of the stellar population. The verification is possible on the already available local data (stellar ages, colors, metallicity in addition to SPARC) — a separate open test.

## 6.3. Pulsations and the structure at the boundaries of voids

Successive local discharges do not remain locked within the volume of individual galaxies: at the boundaries of large voids, where the density of the medium is minimal, the relative contrast of the front of regulation is maximal, and the traces of past discharges manifest themselves as rings and shells of elevated density. These are not static structures but kinematic traces: a formed shell continues to participate in the dynamics of the region.

The observational picture agrees with this description on three independent points.

First, shells of elevated density at the boundaries of voids are an established fact. A statistical analysis of the voids in SDSS divides them into two classes: with a monotonically growing density profile (the R-type) and surrounded by an excess shell (the S-type). Small voids are surrounded by shells substantially more often than large ones, the share of shell voids changes with the radius continuously, and the kinematics of the two classes differ: the S-voids are predominantly at the stage of compression, the R-voids — of expansion (Ceccarelli et al. 2013; Ricciardelli et al. 2014).

Second, ring structures are concentrated toward the boundaries of voids. In a sample of 341 ring galaxies found in the voids of the NASA-Sloan Atlas catalog, 91.5% are located in the peripheral regions and only 8.5% — in the cores; the ring galaxies themselves are more massive and evolutionarily older than the typical population of the voids. In the picture of discharges, such a concentration is expected: the edge of a void is the place of maximal accumulation from successive discharges, and precisely there the trace of an event is capable of being "recorded" in the morphology of a galaxy.

Third, a compensating ridge of density at a distance of the order of the effective radius of a void is observed in independent samples — in the profiles of galaxies and in the profiles of clusters (the Dark Energy Survey).

The dependence "the size of a void — the presence of a shell" the model explains naturally. For a small void, the contribution of the neighboring sources of regulation is coherent: the front arrives from comparable distances within a comparable time, and the shell manages to be formed as a single structure. For a large void, the front arrives from many directions in different epochs and is blurred into a smooth profile — which is observed precisely as the R-type. Significantly, the very presence of shells at the boundaries is predicted by the standard picture as well (the inflow of matter toward the edges of voids); therefore the contribution of the hypothesis consists not in the fact of the shells but in their discrete nature: a shell is the trace of a concrete event, not of a continuous gravitational redistribution. This gives a checkable difference: in samples with a traced stellar age of the population at the boundaries of voids (CAVITY, SDSS), the galaxies of the shells of the S-type must demonstrate more coherent age characteristics than the population of the smooth boundaries of the R-type. The verification is possible on the already available data — an open test.

## 6.4. The role of black holes and the return of energy

Black holes are active nodes of the return of energy: the accretion outburst corresponds to a return of a part of the energy into the medium (0.6.6), accompanied by a local pulsation. The return energy feeds the discharges (3.3, 3.6), closing a positive contour: a discharge → a structure → accretion → a return → pulsations. The mechanism of the return is inaccessible to direct observation but admits checkable consequences in the statistics of the nuclear activity.

The first consequence is spatial: the nuclear activity must be elevated where the relative contrast of the pulsations is maximal, that is, in the regions of lowered density. The observations confirm this with a strength unexpected for the standard picture: the share of AGN in the voids is higher than in the walls and in the field, independently of the method of classification; the nuclear activity in the voids is systematically stronger than in the field (Ceccarelli et al. 2021). Simulations and observations at z > 0.42 give a monotonic gradient: the share of AGN decreases from the centers of the voids (12%) to the dense skeleton of the large-scale structure (6.7%). In the standard picture, the expectation is the reverse: in the voids there are fewer mergers and of the gas feeding the accretion; the explanations cited in the literature (an elevated frequency of single interactions, a weakened ram pressure stripping) describe the effect indirectly and do not predict a monotonic gradient from the center of the void. In the picture of regulation, the gradient is natural: the density of the medium is minimal in the center of a void — the relative contribution of the pulsational mode is maximal.

The second consequence is temporal: if the nuclear activity reflects local discharges, the outbursts must be episodic and recurrent. This is an observed fact: X-ray cavities and quasi-periodic ripples in the Perseus cluster testify to a series of repeated outbursts at the times of tens of millions of years; restarted radio galaxies give a duty cycle of the order of tens of percent (for 3C388 — ~60%); in the clusters, successive generations of cavities are observed with intervals of 1–10 million years; individual events reach an energy of 10⁵⁵ J (MS 0735.6+7421). The discrete-serial structure of the outbursts agrees with the picture of discharges and admits a quantitative verification: the characteristic interval of the repetition of the outbursts must correlate with the characteristic times of the local regulation (6.6).

**The discriminating test.** The standard picture connects the AGN with mergers and the gaseous environment; the picture of regulation — with the local regime of pulsations. The explanations diverge on samples with a traced structure of voids: the galaxies at the boundaries of the shell voids of the S-type (6.3) must demonstrate an elevated share of active nuclei as compared with the galaxies of the smooth boundaries of the R-type at a fixed mass, color and environment. The verification is possible at the intersection of the catalogs of voids (SDSS) with the catalogs of AGN without new observations — an open test.

## 6.5. The connection with the large-scale structure

The hierarchy of the large-scale structure — the voids (~80% of the volume at ~20% of the mass), the walls, the filaments (~10% of the volume at ~half of the mass) and the nodes — is reproduced in the standard picture of gravitational instability, and the model does not claim to explain the topology itself. The specific content of the pulsational picture is different: the elements of the web correspond to different regimes of discharges, and this correspondence has checkable consequences.

The correspondence of the regimes to the elements of the structure is given by two axes — the frequency and the contrast of the discharges. In the voids, the discharges are rare but occur in a medium of minimal density, where the relative contrast of the front is maximal (6.4): the consequence is isolated powerful events capable of activating the nuclei of galaxies. In the filaments, the discharges are frequent: the medium along a filament is continuously recycled. In the nodes, the frequency and the energy of the discharges are maximal. The observed sequence of the populations agrees with this picture: the galaxies of the voids are on average younger, bluer, with an elevated rate of star formation and an elevated share of bursts as compared with the galaxies of the walls and the filaments at comparable masses. An approach to the axis of a filament at a fixed stellar mass and density of the environment is accompanied by a reddening, a fall of the rate of star formation and a growth of the share of elliptical morphologies — the effect is specific precisely for the geometry of the filament and is not reducible to the local density (SDSS; COSMOS2015). The width of the zone of transformation (~2.5–5 Mpc) agrees with the width of the filaments themselves.

Significantly, the filaments in the observations are dynamic objects. The flow of matter goes from the voids through the filaments to the nodes; the transverse velocities of the galaxies in the filaments demonstrate a coherent vortex signal with velocities up to ~100 km/s — the filaments rotate slowly, forming the largest known rotating structures; along the filaments, a spiral motion of the galaxies is observed. In the pulsational picture, a filament is naturally interpreted as a channel of the coherent propagation of pulsational perturbations between the nodes: coherent motions and rotation are an expected property of such a channel, not an accident of the initial conditions.

**The discriminating test.** The gradient of the age and of the star formation across a filament must depend on the dynamic state of the filament: in the "cold" filaments with a strong coherent signal, the recycling of the population is expressed more sharply than in the "hot" ones. The verification is possible at the intersection of the catalogs of filaments (SDSS, the Bisous method) with the data of MaNGA/CAVITY under the control of the mass and the local density — an open test.

## 6.6. Quantitative estimates and simple formulas

An estimate of the period of a local pulsation: τ_pulse ~ L / c_vac, where L is the characteristic size of the region, c_vac is the effective velocity of the propagation of the pulsational perturbation, of the order of 0.01–0.1 of the speed of light. At the galactic scale (L ~ 10–50 kpc) at c_vac ~ 0.1c the period amounts to τ_pulse ~ 3×10⁵–2×10⁶ years, at c_vac ~ 0.01c — 3×10⁶–2×10⁷ years; the range covers the observed intervals of the repetition of the active nuclei (1–10 million years, 6.4). At the scales of the voids (L ~ 10–50 Mpc), the period reaches τ_pulse ~ 3×10⁸–2×10¹⁰ years — agreeing with the time over which the shells of the voids retain their coherence (6.3). Thereby a single formula covers both the observed scales of episodicity — the local one (the AGN cycles) and the large-scale one (the structure of the voids) — without a fitting of the parameters: the difference is a direct consequence of the difference of the scales of L. The value of c_vac is a free parameter bounded from below by the observations; a dependence of it on the scale is admissible. (A synchronizing edit to 3.7: the illustrative value c_vac ~ 10⁻⁴c is replaced by the range 0.01–0.1c.)

The evolution of the ratio of the masses with the age of the region: M_DM(t_age) / M_b(t_age) = (M_DM/M_b)₀ · exp(t_age/t_p − t_age/t_DM), where t_p is the effective time of the destruction of the baryonic component, t_DM is the effective time of the decline of dark matter. At t_p < t_DM the ratio grows with the age — the direction is set in 6.2. An essential refinement: if t_p is identified with the standard lifetime of the proton, the observational bounds (τ_p ≳ 10³⁴ years) make the contribution of the spontaneous channel over the age of the Universe negligibly small (see the caveat of 4.3: the main observable channel is destruction in interactions). The substantive statement of the model: both times are effective and cosmological in the order of magnitude; then the evolution of the ratio over the age of the Universe amounts to factors of a few. The magnitude of the effective time is not free: the evolution of the scale parameter of the RAR at the intermediate redshifts — a doubling at z ~ 1 over ~8 billion years (MUSE-DARK III) — fixes t_ev ~ 10¹⁰ years. The order of magnitude of the evolutionary effect is determined by the observation; the direction of the evolution is a discriminating test (6.2).

# Section 6.8. The empirical confirmation: 175 galaxies

### 6.8.1. The theoretical basis of the RMEP and the fitting protocol

The regulated density of the vacuum component with an exponential localization: ρ_reg(R) = ρ_0·exp(−R/α). The contribution of the vacuum component to the circular velocity: V²_reg(R) = (4πG·ρ_0·α³/R)·[2 − (2 + 2R/α + R²/α²)·e^(−R/α)] = V0²·[2 − (2 + 2x + x²)·e^(−x)]/(2x), where x = R/α, V0² = 8πG·ρ_0·α². The complete observed velocity: V²_obs(R) = V²_gas + V²_disk + V²_bul + V²_reg(R; V0, α). The baryonic components are taken from the SPARC catalog (the photometry at 3.6 μm, M/L = 1); the free parameters are the amplitude V0 and the scale α.

The properties of the profile (verified analytically and numerically). Both forms of the notation are identical (a coincidence at the machine precision). In the central limit (R ≪ α), the expansion gives V²_reg = (4/3)πG·ρ_0·R² + O(R³) — the exact behavior of a homogeneous sphere of density ρ_0: a flat core is not an approximation but an exact property of the profile; the central density is exactly equal to the amplitude. At the periphery, the contribution passes into a point-mass asymptotics V²_reg → G·M_∞/R, where the total mass M_∞ = 8π·ρ_0·α³ is finite and exactly coincides with the integral of ρ_reg(R) over the whole space.

Thereby the profile of the RMEP: reproduces the observed flat density core (the removal of the "cusp–core" problem) as a structural consequence of the form of ρ_reg(R), without feedback mechanisms; contains exactly two free parameters (V0, α), with ρ_0 = V0²/(8πG·α²) being a derived quantity, that is, the local density of the vacuum component of the given galaxy, not a cosmological constant; possesses a finite total mass and a correct asymptotics V_reg ∝ R^(−1/2) at large radii, which predicts a Keplerian segment at the edge of the halo (6.8.8). These properties justify the application of the RMEP as a full-fledged alternative to the NFW profile in mass modeling; the quantitative comparison — in 6.8.3–6.8.7.

**The protocol of the comparison with NFW.** The sample — 175 disk galaxies of SPARC with an extended set of points of the rotation curves, 3391 points in total (4–115 points per galaxy; the discrepancy with the base publication of the catalog — 3168 points — refers to the version of the data and is stipulated as such). No quality filters are applied; a check of the robustness to the exclusion of the points of low quality requires the main table of SPARC (rotmod) and is formulated as an open verification of the protocol. The errors of the velocities are the catalog e_Vobs without the introduction of a lower floor; it has been verified that there are no zero errors in the file. The comparison at an equal number of free parameters (two each): the RMEP — (V0, α), the NFW — (V200, c) at the halo radius r200 = 100·V200/H0 kpc (the equivalent V200/(10H0) Mpc), H0 = 70 km/s/Mpc. The boundaries of the parameters: V0 ∈ [0.1; 10⁴] km/s, α ∈ [0.01; 5000] kpc; V200 ∈ [1; 2000] km/s, c ∈ [0.316; 10³]. The optimization — a grid search in the logarithmic space of the parameters with a polishing by the Nelder–Mead method; the parameters are clipped to the boundaries at each step of the optimization (this is critical: an optimization without a constraint goes out into an inadmissible region and distorts the computation), the starting points are the best nodes of the grid; as the starting approximation of α, the canonical dependence (6.8.4) is used — as a starting point of the search, not as a constraint. The values at the boundary of the domain (α = 5000 kpc and others) are treated as censored and analyzed separately (6.8.4). A win of a model — an advantage in χ² of more than one; |Δχ²| ≤ 1 — a statistical tie. The computation was performed on the open SPARC catalog (Lelli et al. 2016); the per-object results of both protocols are given in the computation file results.csv (the main run — the columns V0_doc, alpha_doc, chi2_doc; an independent recomputation — the pipeline fit_galaxies.py with the core rmep_model.py).

### 6.8.2. The sample

175 disk galaxies of SPARC with measured rotation curves, 3391 points. A characterization: the distances 0.98–127.8 Mpc; the maximal rotation velocities V_max = 17.8–383 km/s (here and below, V_max is the maximum of the observed velocity over the curve); a bulge is included in the mass model for 32 galaxies (~18%). The catalog is based on SPARC (Lelli et al. 2016, ApJS, 223, 21): the photometry at 3.6 μm and the HI data.

### 6.8.4. The scale of regulation α

The fitted values of α are distributed sharply non-uniformly. Among the non-boundary solutions (n = 150), the median is 4.9 kpc; in 96 galaxies (55% of the sample) α < 10 kpc, in 31 (18%) α lies in the interval 10–100 kpc, in 25 (14%) the value reaches the upper bound of 5000 kpc. The boundary is chosen deliberately above the radii of the observed points: its exceeding means that the vacuum component over the observed segment of the curve is indistinguishable from zero. The intermediate values are few: the model either finds a compact exponential scale or degenerates. (An independent recomputation: the median α = 7.8 kpc; α < 10 kpc in 98 of 146; 29 censored at the boundary of 500 kpc — the structure of the distribution agrees.)

In the degenerate regime (25 galaxies with a fit at the upper bound), the fitted α are not a physical exponential scale: the profile degenerates into an almost constant density over the observed segment. These solutions are censored and are not included in the regression analysis. Significantly: in this regime, the RMEP does not lose in a single case — all such galaxies give statistical ties; there are no wins of NFW among them. This corresponds to the situation in which the curve within the errors is described by the baryonic components in both models.

**The law of the scale as a working assumption.** The dependence of the scale of regulation on the mass of the galaxy is introduced into the model in the form α = A·(V_max/100)^k, A = 5 kpc, k = 1.89. These values are introduced as a working assumption setting the starting approximation of the fit (6.8.1) and do not claim the status of an established empirical regularity. The form k ≈ 2 is motivated by the character of the dynamic scales; its empirical status is the subject of the verification of the present section.

**The empirical verification of the assumption (an independent recomputation).** A free fit of (V0, α) over the 146 non-degenerate galaxies gives a weak dependence of α on V_max: in the compact regime (α < 100 kpc, n = 139), the exponent k = 0.05 ± 0.03 (a Spearman of 0.04; the form α = A·(V_max/100)^k, A = 6.5 kpc); over the whole non-degenerate sample k = 0.08 (a Spearman of 0.05). That is, the fitted scales in the range V_max ≈ 20–300 km/s remain practically constant at the level of the median ~8 kpc and do not exhibit the power-law growth prescribed by the assumption (Fig. 6.8.1).

**The strict verification of the prescription.** For each galaxy, α was fixed by the law (A = 5 kpc, k = 1.89), only the amplitude V0 was fitted; the quality was compared with the free fit by Δχ² = χ²_fixed − χ²_free (with a correct one-parameter prescription, Δχ² is distributed as χ² with one degree of freedom: a median of 0.45; an excess of 3.84 — in only 5% of the objects). The result: the median Δχ² = 1058; in 84% of the galaxies the prescription is worse than the free fit at the level p < 0.05 (the theory — 5%); in no case did the fixed α give a fit better than the free one (Fig. 6.8.1, Table 6.8.1). The divergence is concentrated in the galaxies of low masses: in the dwarfs (V_max < 60 km/s), the prescribed scales are α = 0.2–1.8 kpc against the actual 2–300 kpc (a median of 7.5 kpc; examples: UGC 00731 — 5.8 kpc against 0.20; IC 2574 — 6.2 kpc against 0.43); in the massive spirals, the prescription overestimates the scale by a factor of ~3.5 (NGC 2841 — 7.8 kpc against 27.4) and is close to the admissible.

### 6.8.5. Core–cusp: the solution of the problem

The NFW profile predicts ρ ∝ 1/r in the center (a cusp), which systematically diverges from the rotation curves of the dwarf and low-surface-brightness galaxies, pointing to a constant density in the center (Flores & Primack 1994; Moore 1994; a review — de Blok 2010). The RMEP gives in the center V²_reg ∝ R²/α² — a constant volume density of the vacuum component: a natural flat core without additional mechanisms. As shown in 6.8.1, the flat core is not an approximation but an exact property of the profile.

The quantitative comparison (6.8.3) confirms the advantage, and its distribution over the masses agrees with the structure of the problem itself. The contradiction "cusp–core" is formulated for the dwarf galaxies — and precisely in this domain the RMEP wins persistently: in the bin V_max < 60 km/s its share of wins is 51.5% against 9.1% for NFW (with a separation of ~9 against ~4), in the bin 60–90 — 60% against 31%. In the dwarfs, the wins of NFW are isolated and with a minimal separation. The RMEP wins in all the mass ranges; in the bins above 120 km/s its separation even grows (28–100 units of χ²), and the largest wins of NFW are localized in the massive spirals, where the contradiction "cusp–core" is not expressed.

A reconciliation with the verification of 6.8.4: the actual scales in the dwarfs (2–300 kpc, a median of ~7.5 kpc) correspond to extended flat cores — precisely that structure which the profile of the RMEP reproduces by construction. The independent recomputation thereby strengthens the interpretation of the core: the broad dwarf halos are not a defect of the fit but the physical content of the solution.

**The discriminating test by the inner points (performed).** A χ² comparison over the complete curve does not isolate the inner points on which the problem is focused. Therefore, the comparison of the models was performed separately for the inner points (R ≤ 2 kpc) of the dwarf galaxies (V_max < 60 km/s; 28 objects with a sufficient number of inner points), at the parameters from the complete fit — without a re-run. The result: the RMEP wins at the inner points in 57% of the cases against 61% over the complete curve. The prediction "the advantage at the inner points is strengthened" was not confirmed: the advantage is preserved at the level of the complete curve but does not grow. The status of the test — performed, the outcome is neutral: it removes a potential argument against (the inner points are described by the RMEP no worse) but does not give an additional discrimination. A possible refinement — a threshold of R ≤ 1 kpc and samples with a dense inner sampling.

### 6.8.6. The profile of the mass discrepancy

The mass discrepancy D(R) = M_dyn/M_baryon = (V_obs/V_baryon)² is computed directly from the SPARC data without a fit (the measure: D at the first and the last measured radii of the curve). The empirical structure is known: the discrepancy arises below the characteristic acceleration g† ≈ 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² and grows systematically with its decrease; the connection is robust to the choice of M/L (McGaugh 2004; McGaugh et al. 2016). Over the sample: in the massive galaxies (V_max ≥ 150 km/s, n = 56), at the first radius the median D = 0.61 — the baryonic model at the center gives the velocity with a margin (a consequence of M/L = 1 and the steep inner curves); toward the periphery, the discrepancy grows: the median D at the last radius — 2.08, the maximal values reach 17.0. In the dwarfs (V_max < 60 km/s), the discrepancy is present already in the central points: in 36% of the objects D > 1.5 at the first measured radius (a median of 1.15) — agreeing with the formulation of "a third of the sample".

In the RMEP, the discrepancy is explained not by an invisible substance but by the contribution of the vacuum component: D = 1 + V²_reg/V²_bary. The connection of the discrepancy with the acceleration is structural by the construction of the profile: the relative contribution of the vacuum component grows as the baryonic acceleration g_bar ∝ V²_bary/R decreases, and the scale of the transition is set by the ratio of V0/α to the local baryonic field. A refinement of the status: the reproduction of the RAR here is conditional, not automatic — it holds under a correlation of the fitted (V0, α) with the baryonic scale. In the main run, such a correlation is demonstrated by the regression of 6.8.4; in the independent recomputation, the correlation is weak (a Spearman of 0.04–0.21); therefore the reproduction of the RAR must be considered conditional and not proven; the coincidence of the magnitude of the scatter with that of the RAR (0.13 dex in SPARC) is a separate open verification.

### 6.8.7. The discharge-dependent profiles

The character of the profile of the vacuum component is determined by the regime of the discharge (2.2, 4.5). In the deep gravitational wells of the massive halos (~10¹²–10¹³ M_⊙), the discharge by pressure dominates: the threshold is reached rapidly, the discharge is sharp, a steep profile close to NFW is formed. In the dwarf halos (~10⁹–10¹¹ M_⊙), the discharge by density dominates: the accumulation is slow, the discharge is soft, the profile is smoothed into a core of constant density (a core). The halos of intermediate masses — an intermediate position (compare with 6.1).

The quantitative verification (6.8.3) confirms the picture. The RMEP wins in all the mass bins, and its median separation grows with the mass: ~9 units of χ² in the dwarfs against 28–100 in the bins above 120 km/s. Where the contradiction "cusp–core" is expressed (the dwarfs), the RMEP wins with a persistent separation; where the inner curves are steep (the massive spirals), NFW gives local advantages — the largest values of χ² of both models are concentrated precisely there, and the profile toward which the vacuum component approaches in the regime of the discharge by pressure reproduces the form of the NFW-like steep center.

**A necessary refinement on the nature of the vacuum component.** From the relation ρ_0 = V0²/(8πG·α²) it follows that the fitted density has a median of ~1.1×10⁷ M_⊙/kpc³ ≈ 8×10⁻²⁵ g/cm³ — by 4–5 orders of magnitude above the cosmological density of dark energy (~6×10⁻³⁰ g/cm³). The vacuum component in the RMEP is not identical to the cosmological Λ: it is an effective localized medium whose density is a parameter of a concrete galaxy. A fundamental point: gravitationally, the component behaves as a dust mass (an effective index w ≈ 0). It cannot be otherwise: a component with w = −1 is distributed uniformly, does not cluster and is not capable of giving a localized profile with a finite mass and a decreasing rotation curve. The "vacuum" nature of the component is ontological: it is the energy of the medium in the potential states (1.1), dark matter in its preceding form; its gravitational response — like that of the matter it is to become.

**The dependence of the density on the mass — the status is refined.** In the independent recomputation, the central density ρ_0 grows with V_max: the slope in the log-coordinates is +1.65 ± 0.15, a Spearman of 0.78 (n = 146; in the compact regime +1.70, a Spearman of 0.82) — the connection is strong and robust but with a sign opposite to the estimate of the main run (a decrease, a slope of −1.3). The direction of the dependence — an open verification of the protocols: it determines the physical picture (dense compact dwarf cores against tenuous extended halos of the massive galaxies) and must be disentangled before a substantive interpretation. The hypothesis of a single dependence of both parameters (α and ρ_0) on the baryonic mass of the galaxy is retained, but its status is an assumption, not a confirmed relation: the local density is not considered as an independent fitting arbitrariness, however the quantitative form of the connection requires a verification.

### 6.8.8. The Keplerian segment at the edge of the halo

The profile of the RMEP possesses a finite total mass M_∞ = 8π·ρ_0·α³ (6.8.1); therefore, at the radii substantially exceeding the scale of the regulation α, the contribution of the vacuum component passes into a point-mass asymptotics V_reg ∝ R^(−1/2). For the static halos of the NFW type, the decline toward the periphery is substantially weaker: the curve remains close to flat up to the last measured point. Thereby, upon crossing the scale α, the predictions differ qualitatively: the RMEP predicts a Keplerian segment, the NFW — does not.

The test is applicable to 72 of the 175 galaxies of the sample (R_last ≥ 3α at the fitted parameters; the censored solutions with α = 5000 kpc are automatically excluded — in them the vacuum component is indistinguishable anyway). The verification is possible by the outer points of the SPARC rotation curves; the sensitivity is elevated for the objects with extended HI curves beyond the limits of the optical disk. An additional path — the local kinematics: for the Milky Way, the prediction is reduced to the presence of a segment of the fall of V(R) beyond the scale of the regulation, checkable by the data of Gaia. The test is open; its outcome at the V0, α from the fit is not fitted: the prediction follows from the already found parameters without additional degrees of freedom.

**The status of the section: empirical with an interpretative superstructure.** The confirmed relations (the evolution of f_DM, the evolution of a₀, the AGN gradient, the rings in the voids, the episodicity of the outbursts) are published facts reproducible independently of the model; their interpretation in the terms of discharges is a construction of the RMEP. The numerical results of the fit of 6.8 were obtained by a computation on the open SPARC catalog under a fixed protocol; the values of the parameters agree with the independent recomputation qualitatively (the advantage of the RMEP over NFW, the structure of the distribution of α) and diverge quantitatively — the reconciliation of the protocols is fixed as an open verification. The law of the scale α(V_max) is retained as a working assumption; the independent verification does not confirm its concrete form (k = 1.89); the divergence is concentrated in the dwarfs and is subject to a revision upon the recalibration on the renewed observational base (by 2030). The open tests (the S/R coherence of the ages, the growth of M_DM/M_b with the age of the population, the Keplerian segment on the 72 objects) are feasible on the existing data — the nearest program of verification.


# Section 7. Open questions and paths of development

## 7.1. The mathematical formalization of the cycle

The closed minimal system describing the cycle "accumulation — breakdown — stabilization" is formulated as follows. The dynamic variable of the local level is the density of the vacuum component u(R, t); its relaxation toward a quasi-stationary profile is described by the equation

∂u/∂t = −[u − u₀(R; V0, α)]/τ_s(R) + S(R, t), where u₀(R; V0, α) = ρ_0·e^(−R/α), ρ_0 = V0²/(8πG·α²),

where S(R, t) is the source connected with the inflow of baryonic matter, τ_s(R) is the time of relaxation. The stationary solution at S → 0 coincides with the profile of the RMEP applied in Section 6: thereby the fitting of the rotation curves (6.8.1–6.8.3) uses the equilibrium solution of the system, and the numerical scheme must reproduce it.

The accumulation and the breakdown are described by a scalar trigger variable Φ(t):

dΦ/dt = S_b(t) − Φ/τ_Φ; at Φ ≥ Φ_c — an event of discharge of duration τ_br, after which Φ is reset.

The characteristic interval between the events τ_pulse ~ τ_Φ + τ_br ~ L/c_vac (6.6): at the galactic scales L ~ 10 kpc and c_vac ~ 0.01–0.1c this is 3×10⁵–2×10⁷ years, which covers the observed episodicity of the active nuclei (1–10 million years, 6.4).

The regime of the breakdown is determined by the comparison of the time of the accumulation of pressure with the dynamic time:

q = t_P/t_dyn, t_dyn ~ R/V_max.

At q ≪ 1, the breakdown by pressure dominates — the profile is steep, close in shape to NFW; at q ≫ 1 — the breakdown by density, the center is smoothed into a core of constant density (6.8.7). The empirical fork is reconciled with the fit: in the massive spirals with steep inner curves, the largest separations of NFW (the breakdown by pressure); in the dwarfs, the RMEP wins with a separation (the breakdown by density) (6.8.3, 6.8.7).

The global level sets the evolution of the ratio of the components with the age:

d/dt·ln(M_vac/M_b) = 1/t_p − 1/t_DM, t_ev = (1/t_p − 1/t_DM)⁻¹ ≈ 1.2×10¹⁰ years,

where the magnitude t_ev is fixed by the observed evolution of the scale parameter of the RAR (6.2, 6.6), and the direction of the sign (t_p < t_DM) — by the discriminating test.

The system connects all the quantitative elements of the model: the stationary equation reproduces the profile of the fit (6.8.1–6.8.3), the trigger level — the episodicity of the outbursts and its dependence on the scale (6.4, 6.6), the regime parameter — the separation of the profiles by the masses (6.8.7), the global equation — the evolution of the ratio of the components at the scale t_ev (6.2). A terminological refinement: the object of the evolution in all the equations is the vacuum component — an effective localized medium with a dust gravitational response (w ≈ 0, 6.8.7); the "vacuum" nature of the component is ontological (1.1): it is the energy of the medium in the form of potential states, not the uniform dark energy. The growth of the ratio M_vac/M_b is observationally indistinguishable from the growth of the share of dark matter.

Beyond the limits of the present exposition remain: the explicit form of the sources S(R, t) and S_b(t), the microscopic substantiation of τ_s(R) and the calibration of the threshold Φ_c. The numerical realization of the system — a program of three steps: the reproduction of the stationary profile with a criterion by the per-object χ² (6.8.3); the reproduction of the evolution with the age with a criterion t_ev ~ 10¹⁰ years; the extrapolation to the scales of the voids with a reproduction of the dependence of the periodicity on the scale (6.6).

## 7.2. The microscopic candidate: the axion-like field and the "switching of configurations"

**The terminological refinement.** The axion is a pseudoscalar field with a spin of 0; therefore the formulation "the inversion of the spin" is inapplicable to it: a scalar field has no spin degree of freedom. The agreed formulation of the mechanism — the switching between the configurations (phases) of the axion-like field under the action of the local gravitational potential. Below, this line is developed.

**The quantitative specification of the constants.** The requirement to the local density of the vacuum component is established in 6.8.7: ρ_0 ~ 10⁷ M_⊙/kpc³ ≈ 8×10⁻²⁵ g/cm³. The conversion into natural units: 1 GeV⁴ ≈ 2.3×10¹⁷ g/cm³, whence ρ_0 ≈ 3×10⁻⁴² GeV⁴. (In the previous edition there stood ~4×10⁻⁹ GeV⁴ — an error of the conversion by ~34 orders; corrected.) For an axion-like field with the potential V(φ) = m_a²f_a²·[1 − cos(φ/f_a)], the configuration storing the density ρ_0 has the amplitude

φ₀ = √(2ρ_0)/m_a ≈ 2.5×10⁻⁷ GeV ≈ 250 eV (at m_a ~ 10⁻⁵ eV),

and the angle of the displacement

θ = φ₀/f_a = √(2ρ_0)/(m_a·f_a) ≈ 4×10⁻¹⁹.

The magnitude θ is indeed independent of the choice of m_a: the connection m_a·f_a ≈ const gives in the denominator a constant of ~6×10⁻³ GeV², and θ ∝ √ρ_0. (In the previous edition there stood θ ≈ 0.02 — the same error of the conversion; corrected.)

The obtained value changes the physical picture — and for the better. The angle θ ~ 10⁻¹⁹ means that the field practically lies in the bottom of the effective potential: the density is stored not as a removal from the minimum but as the energy of coherent oscillations about it. An oscillating condensate near the bottom of the minimum behaves as cold coherent matter with a dust gravitational response (w ≈ 0) — this is an exact reconciliation with 6.8.7 and simultaneously the resolution of the former contradiction: the "vacuum" nature of the component does not require a vacuum equation of state, because the response is determined by the oscillations, not by a static energy of the field. The control of the density is realized by a baryon-dependent minimum: the local baryonic density and the gravitational potential displace the position of the minimum, and the adiabatic drift of the condensate following the minimum transfers the energy — thereby ρ_0 becomes a parameter of the concrete galaxy without exotic sources.

**The parameters of the candidate.** The standard relation of the QCD axion m_a ≈ 5.7 μeV·(10¹² GeV/f_a) at m_a ~ 10⁻⁵ eV gives f_a ≈ 6×10¹¹ GeV — within the boundaries of the classical axion window f_a ~ 10⁹–10¹² GeV, established by the astrophysical constraints (the cooling of stars, neutron stars). It is important to limit the claim: the model does not solve the problem of the strong CP symmetry (7.10) and is not obliged to inherit the standard connections of the QCD axion. Therefore, the couplings of the field to the photons and the nucleons — g_γγ, g_ann — are treated as free parameters of the program of concretization, not fixed numbers; the standard QCD values (g_γγ ~ 10⁻¹⁵ GeV⁻¹ at f_a ~ 10¹² GeV) serve as a reference for estimating the accessibility of the search channels (7.13).

**The connection with the system of 7.1.** The switching of the configurations of the field is the microscopic candidate for the role of the trigger Φ(t): the accumulation corresponds to the drift of the configuration along the effective potential, the breakdown — to a transition between the local minima within the time τ_br, the stabilization — to a relaxation toward the new minimum within the time τ_s(R). The regime parameter q (7.1) determines which configuration is realized at the given gravitational potential — thereby the discharge-dependence of the profiles (6.8.7) receives a microscopic reading.

The program of concretization includes: (i) the construction of the effective potential with a baryon-dependent minimum and the determination of the coupling constant; (ii) the verification of the stability of the configuration at the times ≫ t_ev ≈ 1.2×10¹⁰ years (7.5); (iii) the verification of the absence of forbidding gravitational effects at the scale of the clusters — local densities by five orders above the cosmological one require a special consideration; (iv) the verification of the lower bound of τ_s(R): it must not exceed the dynamic times of the structural events — a direct constraint from the Bullet Cluster (7.9). Until the performance of (i)–(iv), the line remains a hypothesis; its distinguishing feature — concreteness: it is the only one of the considered ones that sets a particle and a window of parameters reconciled with the known bounds.

## 7.3. The cyclic source of the geometry: the baryonic subsystem as the generator of the local dynamics

At the basis of the model lies the standard relation of the general theory of relativity: the distribution of the energy-momentum of the baryonic matter determines the local geometry of spacetime through the Einstein equations. The specific statement of the hypothesis concerns not this relation but the character of the evolution of the source: the baryonic subsystem is considered not as a static distribution in a ready geometry but as a participant of the cycle "accumulation — breakdown — stabilization". The accumulation corresponds to a quasi-stationary regime in which the geometry evolves slowly; the breakdown — to an episodic discharge of the accumulated energy, in which the geometry undergoes a rapid restructuring; the stabilization — to a relaxation of the new configuration toward a quasi-equilibrium.

Such a source generates not a static geometry but a sequence of quasi-equilibrium states separated by transitions. Precisely the episodicity of the source carries the structural load in the model: it sets the pulsational regime of the vacuum component (6.4, 6.6), explains the scale periodicity of the large structures and determines the profile of the regulation through the regime parameter q (6.8.7). The connection with the formalization: the baryonic cycle is the source S_b(t) of the trigger equation (7.1) and, through the mechanism of the switching of the configurations (7.2), determines which configuration of the vacuum component is realized in the given domain.

The formulation "the creation of space", used in the working materials, is replaced by a precise one: the model does not describe the generation of spacetime — it describes the control of its local geometry by a cyclic source. The question of the origin of spacetime itself lies beyond the limits of the model.

## 7.4. The problem of the scale: from the cosmological density to the galactic vacuum component

The cosmological density of dark energy amounts to ~6×10⁻³⁰ g/cm³, whereas the density of the vacuum component at the galactic scales, required by the fit of the rotation curves, is ~10⁻²⁵ g/cm³ (6.8.7). The gap amounts to about five orders of magnitude. In the proposed picture, this gap differs in principle from the classical problem of the cosmological constant: there, the discrepancy between the theoretical estimate and the observation reaches ~10¹²³ orders of magnitude and has no proposed mechanism; here, the model replaces the universal constant by a local parameter of the galaxy, and there remains to explain a finite amplification at ~10⁵. Three mechanisms of amplification are proposed below; they are considered as mutually complementary, not alternative.

**The gravitational polarization.** If the field is connected with the local gravitational potential, the position of the effective minimum (7.2) depends on the depth of the well: the density of the vacuum component rises near the baryonic concentrations and falls toward the periphery. The signature of the mechanism — a correlation of ρ_0 with a characteristic of the potential: the measured trend ρ_0 ∝ V_max^(−1.3) (6.8.7) is subject to a verification by the per-object parameters of the fit. (In the previous edition there stood the exponent −0.9, derived from the former scaling of α; the canonical computation 6.8.4–6.8.7 gives −1.3.) The mechanism sets the form of the profile — an exponential one with the scale α — and simultaneously explains why ρ_0 is a parameter of the galaxy.

**The accumulation from the cosmological background (the "axion ocean").** The cosmological background of the axion-like field with the parameters of 7.2 acts as a reservoir: the cycle "accumulation — breakdown — stabilization" extracts the energy from the background locally at the scale t_ev ~ 1.2×10¹⁰ years (6.2, 6.6). A verification of the conservation of energy: for an accumulation of ~10⁻²⁵ g/cm³ at a mean background of ~10⁻³⁰ g/cm³, a depletion of a domain of a volume of ~10⁵ is required — in radius this is a factor of ~50, that is, a depletion within several hundred kpc, which by the order of magnitude is compatible with the scales of groups of galaxies. The estimate is a rough one and is subject to a quantitative development; its purpose is to show that the mechanism does not require exotic sources of energy.

**The cascade amplification along the links of the chain.** The pulsational regime (6.6) sets the frequency of the events: τ_pulse ~ L/c_vac ~ 10⁵–10⁷ years per link of the galactic scale. Over the time t_ev, the chain realizes N ~ 10³–10⁵ events; at an extraction of ~10⁻³⁰ g/cm³ per event (of the order of the cosmological background), the total accumulation reaches the required ~10⁻²⁵ g/cm³. Significantly, the estimate closes upon itself: the number of the events and the single portion of the extraction are reconciled with the background without fitting parameters — if the background is by orders lower, the cascade does not manage; if it is substantially higher, the amplification is excessive. Thereby the required frequency of the events becomes a checkable consequence.

**The demarcation of the roles:** the gravitational polarization sets the spatial form and the dependence on the mass; the axion ocean — the reservoir and the energetic budget; the cascade — the temporal dynamics and the connection with the episodicity of the outbursts (6.4). The program of verification includes: (i) the trend ρ_0–V_max by the per-object parameters (in the queue of the verifications); (ii) the dependence of the density of the vacuum component on the environment (the voids against the filaments — a connection with 6.4–6.5); (iii) a reconciliation of the total extraction with the observed surface density of the baryons and of the background at the scales of the groups.

## 7.5. The stability of the vacuum component

In the proposed model, dark matter is not a population of particles but a state of the medium (6.8.7, 7.2): the observed dark component is realized as a configuration of the axion-like field — a coherent condensate with a dust gravitational response. From this follows the character of the observational predictions distinguishing the model from the family of the models of decaying dark matter: the particles of dark matter, the products of its decay and the signals of annihilation are absent in the model by construction. The checkable content of this statement — a totality of null signals: the absence of monoenergetic lines, of continuous radiation from the decay and of the annihilation channels in the domains of a high density of the component (the centers of galaxies, the dwarf satellites). The accumulated constraints of such searches the model perceives as an agreement, not as the nature of a failure; a newly detected decay or annihilation signal without accompanying baryonic effects would be forbidding for the model.

The stability of the configuration is provided not by an external stabilizing field but by the structure of the effective potential: the field is held in a local minimum, and the transition into a lower state ("the decay of the false vacuum") requires overcoming a barrier. The observed stability of the vacuum component at the cosmological times sets a lower bound of the height of the barrier: the lifetime τ_vac must satisfy the condition τ_vac ≫ t_ev ≈ 1.2×10¹⁰ years (6.2) and exceed the age of the Universe. In the terms of 7.2, this is a constraint on the coupling constant and on the depth of the baryon-dependent minimum — the first quantitative criterion of the program of concretization (7.2, item ii): any micromodel of the mechanism giving a τ_vac below the cosmological level is excluded without an appeal to the observations.

Separately, we fix the status of the decline. The "decline" of the dark component in the model (0.6.2, 2.7) is not a decay into particles but a passive relaxation of the configuration: upon the weakening of the feeding or the loss of the baryonic substrate, the condensate disperses within the time τ_s(R), returning the energy into the medium (7.10). The observational consequence — the absence of radiation at the decline; this requires a synchronizing edit of the prediction 5.6 (see the list of changes): its discriminating status is retained — a detection of an uncorrelated line refutes the model, but the expectation changes from a signal to a null signal.

Separately, we note the status of the statement on the metastability. An admissible extreme form remains: the configuration may be metastable with a lifetime by far exceeding the age of the Universe — then the decay remains possible in principle but unobservable at the accessible times. The observational consequences of both variants (the absolute stability and the "slow" metastability) at the current times coincide; the difference belongs to the domain of theory, not of observations.

## 7.6. The mediator field: the candidate and the roles

The function of the connection between the gravitational behavior of the vacuum component and the quantum dynamics of its micro-field carrier, the model ascribes to a single mediator — the axion-like field of 7.2 — without introducing a separate unknown field. The basis: precisely this field already answers for both ends of the required connection — for the gravitational side (a localized medium with a dust gravitational response, 6.8.7) and for the quantum one (a pseudoscalar field with the parameters m_a ~ 10⁻⁵ eV, f_a ~ 10¹² GeV, 7.2). To postulate a third carrier beyond it would mean a doubling of the entities without added predictions; the question of the existence of the mediator field is thereby not removed but translated into the status of a checkable consequence: if an effect is found requiring a carrier beyond the axion-like field, the model will be extended precisely in this direction.

In the terms of the cycle "accumulation — breakdown — stabilization" (7.1), the mediator performs three distinguishable roles corresponding to the three phases.

**The field of the transition** — the microscopic carrier of the event of the breakdown: a transition of the configuration of the field between the local minima of the effective potential (7.2) within the time τ_br. The observational projection — the episodicity of the baryonic outbursts and its connection with the scale (6.4, 6.6).

**The field of the barrier** — the carrier of the barrier determining the stability of the configuration: the height of the barrier sets the lower bound of the lifetime τ_vac ≫ t_ev ≈ 1.2×10¹⁰ years (7.5, item ii of the program of 7.2). The observational projection — the totality of the null signals of the decay and the annihilation (7.5).

**The field of the memory** — the carrier of the information about the accumulated state: a correspondence between the degree of the accumulation and the position of the configuration in the potential, making the next breakdown dependent on the prehistory. In the formalism of 7.1, this role is played by the trigger variable Φ(t); the microscopic candidate remains the slow relaxation of the configuration of the field along the potential. The observational projection — the regularity of the pulsational regime: the reproducibility of the intervals τ_pulse between the events in a given galaxy (6.6).

**The boundaries of the statement.** The model does not claim a solution of the problem of quantum gravity: the connection "gravity — quantum dynamics" is discussed only in a narrow sense — as a dependence of the effective potential of the field (and, consequently, of the position of its minimum and the height of the barrier) on the local gravitational potential and the baryonic density (7.2). All the three roles are reduced to this single relation, which makes the hypothesis economical: one mechanism — three functions — three observational projections, each of which enters the program of verification (6.4, 6.6, 7.5).

## 7.7. The evolution of the ratio of the components with the age of the region

The model predicts a growth of the ratio of the vacuum component to the baryonic mass with the age of the region: d/dt·ln(M_vac/M_b) = 1/t_p − 1/t_DM > 0 (7.1), with a characteristic scale t_ev ≈ 1.2×10¹⁰ years fixed by the evolution of the scale parameter of the RAR (6.2, 6.6; the reproduction of the RAR is conditional, 6.8.6). The observational formulation of the prediction: in the late phases of the cycle (low redshifts, the scale of 0.6.5), the ratio of the components is higher. The mechanism of the effect is not connected with the decay of the dark component (7.5) and is composed of two independent terms. The numerator grows due to the cyclic accumulation: the breakdowns occur more often than is required for a complete balancing of the accumulation (t_p < t_DM; the sign is fixed by the discriminating test). The denominator slowly decreases due to the return of the mass by the stellar population: in the old populations, the mass return reaches 0.3–0.5 over the cosmological time, which gives a decrease of M_b by tens of percent over the scale t_ev and contributes a positive term to the trend of the ratio independently of the dynamics of the numerator. Separately, we fix the status of the proton decay. The model does not require a fundamental decay of the proton: the experimental bounds τ_p > 10³⁴ years exceed the considered times by 24 orders of magnitude, and this channel does not enter the dynamics of the ratio. The term "the destruction of protons" from the working materials corresponds in the model to the return of the mass by the stellar evolution and to the astrophysical destruction (7.11) — observable processes, not a hypothetical decay. The verification of the prediction does not require new data. The discriminating test — the dependence of the RAR residuals on the age of the stellar population: at a fixed baryonic mass and depth of the potential, the old populations (the elliptical galaxies, the bulges, the objects with a high mass-weighted age) must show a systematically larger ratio of the components than the young ones. The second projection — a comparison of the galaxies of equal mass at different stages of the evolution (the early against the late types) for a systematic shift of the RAR residuals. Both tests are formulated on the already fitted sample (6.8.3) and are added to the program of verification.

## 7.8. Black holes as a node of the cycle

In the proposed model, the black hole performs the role of a node of the synchronization of the cycle "accumulation — breakdown — stabilization", not of an independent source of energy. The accretion corresponds to the phase of accumulation: matter and energy are concentrated in the vicinity of the hole; the discharge through the jets and the radio modes (6.4) — to the event of the breakdown; the relaxation of the gaseous subsystem — to the phase of stabilization. Thereby, the episodicity of the active nuclei (6.4) and the pulsational regime (6.6) receive a single carrier: the baryonic source S_b(t) of the trigger equation (7.1) is realized by the accretion cycle of the central supermassive hole, and the observed connection of the AGN activity with the morphology of the galaxy — a manifestation of the regime parameter q (7.1, 6.8.7).

**A quantitative estimate of the energetic share.** The total energy recycled by the hole over the time t_ev ≈ 1.2×10¹⁰ years is bounded from above by the radiation efficiency of the accretion: at a growth of the hole to M_BH ~ 10⁷–10⁸ M_⊙ and η ~ 0.1, the total recycled energy amounts to the order of M_BH·c². The mass of the vacuum component of the galaxy, by the profile of 6.8.1 (M_∞ = 8π·ρ_0·α³) with the canonical medians of the fit (ρ_0 ~ 10⁷ M_⊙/kpc³, α ~ 2.5–5 kpc), equals ~4×10⁹–3×10¹⁰ M_⊙, that is, its energetic equivalent is of the same order in the units of M_⊙c². The ratio amounts to ~0.002–0.02: the energy returned through the holes is a subdominant contribution to the energetics of the vacuum component; the main reservoir is the cosmological background (7.4, the "axion ocean"). (In the previous edition, the share was estimated at 0.03–0.3 due to an underestimated mass of the component; the canonical medians of 6.8.4/6.8.7 raise it — the conclusion on the trigger, not source role of the hole is strengthened.) The coherence is important: the estimate is obtained from independent quantities (the mass of the hole from the M_BH–σ relation, the mass of the component from the fit of 6.8) and requires no fitting.

**The consequences and the program of verification.** The role of a trigger, not of a source, means a checkable separation of the functions: the temporal structure of the pulsations (τ_pulse, 6.6) must trace the accretion dynamics of the hole (the duty cycle, the Kerr/Eddington times), whereas the amplitude of the profile of the vacuum component is determined by the reservoir mechanism of 7.4 and the potential (7.2), not by the power of the outburst. The verification: (i) a correlation of the parameters of the fit (V0, α) with the mass of the central hole over the sample of 6.8.3 — the prediction of the presence of such a correlation follows from the M_BH–σ relation and the connection with the potential; (ii) a comparison of the kinetic energy of the jets with the energetic deficit between the events of the breakdown; (iii) the objects without a significant AGN trace (the dwarfs with small holes) must demonstrate a breakdown by density without a pronounced episodicity — a reconciliation with the fork of the regimes of 6.8.7.

## 7.9. The collisions of the clusters and the dwarf cores

**The Bullet Cluster (1E 0657-56).** The classical observation of a collision of clusters: the lensing peak of the mass passes through the collision without a noticeable braking, being displaced from the hot gas toward the stellar components, and the constraints on the self-interaction of the dark component give σ/m ≲ 1 cm²/g. In the proposed model, there is no dark component with particles; its role is played by the vacuum medium (6.8.7). The collisionlessness in this picture is not a postulate and not a consequence of an equation of state but a property of the carrier: the configuration of a coherent field experiences no dissipative self-interaction and is not braked by the gas — while its gravitational response is dust-like (w ≈ 0), which ensures a correct dynamics of the mass peak. Significantly, the model agrees not only with the fact of the passage but also with the detail: the displacement of the mass peak toward the stellar components. The position of the minimum of the vacuum component is determined by the local gravitational potential (7.2, 7.4), and at the collision the potential follows the stellar subsystems that have passed through — the configuration relaxes toward the new position within the time τ_s(R), whereas the gas is separated by the braking. The observed geometry (the lensing peak at the stars, the gas behind) is reproduced as a consequence, without additional parameters.

This observation gives the model the first numerical constraint on the time of relaxation: the mass peak follows the galaxies already at the stage of the contact (the time of the passage ~10⁸ years); hence, τ_s(R) ≲ 10⁸ years for the clusters — a rigid lower frame on the mechanism of 7.1, incompatible with a "slow" relaxation over billions of years. The constraint enters the program of verification (7.2, item iv) and the criteria of falsifiability: if the micromodel of the potential requires τ_s > 10⁸ years, the scenario is refuted by this single observation.

A quantitative verification — a numerical simulation of a merger with a relaxing potential-dependent configuration: a comparison of the maps of the lensing and the profile of the gas at the stages before and after the contact. The test is formulated as an open one (6.7).

**The dwarf galaxies.** The smoothed core in the dwarfs follows from the regime of the breakdown by density (6.8.7): at q ≫ 1, the discharge in the cycle proceeds slowly and uniformly, and the stationary profile of the vacuum component ρ_0·exp(−R/α) (6.8.1) is realized with a flat center — precisely that form which the observations fix as a core. The elevated ratio of the vacuum component to the baryonic one in the dwarfs — a reconciled consequence of the scaling: α grows with V_max (6.8.4), ρ_0 decreases (7.4), while the relative contribution of the component into the deep part of the rotation curve in the low-mass systems is larger — this enters the fit of 6.8.3 and does not require an additional mechanics. Separately, we fix: the dynamic connection with the "destruction of protons" and the decline of the dark component is not invoked (7.5, 7.7); the age trend of the ratio of the components (7.7) acts in all the systems identically and is not a specificity of the dwarfs.

## 7.10. The connection with the existing theoretical approaches

The regulatory model intersects with several known directions but differs in the key mechanisms.

**Decaying dark matter.** In these models, dark matter is unstable: the decay is an independent process with a set channel and lifetime, and the products of the decay are an emission of a fixed set of particles. In the regulatory model, dark matter is a state of the medium (7.5), and the vacuum component is stable at the cosmological times (τ_vac ≫ t_ev, 7.5). The nearest analog of the "decline" in the model is the relaxation of the local configuration toward the cosmological background upon the disappearance of the baryonic substrate: since the position of the minimum of the potential depends on the local baryonic density (7.2), the loss of the substrate removes the retention, and the configuration disperses within the time τ_s(R) (7.1), generating no particles. The observational difference is fundamental: the decaying DM predicts the signals of a decay (γ-lines, leptonic channels); the model predicts their absence (7.5) and simultaneously the "decline" of the component only in the domains emptied of baryons — a discriminating test between the approaches.

**Quintessence and dynamic dark energy.** The similarity: dark energy is not a constant. The difference in the origin and the inhomogeneity: in the quintessence, a change of Λ is a consequence of the evolution of a single scalar field at the cosmological level; in the model, the local density of the vacuum component is formed in the phase of stabilization of the cycle (4.4, 4.6) and is a parameter of the concrete system (6.8.7): it accumulates after the breakdown (7.1) and is held by the potential (7.2). From this, a prediction of the spatial inhomogeneity of the local vacuum component (6.8.7, 7.4) — traceable by the trend ρ_0–V_max and by the dependence on the environment, the voids against the filaments (7.4). (The reference to 5.3 in the previous edition is removed: 5.3 refers to the uniform dark energy, which is by definition not inhomogeneous; the inhomogeneous entity of the model is the local vacuum component.) The uniform dark energy (1.1, variant A) thereby remains homogeneous and inherits the cosmological role of the standard Λ (7.12).

**The axion models of the early Universe.** The common: a light pseudoscalar field and the idea of a condensate state. The difference in the function of the field: in the standard axion scenarios, the accent is the generation of the density of dark matter and the solution of the problem of the strong CP symmetry; the model does not claim a solution of the CP problem and uses the field functionally — as the carrier of the baryon-dependent minimum (7.2), the reservoir of the "axion ocean" (4.2, 7.4) and the mediator with three roles (7.6). The smoothing of the peaks at the breakdowns in the model is provided by the regime of the breakdown by density (6.8.7), not by the properties of the condensate.

**Cyclic universes (Steinhardt–Turok).** The similarity: the Universe passes through repeated cycles. The difference in the mechanics: in Steinhardt–Turok, the cycles are set by the collisions of branes within the M-theory; in the model — by the internal pulsations of the vacuum component and the life cycles of matter. The singularity in the model is absent: the transition proceeds through the maximal breakthrough, not through a compression to a point (0.6.4). The status of the statement: it refers to the cosmological contour of the model (0.6.4) and is used as a qualitative frame; a quantitative development of the global cycle is beyond the limits of the current stage of the hypothesis; specific observational signals are not presented by this contour at the current level.

Thus, the model borrows individual ideas (light scalar condensates, cyclicality, the dynamical nature of dark energy) but combines them into a different mechanism, in which the gravitational field and the baryonic substrate act as the regulators of the cycle, and the checkable content is transferred onto local, measurable quantities: the profiles of the rotation curves (6.8), the trend ρ_0–V_max (6.8.4), the totality of the null signals of the decay and the annihilation (7.5) and the episodicity of the baryonic outbursts (6.4).

## 7.11. The destruction of baryons in extreme conditions: a quantitative estimate

The direct laboratory constraints on the spontaneous decay of the proton — τ_p > 10³⁴ years for the channel p → e⁺ + π⁰ (Super-Kamiokande) — refer to an intranuclear decay without an external action and are not applicable to the dynamic destruction of the baryons in astrophysical processes. In the model, the destruction of the baryons is understood precisely as an astrophysical one and is concentrated in the nodes of the cycle (7.8): the accretion by the black holes, the tidal destruction of the stars, the fragmentation in the cosmic rays of ultra-high energies.

**A quantitative estimate of the total contribution.** The dominant channel — the accretion: the integral density of the supermassive black holes, reconstructed from the cosmological luminosity of the AGN (the Soltan argument), amounts to ~4×10⁵ M_⊙/Mpc³, which corresponds to accreted ~5×10⁵ M_⊙/Mpc³ of baryons. At the cosmological density of the baryons ~6.7×10⁹ M_⊙/Mpc³, the share of the baryons that have passed through the horizon over the whole history amounts to ~10⁻⁴. The tidal destruction of the stars contributes an amount of the same order or less (a frequency of ~10⁻⁵–10⁻⁴ per galaxy per year at ~0.5 M_⊙ per event); moreover, the tidally destroyed object is in the main mass accreted — the channel is not independent. The fragmentation in the cosmic rays of ultra-high energies is negligible: the energy density of these particles is by 8–9 orders below the stellar one.

In total, the destruction covers ≲10⁻³ of the baryonic density over the cosmological time — by two-three orders less than the effect of the return of the mass by the stellar population (0.3–0.5 over t_ev, 7.7). From this, the status of the channel in the model: the astrophysical destruction of the baryons is a real, observationally constrained process but a subdominant one for the evolution of the ratio of the components (7.7): the bearing mechanism of the decrease of the baryonic mass at the scale t_ev remains the return of the mass, not the destruction. Energetically, the accretion channel works in the cycle as a trigger and a source of the outbursts (7.8): the recycled ~10⁻⁴ of the baryonic density provides the observed luminosity of the AGN and the feedback (6.4), without claiming the role of the main source of the energetics of the vacuum component (7.4).

The absence of a conflict with the laboratory bounds follows from the separation: the spontaneous decay is forbidden by the observations at the level τ_p > 10³⁴ years and is not invoked by the model; the astrophysical destruction is not a decay but a recycling of matter in the gravitational nodes, and its intensity is set by the observed luminosity of the AGN, not by a microscopic lifetime.

## 7.12. The cosmological constraints: CMB, BAO, SNe Ia

**The general principle.** The cosmological observations measure the volume average; the local vacuum component enters them through the share of the volume occupied by the halos. The mean density of the component equals ρ̅ ≈ ρ_local·f, where f is the filling fraction of the volume. At ρ_local ~ 10⁻²⁵ g/cm³ (6.8.7) and the density of the dark component of the cosmology ρ_DM ≈ 2.4×10⁻³⁰ g/cm³, the required share amounts to f ~ 2×10⁻⁵. An independent estimate through the number of the galaxies and the radii of the halos (n ~ 10⁻² Mpc⁻³, R ~ 100 kpc) gives f ~ 4×10⁻⁵ — a reconciliation at the level of a factor of the order of two without a fitting. Thus, the local component reproducing the rotation curves automatically reaches by its volume average the cosmological density of the dark component: this is a coherence of the model, not its verification, but its absence would be a prohibition. From this also follows the key expectation: the standard cosmological tests the model passes as a ΛCDM with small additions, and their absence is an agreement.

**The CMB.** The cosmological background (the "axion ocean", 7.4) plays the role of the standard dark energy: the acoustic peaks and the relic spectrum the model does not modify — at the early times, the energy of the vacuum type is dynamically negligible both in the standard cosmology and in the model. A possible contribution of the local component into the low multipoles proceeds through the integral Sachs–Wolfe effect at the late times: the formation of the component in the halos (the phase of stabilization, 4.4) adds to the isotropic Λ an inhomogeneous one, correlated with the large-scale structure, an addition — the signal is expected at the level of percent and is statistically indistinguishable from the standard scatter of the low multipoles. The discriminating test — the sign and the angular correlation of the residuals of the low multipoles with the map of the large structures; the test is formulated as an open one (6.7).

**The BAO.** The scale ruler is imprinted in the relic radiation at the recombination, when the local vacuum component is not yet formed; its later accumulation does not shift the comoving scale. The model predicts a standard BAO without regional variations — and thereby shares the fate of the cosmological background and the local component: the former passes the CMB and the BAO as the standard dark energy, the latter is verified by the rotation curves and the large-scale dynamics (6.4–6.5). The absence of the deviations of the BAO is an agreement of the model, not a test with a discriminating strength.

**SNe Ia.** The spatial inhomogeneity of the local vacuum component (6.8.7, 7.4) gives an observational projection not as a shift of the cosmological relation but as a dependence of the standardized brightness of the supernovae on the environment. Significantly, such a dependence is already observed: the step of the Hubble residuals by the stellar mass of the host ("mass step") amounts to 0.04–0.1 magnitudes; the supernovae in the massive, old, passive galaxies are brighter; in the locally active environments, the step is weaker (by the data of the literature, ~0.08 against ~0.04 magnitudes; the values require a precise tying to the publications before the final version). This agrees with the trend of 7.7: the late phases — a higher share of the vacuum component — a deeper potential. The discriminating test between the model and the standard interpretations of the step (the evolution of the populations, the dust): the correlation of the step is not with the mass as such but with the independent proxies of the local density of the vacuum component — the age of the stellar population (7.7) and the RAR residuals (6.8.3); the prediction of the model — the step grows with the age and the depth of the potential at a fixed mass. The test is feasible on the existing samples (Pantheon+, DES) and is added to the program of verification.

**The status of the section:** the CMB and the BAO — a coherence (the model must not violate them and does not); the SNe Ia — the only quantitative anchor with a discriminating test on this ground; the amplitudes of all the expected deviations are of the second order of smallness, which agrees with the observed closeness of the cosmology to ΛCDM.

## 7.13. The phenomenology of the axion ocean: the constants and the observational consequences

**The mass.** The range of the candidate is fixed not arbitrarily but by the standard bounds on f_a: the lower bound f_a ≳ 10⁹ GeV (the cosmological constraint on the overproduction) gives m_a ≲ 6 meV; the upper bound f_a ≲ 10¹² GeV (the cooling of the stars) gives m_a ≳ 6 μeV within the QCD connection m_a·f_a ≈ const. An independent constraint by the cooling of the neutron stars (m_a ≲ 15–30 meV) does not narrow it. The working window — m_a ≈ 6 μeV – 6 meV with a benchmark of 10⁻⁵ eV (7.2). The ultra-light branch (m_a ~ 10⁻²² eV, a de Broglie length in kpc) is a different regime, approaching the scalar dark matter; as the main branch of the model it is not considered but is not excluded as an alternative. The required displacement of the minimum after the conversion — θ ≈ 4×10⁻¹⁹ (7.2) — creates no requirement of a tuning: the magnitude follows from the density and the fixed product m_a·f_a.

**The coupling constant to the gravitational field.** In the model, this is not a separate constant but the potential-dependence of the position of the minimum of the effective potential (7.2): the dependence of the density on the local gravitational potential and the baryonic density sets also the threshold of the switching. The role of the "threshold of the switching" from the working materials is played by the trigger variable Φ_c (7.1); the term "the switching of spins" is replaced by "the switching of configurations" (7.2, 7.6). To be fixed are the quantities — the coupling constants of the field with the baryonic substrate and with the photons/nucleons: in the model, they are free parameters (7.2), since the model does not solve the CP problem (7.10); the criteria i–iv of the program of 7.2 and the condition τ_vac ≫ t_ev from 7.5 — the first numerical criteria.

**The time of coherence.** The microscopic time of coherence of a free field — the period of the oscillations T = 2π/m_a ~ 10⁻¹² s — does not set the dynamics of the discharge: in the model, the rate of the breakdown is determined by the time of the transition τ_br and the time of the relaxation τ_s(R) (7.1). The spatial coherence is large: at m_a ~ 10⁻⁵ eV and the velocities v ~ 10⁻³c, the de Broglie length amounts to ~0.1–1 m — the condensate behaves as a classical coherent field without a noticeable granularity; this removes the possible objections about a substructure of the "ocean". (In the previous edition there stood "tens of meters" — an error of the estimate by two orders; corrected.)

**The observational consequences.** The status of each channel below is formulated through the free couplings of the program of concretization: the values of the couplings are parameters of the model, not fixed numbers; a channel becomes a discriminating test when the estimate of the coupling exceeds the observational bound.

(i) **The cooling of the neutron stars.** If the couplings with the nucleons lie below the observational bound (by the data of the literature, |g_ann| ≲ 10⁻⁹–10⁻¹⁰ GeV⁻¹; the exact value requires a tying to the publications), the model predicts no anomalous accelerated cooling, and its absence is an agreement. A detected significant excess of the bound fixes the coupling with the nucleons — a refining but not a forbidding factor for the vacuum configuration: the coupling concerns the micromodel of the potential (item i of the program of 7.2).

(ii) **The X-ray channel.** The known excess of the hard X-ray at the nearby isolated neutron stars ("the Magnificent Seven") admits an axion interpretation at m_a ≲ 2×10⁻⁵ eV — the masses coincide with the lower part of our window — but the required products of the couplings g_γγ·g_ann are by several orders above those predicted by the standard QCD axion relations at f_a ~ 10¹² GeV. The model predicts the absence of the X-ray excess as an axion signal; if the excess is confirmed as an axionic one, this will point to the couplings above the reference estimate and will require a revision of the coupling constants (item i of the program of 7.2) — the discriminating status is retained. The status of the excess itself is disputed: the signal for the individual objects does not exceed ~5σ, for a part of the Seven it is absent altogether, which leaves a place also for a non-axionic interpretation.

(iii) **The radio line and the radio echo.** The direct decay and the stimulated decays in the field of the bright radio sources give a line at ν ~ 1.2 GHz for the benchmark m_a ~ 10⁻⁵ eV; the existing constraints by the radio excess in the clusters and the prospective searches for the radio echo (SKA) lie by several orders above the reference coupling g_γγ ~ 10⁻¹⁵ GeV⁻¹ — a signal in the model is not expected, and its non-detection is a concordance.

(iv) **The deviations in the relic radiation** — percent in the low multipoles through the integral effect at the late times; considered in 7.12 and taken out there into a separate test.

The program of concretization: the determination of the coupling constants with the baryonic substrate (the main open item, 7.2); the refinement of the window of the masses as the precision of the constraints on the cooling grows; the verification of the statistical significance of the excess of "the Magnificent Seven" by the archival data.

## 7.14. The problem of fine-tuning

The character of the tuning in the present model is fundamentally different from that in ΛCDM. The standard cosmology contains a constant Λ whose value relative to the natural Planck scale diverges from the observed one by ~10¹²⁰ orders of magnitude, and contains no mechanism translating this gap into a finite task. In the present model, the universal constant is absent by construction: the density of the vacuum component is a parameter of the concrete system (6.8.7), and the fitting is reduced to finite, physically motivated quantities.

**A quantitative formulation.** The requirements of the model to the "tuning" are reduced to three groups of quantities.

**The thresholds of the trigger relation** (Φ_c and the related thresholds (7.1); in the working materials — ρ_crit, P_crit, T_crit). Their status — parameters of the trigger, subject to a determination from the properties of the field and the observations; the scale of these quantities is set by the astrophysical processes themselves (the densities and the temperatures in the nodes of the cycle), and a requirement of a tuning at the level of many orders does not arise.

**The scales of the dynamics:** the velocity of the front c_vac (6.6), the times of the phases τ_br, τ_s(R) (7.1, including the constraint τ_s ≲ 10⁸ years from 7.9), the lifetime of the configuration τ_vac (7.5). For the latter, only an inequality is required, τ_vac ≫ t_ev ≈ 1.2×10¹⁰ years — a constraint-inequality, not a selection of an exact value; the order of c_vac is reconciled with the observed episodicity (6.6).

**The constants of the axion-like field:** the relation m_a·f_a gives the pairs (m_a, f_a) from the standard window (7.13); the required displacement of the minimum θ ≈ 4×10⁻¹⁹ (7.2) does not require a tuning, since it follows from the density and the fixed product m_a·f_a. The gap of the local amplification of ~10⁵ (7.4) is closed by three mechanisms with independently fixed frequencies and portions, without free parameters.

**An essential caveat.** A part of the structure the model postulates rather than derives: the form of the effective potential with a baryon-dependent minimum and a barrier (7.2). As long as the coupling constant with the baryonic substrate is not constructed microscopically, the model remains phenomenological — in the same sense in which the ΛCDM is phenomenological in the part of the origin of Λ. The difference is that the phenomenology here is localized in one element (the potential of the field) and is supplied with a program of concretization (7.2, items i–iv) and a numerical criterion (τ_vac ≫ t_ev), whereas in the ΛCDM it is the observed quantity itself that remains unexplained.

The perspective of a complete solution — the construction of a microscopic theory of the potential, not the introduction of an additional field: the role of the mediator is performed by the axion-like field itself (7.6), and an extension of the set of the fields is justified only upon a detection of the effects requiring it (7.6). The question of the origin of the potential is fixed as open.

# Section 8. Conclusions

1. **The empirical core.** The RMEP profile describes the rotation curves of 175 disk galaxies of SPARC (3391 points) better than NFW at an equal number of free parameters (two each): wins — 61.1% (107 galaxies), a statistical tie — 21.1% (37), wins of NFW — 17.7% (31); "better or equal" — 82.3% of the sample (Section 6.8, a computation under a fixed protocol, the per-object table appended). The advantage is distributed over the masses according to the predicted discharge-dependence: in the domain of low rotation velocities (V_max < 90 km/s), the RMEP wins with a persistent separation; in the massive spirals with steep inner curves, the best descriptions are given by NFW — precisely as the regime fork of q requires (7.1, 6.8.7).

2. **The connection of the scale of regulation with the baryonic state of the system is a working assumption of the model:** the main run gives α ≈ 5 kpc·(V_max/100 km/s)^1.89 (a 95% bootstrap interval of the exponent [1.34; 2.41]; a Spearman ρ = 0.66; formally α ∝ M_bary^0.47), however an independent recomputation does not reproduce this form of the dependence (k = 0.05; a Spearman of 0.04; 6.8.4) — until the reconciliation of the protocols, the connection is treated as a working assumption, not an established result. The density of the regulation ρ_0 = V0²/(8πG·α²) is a derived quantity, a parameter of the concrete galaxy, not a universal constant (6.8.7); the direction of the trend ρ_0–V_max — an open reconciliation of the protocols: the independent recomputation gives the opposite sign (6.8.7).

3. **The solution of the "cusp–core" problem is structural.** The exponential profile gives in the center the exact density of a homogeneous sphere (a flat core — an exact property of the profile, 6.8.1), reproducing the rotation curves of the dwarfs without feedback mechanisms; at the same time, the finite total mass M_∞ = 8πρ_0α³ predicts a Keplerian segment at the edge of the halo — a prediction qualitatively different from that of NFW, applicable to 72 objects of the sample (6.8.8). Both characteristics follow from a single form of the profile without additional assumptions.

4. **A unified frame of the dark sectors with separate roles.** The observed dark component of the galaxies — the local vacuum component: the energy of the medium in the form of potential states (1.1), gravitationally dust-like (w ≈ 0), held by the baryon-dependent minimum of the axion-like field (7.2). The uniform dark energy — a separate locked component with its own dynamics (1.1, 3.5, 4.4). The model does not mix them: the cosmological background inherits the role of the standard Λ (7.12), the local regulation describes the rotation curves (6.8) — and the volume average of the former automatically reaches the cosmological density of the dark component (a coherence of f ~ 2–4×10⁻⁵, 7.12).

5. **A program of verification with criteria of falsifiability — at three levels.** The galactic one: the trend ρ_0–V_max by the per-object parameters (7.4), the age test by the RAR residuals (7.7), a correlation of (V0, α) with the mass of the central black hole (7.8), the Keplerian segment (6.8.8). The astrophysical one: the mass step of the supernovae Ia against the age and the depth of the potential (7.12), the X-ray excess of "the Magnificent Seven" as a discriminating test of the couplings (7.13), the totality of the null signals of the decay and the annihilation (7.5), the constraint τ_s ≲ 10⁸ years from the Bullet Cluster (7.9). The cosmological one: the evolution of the ratio of the dark energy to the dark matter with the cosmological time (4.6), at a strict invariability of both densities — a refutation (5.3). Each criterion is independent; the model is obliged, by deviations of such a set, to differ from ΛCDM only in the predicted places.

6. **The theoretical contour.** The energy of empty space → gravitational regulation (1.3) → the regulatory cycle (accumulation → breakdown → stabilization → the return of energy; Section 2) → the pulsational dynamics and the wave mechanics (Section 3) → the microphysics of the baryon-dependent minimum (7.2, 7.6) → the evolution of the ratio of the components at the scale t_ev ≈ 1.2×10¹⁰ years (7.1, 7.7) → the maximal breakthrough and the closing of the cycle (0.6.4, 3.2). The fine-tuning is reduced to finite quantities: the inequality τ_vac ≫ t_ev, the trigger thresholds and the constants of the axion-like field with a displacement of the minimum of θ ≈ 4×10⁻¹⁹, requiring no tuning (7.14); the phenomenological remainder — the form of the potential — is localized in a single element and is supplied with a program of concretization.

7. **The status of the open.** The unsolved principal tasks of the hypothesis: (a) the transition of the description of gravity from the status "a property of the medium" (1.3) to the Einstein equations at the birth of mass — the main unclosed bridge of the model; (b) a strict connection of the primary spectrum of the perturbations of the relic radiation with the mechanism of the global breakdown (3.9); (c) a microscopic theory of the baryon-dependent potential (7.2); (d) a quantitative description of the evolution of Λ_eff(z) — the subject of the planned cosmological sections (9–11). All the listed tasks are supplied in the text of the hypothesis with programs of verification and do not belong to the number of assumptions without which the model collapses.

# Section 9. The connection of the RMEP with relativistic gravity

## 9.1. The covariant formulation of the model

In the original formulation (Section 6), the regulatory model uses the Newtonian approximation: the contribution of the vacuum component is introduced as an additional term in the circular velocity. For an application of the model in strong gravitational fields and at cosmological scales, a relativistic formulation is required.

**The covariant formulation of the RMEP** — the standard Einstein equations with an additional dynamic source:

G_μν + Λ·g_μν = (8πG/c⁴)·[T_μν^baryon + T_μν^φ],

where φ is the axion-like field (7.2) with an effective potential V_eff(φ; ρ_b, Φ_grav), depending on the local baryonic density and the gravitational potential (7.2, 7.4). The local vacuum component is an equilibrium configuration of φ: its profile ρ_0·exp(−R/α) is a quasi-stationary solution of the relaxation equation of 7.1, not a postulate of the metric. The dark component enters T_μν as a real energy density of a coherent condensate — with a dust gravitational response (w ≈ 0, 6.8.7), a finite mass M_∞ = 8πρ_0α³ and standard lensing.

**Why not a modification Λ_eff(R).** An alternative formulation was considered and rejected — a replacement of Λ by a locally regulated function Λ_eff(R) = Λ·F(ρ_b) with a suppression in the center and an approach to Λ at the periphery. Three independent reasons against:

1. **Kinematics.** The contribution of the Λ-term to the circular velocity: V_Λ² = −(Λc²/3)R². A positive Λ is repulsive: its contribution decreases the rotation velocity and grows quadratically with the radius. No monotonic Λ_eff ≥ 0 reproduces flat curves and a mass deficit — it gives the opposite effect.

2. **Conservation.** Λ_eff(R) ≠ const requires an exchange term: ∇^μT_μν^baryon = (c⁴/8πG)·∂_νΛ_eff — the baryons must feel a force along the gradient of Λ_eff, which is not observed. The exchange term is physically correct only as a consequence of the equation of motion of a real field: the carrier of the exchange is φ. Thereby "a modification of Einstein without a field" is internally contradictory, and with a field — redundant: the field already does all the work.

3. **Empirics.** The fit of 6.8 shows the dust-like behavior of the component (a finite mass, a Keplerian asymptotics, localized dense cores in the dwarfs) — the behavior of a condensate, not of vacuum pressure.

The notation via Λ_eff(R) is retained only as an effective shorthand in the regimes where the condensate is quasi-uniform; the fundamental object of the model is the field φ.

**The bridge of formalization.** By this section, the main unclosed bridge of the hypothesis is closed: the coupling of the ontology of 1.3 ("gravity as a property of the medium") with the Einstein equations. The fixation: the gravitational sector is described by standard GR in all post-breakdown regimes; the specificity of the RMEP is contained entirely in the source — the field φ. The formulation of 1.3 refers to the pre-mass regime (the regulatory dynamics of the medium itself, Sections 2–3); the transition at the birth of mass is not a modification of the geometry but the appearance of sources. The baryonic cycle (7.3) is realized standardly: the baryons curve spacetime according to Einstein, the configuration of φ follows the baryonic potential through V_eff, and it is precisely the distribution of the condensate that modifies the local geometry in practice. In the classical limit, the bridge is closed; a strict derivation of the pre-mass regime from a microscopic theory is an open task (Section 13).

The scale of regulation α is not a free parameter: it is set by the canonical assumption α ≈ 5 kpc·(V_max/100 km/s)^1.89 (6.8.4 — a working assumption; an independent check does not confirm the concrete form) — and is thereby connected with the baryonic properties of the system.

**The key difference from ΛCDM** is not in the metric (the metric is standard) but in the nature of the dark source: not a population of non-interacting particles with an arbitrary formation history but a relaxing configuration of the medium tracing the baryonic state (7.1–7.2). A consequence — the finite total mass of the regulation and the prediction of a Keplerian segment (6.8.8), absent for NFW halos.

## 9.2. The weak-field limit

In the weak-field limit, the relativistic formulation passes into a Newtonian potential with the contribution of the condensate:

Φ(R) = Φ_N(R) + Φ_reg(R), Φ_N(R) = −G·M_baryon(R)/R,

where Φ_reg(R) is the Newtonian potential of the spherical configuration ρ_0·exp(−R/α). An exact reconciliation with Section 6: the contribution to the circular velocity

V²_reg(R) = G·M(<R)/R = (4πG·ρ_0·α³/R)·[2 − (2 + 2R/α + R²/α²)·e^(−R/α)]

— precisely the formula of 6.8.1 used in the fitting of the 175 rotation curves. The relativistic formulation in the weak-field limit reproduces the empirical profile without an adjustment.

**The limiting regimes:** in the center (R ≪ α) — a harmonic potential, V²_reg = (4/3)πG·ρ_0·R² — the exact behavior of a homogeneous sphere; at the periphery (R ≫ α) — a Keplerian segment V²_reg → G·M_∞/R, predicting a fall of the curve beyond the scale of regulation (6.8.8). The flat segments of the observed curves are formed by the slow growth of M(<R) in the transition zone — not a "plateau of a constant": a constant potential gives a zero contribution to the velocity, and the former parameterization with the saturation (1 − e^(−R²/α²)) is untenable in this role mathematically, not only empirically.

ρ_0 is a locally effective density of the condensate: after the cascade amplification (7.4) it exceeds the cosmological mean by five orders; precisely with the amplified value the model reproduces the rotation curves of Section 6. The cosmological mean gives a negligible contribution to the galactic dynamics.

The weak-field limit is applicable to the overwhelming majority of the observed galaxies (g_N ~ 10⁻¹⁰–10⁻⁸ m/s²) — this is precisely the regime of the analysis of the 175 rotation curves (Section 6).

## 9.3. Strong fields: black holes

In strong gravitational fields (R ~ r_s, where r_s is the Schwarzschild radius), the Einstein equations are not modified: the configuration of φ is a source on a par with any matter, and its local density is finite and small.

**An estimate of the correction — by the densities, not only by the scales.** The mean density of a black hole inside the horizon ρ̄ = 3c⁶/(32πG³M²): for M ~ 10⁸ M_⊙ it amounts to ~1.8 g/cm³, for the limiting supermassive objects (10¹⁰–10¹¹ M_⊙) — ~2×10⁻⁴–2×10⁻⁶ g/cm³. The ratio of the condensate density to the mean for the typical supermassive holes (10⁷–10⁸ M_⊙) is ρ_0/ρ̄ ~ 10⁻²⁴–10⁻²⁶; for the limiting objects it rises only to ~10⁻¹⁹: in the whole range, the correction to the metric at the horizon is negligible — harder by nineteen orders than the former estimate via r_s/α ≤ 10⁻⁵, which is retained as an additional argument of the separation of the scales (r_s ~ 10⁻⁷–10⁻² pc against α ~ kpc).

**The key conclusion:** the black holes require no modification of the standard theory. The properties of the horizon, the Hawking radiation, the thermodynamics are preserved, since the condensate is an ordinary source with a finite density, incapable of competing with the curvature at the horizon. The black holes in the RMEP are the nodes of the return of energy (0.6.6, 7.8), untouched by the regulation itself. An open question: the dynamics of φ in the vicinity of the hole — the accretion of the condensate, possible bound cloud-like configurations (analogous to the superradiance effects of axions, known in standard physics); the formulation — in the program of Section 13.

## 9.4. The law of conservation of energy in the RMEP

In the covariant formulation, the condensate is represented by its own energy–momentum tensor T_μν^φ. Since the field φ obeys its equation of motion, the total conservation law

∇^μ[T_μν^baryon + T_μν^φ] = 0

is fulfilled identically, not postulated. The sectors are conservative separately only in the homogeneous limit (∇V_eff = 0); the exchange between the condensate and the baryonic medium is mediated by the dependence of V_eff on the baryonic density: a change of the substrate changes the energy of the configuration — the exchange term is proportional to the gradient of V_eff and in the effective description reproduces the structure "∇T_μν^vac ∝ ∂_νΛ_eff" already as a stricter consequence, without violating the Bianchi identities. At the level of the microworld, the suppressed contribution passes into the configuration of the regulatory field (7.2, 7.4); the identification of an explicit Lagrangian formulation is a task of Section 13.

For a galaxy — a stationary system — the total energy, including the configuration of the condensate, is defined and conserved. The statement of the balance refers to bound stationary systems; in the expanding Universe, a global balance is not defined (9.4.1) — as in ΛCDM.

### 9.4.1. The channel of transfer and the estimates of the scales

A locally covariant conservation law is fulfilled in all regimes of the model. The suppression of the vacuum energy in the presence of matter does not mean its disappearance: the redistribution proceeds between the configuration of the condensate and the medium through the dependence of V_eff. An estimate of the scales: the material equivalent of the exchange flux at the galactic scales (≤10⁵–10⁶ M_⊙) is smaller than the mass of the regulation by four-five orders — the dynamics is set by the distribution of the condensate directly, not by a redistribution of the energy into a material form. The identification of the channel of transfer in an explicit Lagrangian formulation is a task of Section 13. A global balance of the energy in the expanding Universe is not defined — just as in ΛCDM; the energy of the reddening photons is not transferred into any substrate: the homogeneity of the spectrum of the relic radiation, measured with a precision of ~10⁻⁴ (FIRAS), excludes any channel of absorption at a level above 10⁻⁴ of the density of the radiation.

# Section 10. Comparison with MOND and relativistic generalizations

## 10.1. The phenomenology of MOND

Modified Newtonian dynamics (Milgrom 1983 [27]) introduces an acceleration a₀ ≈ 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ m/s²: at a baryonic acceleration g_N ≫ a₀ the dynamics is Newtonian; at g_N ≲ a₀ the effective acceleration enters the regime g ∝ √(g_N·a₀). The successes of the phenomenology are substantial: the rotation curves of spiral galaxies are described with a single parameter (the M/L of the stellar population); the Tully–Fisher relation and the radial acceleration relation (RAR) are reproduced as direct consequences of the scale transformation; the scatter of the RAR in the local sample is small (~0.13 dex).

However, MOND is a phenomenology: the transition function is postulated without a microscopic justification. It also has established limits of applicability: in clusters of galaxies MOND removes only a part of the missing mass (a residual of ~a factor of two requires neutrinos with a mass of the order of 2 eV or an additional component); gravitational lensing in the Newtonian frame of MOND is not described; cosmology requires relativization, and the relativistic generalizations after GW170817 are squeezed by the rigid constraint on the speed of gravity (10.4).

## 10.2. The transition regime of the RMEP and the scale a₀

In the RMEP, the transition to the "modified" regime arises from the shape of the profile: at a baryonic acceleration exceeding the scale of the regulation field, the contribution of the condensate is small; below the transition acceleration the contribution of the regulation begins to dominate and the rotation curve enters a flat segment. The transition acceleration in the model is not a postulate but a consequence of the relation of the amplitude and the scale of the configuration (V0, α) with the local baryonic field (6.8.6): the relative contribution of the regulation grows as g_bary ∝ V²_bary/R decreases, by the construction of the profile.

**The key difference in the status of the parameters.** In MOND, a₀ is a universal constant, fitted from the data once. In the RMEP, two free parameters (V0, α) fall per galaxy; however, the connection of the scale with the baryonic state is formulated as a working assumption: the main run gives α ≈ 5 kpc·(V_max/100 km/s)^1.89 (a bootstrap interval of the exponent [1.34; 2.41]; Spearman ρ = 0.66), but an independent recalculation does not reproduce this form of the dependence (k = 0.05; Spearman 0.04) — until the protocols are reconciled it is not treated as an established result (6.8.4). The density ρ_0 = V0²/(8πG·α²) is a derived quantity, a parameter of the concrete galaxy; the direction of its trend with mass is an open reconciliation of the protocols (6.8.7). Thereby the reduction of the degrees of freedom to a function of the baryonic state in the RMEP is a constructive requirement of the model, not a measured result: the status of this reduction is the same as that of the assumption on the scale, and it is subject to verification under the program of Sections 6–8.

The status of the universality of the transition scale — a discriminating test, performed by a computation under the protocol of 6.8.1. From the canonical scaling (6.8.4, 6.8.7), the universality of the value of the transition acceleration does not follow explicitly: if the scale and the amplitude change with the mass of the galaxy, the transition acceleration may vary. The exponent of the canonical assumption gives a preliminary indication of a weak dependence: a rough estimate of the transition acceleration g_trans ~ V0²/α at V0 ∝ V_max and α ∝ V_max^1.89 (6.8.4 — a working assumption; an independent check does not confirm this form) gives g_trans ∝ V_max^0.11 — an almost indifference to mass. There is also an indirect support from above: the fit reproduces the radial correlation of the discrepancy with the acceleration — the structural basis of the RAR (the reproduction is conditional, 6.8.6). The quantitative check is performed under the protocol of 6.8.1: for each of the 175 galaxies, from the per-object parameters of the fit (V0, α), the transition acceleration g_trans is computed as the first intersection g_reg(R) = g_bary(R) along the curve. The result: an intersection exists in 138 objects (all 25 censored solutions of 6.8.4, where the regulation is indistinguishable from zero, and 12 objects with a domination of baryons along the whole curve are excluded automatically); the median transition acceleration is 0.14·a₀ (a₀ = 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ m/s²), the width of the 16–84 percentiles is 0.55 dex. For comparison: the MOND-equivalent point of the equality of the addition to the baryonic contribution follows from the deep limit (√(g_bar·a₀) = 2g_bar) and equals 0.25·a₀ — the median of the RMEP lies within half a dex of it. The outcome of the test is intermediate between the criteria (a) and (b): the order of magnitude of the transition acceleration is reproduced over the whole sample (without a failure by criterion (c)), however a systematic trend with mass is revealed — Spearman 0.56 (p ~ 10⁻¹²); the medians over the bins grow from 0.07·a₀ at V_max < 60 km/s to 0.21·a₀ at V_max > 200 km/s. Thereby the model predicts a mass dependence of the transition acceleration — a property absent for the constant a₀ of MOND and accessible to an independent check on the sets where the transition acceleration is measurable separately from the fit: a discriminating prediction, entered into the verification program.

## 10.4. The relativization of MOND (TeVeS and the descendants)

The theory TeVeS and its modifications remove the problem of lensing at the cost of a complication: two metrics, a vector field, additional free functions. The observations of gravitational waves (GW170817: the speed of gravity equals the speed of light with a precision of ~10⁻¹⁵) have narrowed the admissible class of such theories — the majority of the vector–tensor realizations with a dynamical metric is refuted.

The RMEP builds its relativistic formulation more simply and in a different place: the gravitational sector is not modified at all (9.1); the specific content lives in a single dynamical scalar field φ with a baryon-dependent effective potential. The speed of the propagation of the gravitational perturbations of the metric is the speed of light by construction: GW170817 does not constrain the model. The constraining factor for φ is the sensitivity of the condensate to strong fields (9.3) and the program of the concretization of the potential (7.2). A complete Lagrangian formulation is a task of Section 13.

## 10.5. A summary comparison

The RMEP does not reject MOND as a phenomenology — it reproduces its main empirical support (the radial connection of the discrepancy with the acceleration — the reproduction is conditional, 6.8.6) and explains its origin: the transition regime is a consequence of the shape of the profile of the regulation and its connection with the baryonic state (the connection — a working assumption, 6.8.4; 10.2). The computation by the per-object parameters (10.2) gives: a median transition acceleration of 0.14·a₀ — within half a dex of the MOND-equivalent point of the equality of the contributions (0.25·a₀) — at a scatter of 0.55 dex and a systematic trend with mass. The RMEP reproduces the phenomenon of a₀ in order of magnitude without postulating a constant and adds to it a verifiable mass dependence (the status of the dependence — a working hypothesis, 6.8.4). At the same time, the RMEP gives what MOND lacks: a relativistic formulation without a modification of the metric (9.1), standard lensing, a finite total mass of the regulation with the prediction of a Keplerian segment in 72 objects of the sample (6.8.8) and a cosmological generalization without a superstructure. A possible analog of the external-field effect of MOND in the RMEP — a dependence of the configuration on the environment through V_eff (7.4) — is considered an open question, not a declared feature.

# Section 11. Cosmological evolution

## 11.1. The frame: the standard background and two components

The relativistic formulation (9.1) fixes: the gravitational sector — standard GR, the Friedmann geometry is not modified. The cosmological dynamics is determined by the sum of the components: the baryonic matter (ρ_b), the local vacuum component (ρ_0) — localized configurations of the condensate with a dust-like gravitational response (w ≈ 0, 6.8.7), the uniform dark energy — the cosmological background (1.1, 7.12) — and the standard radiation (essential at the early epochs).

**The key distinction of the two levels of evolution.** Equation 7.1 — d/dt ln(M_vac/M_b) = 1/t_p − 1/t_DM, t_ev ≈ 1.2×10¹⁰ years — describes the evolution of the regulation inside a connected system: in the halo of a concrete galaxy the fraction of the dark component grows with age. This is not the evolution of the volume mean: the integral cosmological density of the component equals ρ̅ ≈ f·ρ_local (7.12) and reaches the standard Ω_cdm ≈ 0.26 through the matching of the filling fraction f ~ 2–4×10⁻⁵ — a consistency of the model, not its verification (7.12), but its absence would be a prohibition. With time it is not the density that grows but the degree of organization: the fraction of the component inside the halos and disks, the depth of the connections of the regulation with the baryonic substrate. The cosmological mean behaves as matter; the structural distribution — evolves.

## 11.2. The background dynamics: H(z) and BAO

From 11.1 follows the central cosmological prediction of the hypothesis — more precisely, its absence: H(z) in the RMEP agrees with ΛCDM within the errors. The geometry is standard, the volume mean of the material components is standard; the background dark energy is standard in the zeroth approximation — the only deviation from the exact Λ-background is set by the small leakage channel (4.6, 11.4), whose effect on H(z) is orders of magnitude below the observational errors; the model passes the cosmological checks as ΛCDM with small additions (7.12). The former formulation "H(z) lower by 5–10% at z > 2" referred to the withdrawn branch with a suppressed Λ_eff and is not part of the hypothesis.

This agreement is a strong constraint on itself, not a neutral background: the exponential growth of the ratio of the components inside the system (7.1) is not transferred to the volume mean — otherwise the total density of matter at z ~ 2 would be twice below the standard one, which is refuted by the existence of massive clusters at z ~ 1–2 and by the BAO data. The consistency of the two levels — the structural and the volumetric one — is a mandatory requirement of the correctness of the hypothesis.

**BAO.** The scale ruler is imprinted at the recombination, long before the formation of the local configurations; its later accumulation does not shift the comoving scale (7.12). The prediction — a standard BAO without regional variations; the absence of deviations is a consistency of the model, not a discriminating test.

## 11.3. CMB

The CMB spectrum and the acoustic peaks are not modified by the model: at the early times the contribution of the local component is dynamically negligible, the background inherits the standard Λ-cosmology with a small addition of the leakage channel (4.6, 11.2) — of the second order of smallness, not affecting the peaks. Additional consequences — in the low multipoles through the integrated Sachs–Wolfe effect: the formation of the component in the halos adds an inhomogeneous addition, correlated with the large-scale structure, at a level of percents — statistically indistinguishable from the standard scatter (7.12). A discriminating test — the sign and the angular correlation of the residuals of the low multipoles with the map of the large structures; the status — open (Planck PR4+; the summary of the tests — 11.5).

## 11.4. The equation of state w(z)

For the background component the model inherits w = −1 in the zeroth approximation: the uniform dark energy is locked in its own states (1.1) and does not cluster. The only deviation from the exact −1 is set by the small leakage channel (4.6): a correction to w of the order of a percent at z ≈ 0 and decreasing with the redshift (w_eff → −1 at the large z); its effect on the background dynamics is orders of magnitude below the observational errors (11.2). The direction of the correction — a weakening of the dark energy with time — coincides with the direction of the DESI hints; this is a qualitative coincidence of the direction, not a quantitative prediction: the rate of the leakage is an open parameter (the status of the channel — 1.1).

For the local component the effective w at the large scales is dust-like (w ≈ 0), both in the volume mean and in the lensing; its equation of state does not evolve against the background.

The DESI hints on the evolution of the dark energy (a w₀wₐ-signal up to 4.2σ depending on the combination of the data; the independent group DES points in the same direction, but the reanalysis of 2026 partially returns the evidence to ΛCDM — the picture is inhomogeneous) refer to the background component and form for the model a discriminating test with two outcomes: (a) at a final confirmation of the evolution — the model provides a mechanism absent for ΛCDM with a constant: the leakage channel and the weakening of the feeding (1.1, 4.6; the prediction of 5.3 — the evolution of the ratio of the dark energy to the dark matter with the cosmological time); (b) at an establishment of a strict constancy — the refutation criterion fixed in 5.3: the leakage channel loses its observational support. Both outcomes preserve the background and structural consistency of the hypothesis; only the status of one of the branches of 4.6 differs.

## 11.5. Structure formation and the cluster counts

The background growth of the perturbations D(z) at a standard volume mean — standard. Specific effects are possible only through the relaxation of the condensate: the time τ_s(R) is bounded from above by the observation of the Bullet Cluster (τ_s ≲ 10⁸ years for the clusters, 7.9) — the configuration follows the baryonic potential quickly, and the gravitational structuring passes practically in the standard way. The former prediction of "an excess of massive clusters at z > 2" referred to the withdrawn branch with a suppressed Λ_eff and is not part of the hypothesis: at standard background Λ and volume density of matter, the early structure formation is not accelerated.

An open question with a concrete trace: if the relaxation of the condensate makes a nonlinear contribution to the compression of the halos (the redistribution between the configuration and the substrate, 7.1), the thin statistics — the mass function of the halos at the low end, the profiles of the satellites — may differ from ΛCDM. A quantitative computation requires a numerical realization of the system of 7.1 by the plan of 7.16 in two stages: the solution of the system for a typical galaxy — a short-term item; an N-body growth of the structure with the thin statistics — a medium-term one; the task is formulated as open, without a declared sign of the effect (the summary of the tests — the table below).

## 11.6. Supernovae Ia: a discriminating test of the structural level

The spatial inhomogeneity of the local regulation (7.4) is projected onto the observations not through a shift of the cosmological relation but through the dependence of the standardized brightness of the supernovae on the environment. The observed mass step (0.04–0.1 magnitudes: the supernovae in massive, old, passive hosts are brighter; the exact binding of the values to the publications — before the final version, 7.12) agrees with the trend of 7.7 — later phases, a higher fraction of the regulation, a deeper potential. The prediction of the model: the step grows with the age of the stellar population and the depth of the potential at a fixed mass of the host. A discriminating test (7.12): the correlation of the step not with the mass as such but with the independent proxies of the local density of the regulation — the age of the stellar population and the RAR residuals. Among the consistency checks of the background (CMB, BAO) this is the only quantitative anchor with a discriminating force (7.12); alongside it, the discriminating tests of the low multipoles of the CMB (11.3) and the DESI outcome on w(z) (11.4) act in the section. The test is feasible on the existing samples (Pantheon+, DES).

## 11.7. The agreement with the evolutionary tests of the structural level

Three effects, observable on the existing data, form the support of the section — all of them structural, not background; for the first two the agreement with the model is already quantitative:

1. The evolution of the fraction of the dark component inside R_e of the disks: ~0.1–0.5 at z ~ 1–2.3 against 0.6–0.9 locally (Genzel et al. 2020; Price et al. 2021; Parti et al. 2023) — a confirmation of the direction of 7.1/7.7: the fraction of the regulation in the structure grows with time (the summary table: confirmed).
2. The evolution of the scale parameter of the RAR: a₀ at z ~ 1 is doubled with respect to the local one (MUSE-DARK III; 6.2, 6.6) — the connection "baryons — regulation" depends on the epoch, as the cyclic picture requires; for a static dark matter with unchanged constants such an evolution is not foreseen (6.2). A quantitative model of the evolution — an open test.
3. The mass step of the supernovae — the correlation of the brightness with the stellar mass and the age of the hosts; the interpretation through the depth of the potential (11.6); a discriminating check through the independent proxies — an open test (11.6).

ΛCDM possesses partial mechanisms for each of the three (the evolution of the baryonic physics with z, the population effects); the discriminating force of these tests is not in the facts but in their consistency with a single scale t_ev ~ 10¹⁰ years, fixed independently of the galactic fit — by the very evolution of the RAR (7.1, 6.6: the value of the effective time is not free). The coincidence of the three scales — the evolution of the RAR, the growth of the fraction of the component, the age dependence of the residuals — a quantitative task of the nearest check (7.16, 13.4).

# Section 12. The internal structure of compact objects: the field interpretation of the center

## 12.0. The relation to Section 9.3 and the ontological frame

Section 9.3 describes the external dynamics of black holes in the RMEP: the metric near the horizon is standard, the Hawking radiation and the thermodynamics are preserved unchanged. For the galactic regulation this follows by the scale: the configurations of the condensate are essential at the scales ~α (kiloparsecs), not at the Schwarzschild radius r_s. For the central structure introduced in the present section, the action of the same condition is different: the modification described by the field dynamics is localized inside the central region and does not modify the external metric — at this level the condition of 9.3 acts as a requirement of the correctness of both mechanisms, not as a consequence of the scale. The present section refers to the internal structure of the central part of the compact object, which is not checked by direct observations and, within the model, is a physical interpretation (a development of 0.6.6 "Black holes as the mechanism of the energy return"). The external observational predictions — the black hole shadow (EHT), the gravitational waves (LIGO/Virgo) — are not modified: the observations agree with the model but neither confirm nor refute the internal field picture. The difference from GR appears only in the central regions and is not yet observationally testable.

**The distinction of the two quantities.** At the galactic scales (6, 9.1–9.2), the model describes the regulation as a real density of the condensate with a flat core — ρ_0 = V0²/(8πG·α²) in the center with a fall on the scale α — entering the stress-energy tensor as an ordinary mass (9.1). The present section considers a different quantity: the ansatz ρ(r) = A/[r·(1 + α·r)²] describes the density of the regulatory field in the central structure of the compact object — the distribution of the field in the zone where the field dynamics takes over the structuring role. The coincidence of the symbol α in both formulas — a claim of the model: the scale of the regulation is single (at the same time, in the ansatz α enters the combination (1 + αr), i.e. acts as an inverse scale, and in the galactic profile — as a length; the unity means one physical scale in two representations). The profiles are nevertheless different: the galactic one — a flat core (6.8.5), the compact one — a cusp form with an integrable divergence ρ ∝ r⁻¹ in the center. Identifying them is impossible: the numerical check shows that the ansatz differs structurally from the galactic profile (12.3), and the transfer of the galactic parametrization to the compact objects is a hypothesis subject to verification (13.4).

## 12.1. Variant 1: the formation of a black hole at the collapse of a massive stellar core

1. **The physical basis of the scenario.** The basic picture is standard: the exhaustion of the thermonuclear fuel of a massive star leads to the loss of pressure and to the gravitational collapse of the core. The RMEP supplements this picture with a field treatment of the central region: instead of a singularity, a stable field configuration with the parameters (A, α) is formed — at the scenario level; the quantitative justification is a numerical modeling (items 5–6, 13.4).
2. **The critical mass threshold.** At the masses of the remnant above ~2–3 solar masses, the pressure of the degenerate neutrons ceases to balance the gravity. Within the RMEP, the place of the particle pressure is taken by the field stability: the regulatory field forms a structure preventing a singularity (the mechanism of the pressure substitution is not derived in the model — the scenario level).
3. **The event horizon and the observable boundary.** The horizon arises at R < R_s = 2GM/c². The model does not revise the physics at the scales accessible to observations, hence it preserves the predictions for the shadow (EHT) and the gravitational waves (LIGO/Virgo).
4. **The microphysics and the transition to the field picture.** At the densities of the order of the nuclear one and above, the notions "atom" and "molecule" lose their meaning; in GR this leads to a singularity as a mathematical limit. In the RMEP, the stability is maintained by the dynamics of the regulatory field: the density grows, but the behavior in the center is determined by the field profile (item 5).
5. **The parametrization and the empirical connection.** The central structure is described by the ansatz ρ_RMEP(r) = A/[r·(1 + α·r)²]. The recalculation of the connection of the parameters is performed by a direct fit of the ansatz to the rotation curves of the SPARC sample (175 galaxies, fitted M/L from the appendix, χ²-minimization: a grid + Nelder–Mead with rigid bounds; the statistics are computed over 171 objects). The connection A ∝ α^(−2.26) (R² = 0.727) — a result of an earlier version with a different profile — is not confirmed by the recalculation: acceptable solutions 57 out of 171 (the rest are censored at the bounds, predominantly a degeneracy of the ansatz beyond the range of the data; the median χ²_red of the acceptable solutions is 2.26); over them the A–α connection is weak — a slope of 0.35, a bootstrap 95% CI [0.08; 0.65], R² = 0.23 — while the amplitude A tracks the mass (a Spearman with V_max of 0.69, p ~ 3×10⁻⁹). The old correlation is recognized as an artifact of the version and moved to the archive; the one-parameter character of the ansatz in the form "α unambiguously determines A" is withdrawn; the coordination of (A, α) is an open task (13.4). The behavior in the center: at r → 0 the density formally diverges as r⁻¹, however the divergence is integrable — the mass within any finite radius is finite: dM = 4πr²·ρ·dr ∝ r·dr → 0. On the periphery the ansatz is not continued: at r ≫ 1/α the density falls as r⁻³, the accumulated mass grows logarithmically — M(r) = (4πA/α²)·[ln(1+αr) − αr/(1+αr)], the same functional form as NFW — therefore the ansatz describes only the central zone and requires a truncation/matching with the outer profile at the scale of the transition. The physical regularization of the center (a saturation of the density at a scale < α) and the matching procedure — tasks of the next stage of the numerical modeling.
6. **The dynamics of the growth and the stability.** Established by the recalculation — a property of the ansatz on the galactic sample: the amplitude A tracks the mass of the system (a Spearman of 0.69). The transfer to the dynamics of the growth of the central structure ("an addition of mass increases A") is a hypothesis of the same status as the scenario itself: the galactic correlation is not a computation of the evolution of a compact object. The statement "a growth without a catastrophic collapse" relies on the stability of the field configuration, which is not yet quantitatively checked; the check of the stability of the solutions in the compact region enters the program of the numerical modeling (13.4). A fixed power-law connection A(α) is not confirmed by the recalculation.

## 12.2. Variant 2: the formation of a black hole as a vortex of the regulatory field

1. **The primacy of the field over the matter.** In this scenario a black hole arises not by a collapse of the matter but as a stable vortex configuration of the regulatory field upon reaching the critical gradients of the curvature and the energy density. The matter may accumulate already after the formation of the vortex.
2. **The conditions of the formation.** The vortexing arises in the regions with the threshold gradients. Possible channels: the density fluctuations in the early Universe (primordial vortices); tidal interactions in dense clusters and at the mergers of galaxies; turbulent regimes of accretion. The status of the channels is hypothetical: an observational basis for their discrimination is absent, and the scenario does not claim to replace the standard channels of the stellar collapse and the mergers but supplements them with an interpretation of the internal structure.
3. **The stability threshold and the horizon.** The horizon is the boundary of the vortex structure: at a sufficient "twisting" of the field the perturbations do not escape outward (the scenario level; the mechanism of reaching the boundary — a task of the modeling, 13.4). The radius of the boundary — R_s = 2GM/c², where M is the effective mass of the vortex and of the captured matter.
4. **The structure of the vortex and the asymptotics.** The profile near the center — the same ansatz as in 12.1: ρ_RMEP(r) = A/[r·(1 + α·r)²]. At the intermediate scales (r ≫ 1/α) the ansatz passes into ρ ∝ r⁻³ — an NFW-like asymptotics; a direct fit of the ansatz to the rotation curves of SPARC (12.1.5) gives acceptable solutions for a third of the sample (57 out of 171, a median χ²_red = 2.26) — a limited agreement in the working range, without an advantage over the galactic profile of the RMEP (6.8.3). Let us note precisely: as with NFW, the total mass of such a profile diverges logarithmically — the finite mass and the Keplerian segment (6.8.8) are properties of the galactic profile of the regulation, not of the ansatz. The ansatz describes only the inner region; the matching with the galactic profile at the boundary of the applicability, truncating the divergent asymptotics, is a mandatory element of the numerical realization (13.4). The behavior in the center — as in item 5 of Section 12.1: the divergence r⁻¹ is integrable, the regularization — a task of the next stage.
5. **The empirical connection of the parameters.** The one-parameter character of the ansatz in the form "α determines A" is not confirmed by the recalculation (12.1.5): the connection is weak, R² = 0.23. The correlation −2.26 — an archive of the version. Established by the recalculation — a property of the ansatz on the galactic sample: A tracks V_max (a Spearman of 0.69); the transfer to the compact objects is a hypothesis of the same status as the scenario (12.1.6). The justification of the connection (A, α) — an open task (13.4).
6. **The growth of the mass and the stability.** The capture of the matter by the vortex and the preservation of the structure — a scenario statement: the stability of the field configuration is not quantitatively checked, the check enters the program of the modeling (13.4; the status — as in 12.1.6). The transfer of the galactic tracking of the mass ("an addition of mass increases A") to the compact objects is a hypothesis, not a computation; the coordination of (A, α) — an open task of 13.4.
7. **The absence of a singularity.** The center of the vortex is a region of the maximal intensity of the regulatory field, not an infinite density of the matter. In the field interpretation, the singularity is not a physical result; the formal divergence of the ansatz is removed by an assumed saturation at a scale < α.
8. **The consistency with the observations.** The predictions checkable by the external dynamics are preserved: the black hole shadow (EHT), the gravitational waves (LIGO/Virgo). The power-law asymptotics of the ansatz in the working range — a limited agreement with the data by the results of the direct fit (12.1.5); the finiteness of the mass is a property of the galactic profile, not of the ansatz (12.2.4).

## 12.3. A general conclusion on the section

The two mechanisms of the formation describe different paths of the origin of the objects with an event horizon on a single basis — the regulatory field of the energy of the void. Variant 1 supplements the standard picture of the stellar collapse with a field treatment of the central region, removing the singularity at the scenario level and not contradicting the existing observational constraints. Variant 2 considers the black holes as primordial vortices of the field arising at the critical gradients without a mandatory preliminary collapse of the matter; its status is a hypothesis with a lesser support than that of variant 1, since the standard channels of the formation of the compact objects are well confirmed by the observations.

The unified parametrization (A, α) is preserved as a frame of the section, however the empirical correlation A ∝ α^(−2.26) is not confirmed by the recalculation and moved to the archive (a result of a different version). The recalculation gave two substantial consequences: the ansatz differs structurally from the galactic profile (the acceptable fits — 57 out of 171; the dominating mode of the failure — an internal rise of the ansatz, a quasi-Keplerian V² ∝ R, not matching the steep rise of the real curves of the disk-dominated galaxies), which numerically reinforces the distinction of 12.0; on the galactic sample the amplitude A tracks the mass (a Spearman of 0.69) — a trend in agreement with the scaling of the galactic parameters in 6.8.7, the transfer to the compact objects is a hypothesis (12.1.6, 12.2.6). The checkable content of the section — a confirmation of the tracking of A with the mass on the extended samples and a numerical check of the stability and of the self-consistent growth of the central structure in the module rmep_blackhole.py of the repository RMEP-dark-matter with the updated criterion (13.4). The internal field structure of the center of the compact objects is not testable by direct observations and, within the model, is an interpretation (0.6.6); all the external observational predictions of the RMEP for the black holes coincide with the standard theory (9.3).

# Section 13. Limits of applicability and development plan

## 13.1. Region of applicability

In its current formulation, the Regulatory Model of Empty-Space Energy is quantitatively confirmed in the galactic domain and the transitional regime; in late-epoch cosmology the model is consistent with the data by construction, without discriminating power at the background level (11.8, 11.9):

  Galactic scales — rotation curves of 175 galaxies of the SPARC sample (3391 data points), comparison with NFW at an equal number of free parameters: RMEP wins in 107 cases against 31, with 37 ties; median χ²_red of 23.2 versus 36.3 (6.8.1–6.8.3). The empirical scaling of the regulation scale α ≈ 5 kpc·(V_max/100 km/s)^1.89, the bootstrap interval of the exponent [1.34; 2.41], Spearman ρ = 0.66 (6.8.4); the density ρ_0 decreases with mass in the main run; the direction is an open reconciliation of the protocols (6.8.7).

  Late-epoch cosmology — consistency with H(z), BAO, Type Ia supernovae and the CMB by construction: the geometry is standard (9.1), the volume average of the regulation is reconciled with cosmology (7.12), the background dark energy is locked in the zeroth-order approximation except for a small leakage channel (1.1, 4.6, 11.2). Discriminating cosmological tests are of the structural level; the summary of statuses is Table 11.8.

  Transitional regime — reproduction of the a₀ phenomenon to order of magnitude without postulating a constant: median transitional acceleration 0.14·a₀, scatter 0.55 dex, systematic trend with mass as a discriminating prediction (10.2).

Outside these domains the model remains a theoretical construct: its predictions are formulated but not quantitatively tested.

## 13.2. The early Universe (z > 2)

RMEP does not predict deviations of background cosmological quantities at early epochs from ΛCDM: both former predictions — «H(z) lower by 5–10%» and «an excess of massive clusters at z > 2» — belonged to the withdrawn branch with suppressed Λ_eff and have been archived (11.2, 11.5). Under the standard volume average (11.1), early structure formation is not accelerated.

The model's discriminating predictions for high redshifts are structural; their empirical status: the evolution of the regulation fraction inside R_e of disks (~0.1–0.5 at z ~ 1–2.3 versus 0.6–0.9 locally) — the direction is confirmed, the quantitative connection with t_ev is an open test; the evolution of the transitional acceleration scale (a doubling of a₀ by z ~ 1, MUSE-DARK III; 11.7) — confirmed, the quantitative model is an open test (statuses — Table 11.8). The quantitative connection of both with the scale t_ev ~ 10¹⁰ years is the task of the nearest check (7.16, 13.4).

JWST observations (bright galaxies at z > 10) indicate tension in the standard model at early epochs. RMEP does not claim to explain these anomalies in the current version: the model contains no declared effects in the early Universe, and all statements concerning the early Universe should be read as absent, not as predictions subject to verification. The development of this direction requires a numerical implementation of the system of 7.1 (13.4) and is not part of the evidential base of the present work.

## 13.3. Other limitations

  Galaxy clusters — the regulation effect in rich clusters is described qualitatively; the quantitative fit has not been carried out (10.3).

  Black holes — in the strong field the vacuum correction δ_vac is small; the exterior dynamics of black holes corresponds to the standard theory (9.3). The model introduces no changes into the physics of the event horizon; the internal field structure of the center (Section 12) is a physical interpretation within the framework of the model, not an independent quantitative result; the A–α connection for the compact ansatz is not confirmed by recalculation (12.1.5); the tracking of the amplitude with mass is established on the galactic sample (Spearman 0.69), the transfer to compact objects is a hypothesis (12.1.6, 12.2.6); the stability of the field configuration in the compact region is not quantitatively verified — the check is included in the modeling program (13.4).

  Gravitational lensing — the covariant form yields the standard prediction (9.1), but a systematic comparison with lensing data has not been carried out.

  Microscales — beyond Section 12 the model does not touch upon the physics of galactic nuclei and scales smaller than the regulation scale α; quantitative consequences for nuclear dynamics have not been developed (13.4).

# Conclusion

  This work proposes the Regulatory Model of Empty-Space Energy (RMEP) — an alternative to dark matter on a single mechanism: local configurations of the vacuum condensate with a dust-like gravitational response (w ≈ 0, 6.8.7), whose scale and amplitude are tied to the baryonic state of the system (the scale connection is a working assumption, 6.8.4). The relativistic formulation (9.1) preserves standard GR: the condensate enters the stress-energy tensor as ordinary mass, the metric is not modified — standard gravity outside the regulation zone, standard lensing, the speed of gravitational waves equals the speed of light, and the GW170817 constraint is satisfied trivially. The conceptual distinction of RMEP is the unity of the substrate: the gravitating effect of «dark matter», the cosmological dust component and the background dark energy are described by a single vacuum component, and the origin of baryonic matter is a conceptual part of the work (Section 0), whereas in ΛCDM and alternative approaches the dark sector is introduced as a set of additional entities.

  Quantitative results are obtained on galactic scales. On the SPARC sample (175 galaxies, 3391 data points), the RMEP profile — the condensate density with a flat core (6.8.5) — describes the rotation curves substantially better than the standard NFW profile at an equal number of free parameters: RMEP wins in 107 cases against 31, with 37 ties; the median χ²_red — 23.2 versus 36.3 (6.8.1–6.8.3). The core–cusp problem is solved without additional physics — the flat core is a property of the profile. The scaling of the regulation scale α ≈ 5 kpc·(V_max/100 km/s)^1.89 (6.8.4 — a working assumption); the independent check does not reproduce the specific form of the dependence and the direction of the density trend (6.8.7) — the protocol comparison is fixed as an open task. The connection of the fit parameters with the baryonic state of the system remains a working hypothesis of the model. The fit reproduces the radial correlation of the discrepancy with acceleration — the structural basis of the RAR (the reproduction is conditional, 6.8.6), — while the finite total mass of the regulation yields a Keplerian segment of the rotation curve in 72 of 175 objects (6.8.8) — a discriminating prediction against divergent halos. The test of the universality of the transitional acceleration is carried out by computation: the median transitional acceleration 0.14·a₀ — within half a dex of the MOND-equivalent point of equal contributions — with a scatter of 0.55 dex and a systematic trend with mass (Spearman 0.56): the a₀ phenomenon is reproduced to order of magnitude without postulating a constant, and the predicted mass dependence of the transition is a new discriminating prediction absent in MOND (10.2).

  In cosmology the model inherits the standard background by construction: the geometry is standard (9.1), the volume average of the dust component is reconciled with Ω_cdm through the filling fraction f ~ 2–4×10⁻⁵ (7.12), the background dark energy is locked in the zeroth-order approximation except for a small leakage channel (1.1, 4.6, 11.2) — agreement with H(z), BAO and the CMB is an obligation of the model, and the discriminating power is transferred to structural tests (statuses — Table 11.8): the evolution of the regulation fraction inside R_e of disks (~0.1–0.5 at z ~ 1–2.3 versus 0.6–0.9 locally), the evolution of the transitional acceleration scale (a doubling of a₀ by z ~ 1, MUSE-DARK III), the Type Ia supernova mass step through age and potential depth, the low CMB multipoles through the integral Sachs–Wolfe effect, and the DESI outcome on w(z) with two discriminating outcomes (Section 11). The coincidence of the three independent evolutionary scales with a single t_ev ~ 10¹⁰ years is the quantitative task of the nearest check (11.7, 7.16). The predictions of the earlier versions — «H(z) lower by 5–10% at z > 2», «an excess of massive clusters at z > 2», the correlation A ∝ α^(−2.26) — are not reproduced in the current formulation and have been archived with the reasons recorded (11.2, 11.5, 12.1.5).

  In the strong gravitational field the model introduces no observable changes: the exterior dynamics of black holes and the photon sphere are consistent with the EHT and LIGO/Virgo data (9.3), and black holes in RMEP return energy to the vacuum without changing the properties of the event horizon (0.6.6). The internal field structure of the center of compact objects — two formation mechanisms (Section 12) — is a physical interpretation within the framework of the model, not an independent quantitative result: direct observations of this region are inaccessible, and all external predictions coincide with the standard theory.

  The limits of the model are outlined explicitly: the galactic part is quantitatively verified; the early Universe is consistent with ΛCDM by construction in the background part (11.1), galaxy clusters and lensing are described qualitatively, the internal structure of compact objects is an interpretation (Section 13). The development plan (13.4) includes: the age residuals of the RAR — the least costly discriminating test on existing data; the quantitative connection of the three evolutionary scales with t_ev; the numerical implementation of the regulation evolution system (7.1) with fine statistics; a quantitative fit of the regulation in the rich clusters; the numerical implementation of the central structure of the compact ansatz — the regularization of the center, the stitching with the external profile, the check of stability; the matching of the parameters (A, α) of the compact ansatz and a micro-model of the baryon-dependent potential with the criterion τ_vac ≫ t_ev; a micro-derivation of the pre-mass regime of the GR bridge; a systematic comparison with lensing data and a check of the ratio of dark matter to the baryonic one in old and young regions (0.6.5) on the Euclid and LSST data. Within its region of applicability RMEP is consistent with the available observations and yields testable predictions with outcome criteria recorded in advance; the final judgment on the viability of the model belongs to the checks indicated above.
